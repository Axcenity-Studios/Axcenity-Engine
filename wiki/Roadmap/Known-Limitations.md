# Known limitations

What the engine doesn't do, or does with a known flaw, today. Each entry names where it's documented. Performance issues are in [Known bottlenecks](../Performance/Known-Bottlenecks.md); structural problems in [Technical debt](Technical-Debt.md).

## Scripting

- No per-script **memory limit**; long C functions (`string.rep`, pathological patterns) aren't interrupted by the watchdog (`docs/scripting/sandbox.md`).
- `task.defer` runs at the next step (Roblox: later in the same frame); a RemoteEvent message with no handler is dropped (Roblox queues it); `ContextActionResult.Pass` and touch buttons not implemented (`docs/scripting/client-server.md`).
- Vector3 math allocates a userdata per result; `--!native` doesn't speed up engine calls.
- A breakpoint inside a C-called Lua function (a `table.sort` comparator), or in a script-made coroutine resumed from one, can't pause; a step goes on to the next line where it can. The same goes for a conditional breakpoint, a logpoint and Pause (Pause waits for the next script line that can pause, so a game whose scripts are all waiting pauses only when one wakes). Logpoints and conditions make their line yield on every pass, which slows a hot loop.

## Rendering

- Overlapping see-through items can blend in the wrong order (sorted by center).
- AMD/Vulkan/Windows: the sky clear isn't sRGB-encoded through an sRGB view (`docs/rendering/pipeline.md`).
- Lights are tiled in 2D, not clustered; rect lights use a representative point.
- No vertex colors or `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` from glTF; FBX uses the first UV set; glTF animations are read as bone takes, but their morph target weight channels aren't.
- Humanoid retargeting finds roles by bone names or by a rig definition of the project's (`assets/rigs/*.rig.json`); a retargeted take moves role bones only, so a bone without a role keeps its place (`docs/rendering/animation.md`).

## Physics

- MeshParts collide as convex hulls (hollows filled) unless already convex and small, their model has `_COL` collision pieces, or they're set to `PreciseConvexDecomposition` (R15.2). The Model Studio's Convex Pieces (R15.2b) runs on the UI thread and can take a few seconds on a big model.
- Setting `Position` stops a part (Roblox keeps its speed).
- Ragdolls can exceed joint limits briefly on hard landings.

## Networking

- **Game connections trust a typed address on first use** (MP4; a join link pins the fingerprint); a bare join code trusts the relay. Port mapping (MP5b) can't get past a provider's router (carrier-grade NAT; a relay can), and a killed host leaves its mapping until the 30-minute lease ends.
- No matchmaking or public relay (anyone can run `axcenity-relay`, MP5a), and prediction covers only characters (MP6a, under `Players.CharacterAuthority = "Server"`; physics bodies are MP6b; lag compensation and replays exist, Phase 81). Multiplayer has been measured with 16 bots across emulated home routers and 32 on one machine; a 16–32 client run on real machines (G13, MP7) is planned. All of it is planned in `docs/research/multiplayer-readiness.md` (MP1–MP7).
- **Server-moved characters** (MP6a): other players are drawn in the past on a device, so walking into a moving player costs a correction; moving platforms aren't rewound; seats, `MoveTo` and `MoveDirection` don't drive a remote player's character, and a device Script's `Jump` can be lost in a replay.
- Editor Play can't host or join a real network game.

## Characters

The Character Runtime (Character Studio overhaul CO3; [Characters](../Engine-Systems/Characters.md#applying-a-description-character-runtime), `docs/development/character-studio/architecture.md`):

- **Character Studio's interface is CO12's first part.** The workspace edits format 2 descriptions on any body of the library (the realistic one by default), previews them through the applier, and draws its Body, Face, Hair and Clothing pages from the manifest and catalogue (`docs/editor/character-studio.md`). R1 added presets from the library by kind (with a person's own and favourites), Randomize with scopes and locks, held versions (before and after, side by side) and autosave with recovery; wave 3 the asymmetry tools (mirror or reset a side) and a pose and motion preview (Still, Idle, Walk at the Walk take's own speed, Run, five gestures; the camera follows the head); wave 4 (R9) the Expressions page's face controls in six groups and a face preview (close on the face, still, no blinking, a neutral light). The face controls move left and right together (no one-sided preview), the expression tiles have no pictures, and the neutral light is not in the bottom bar (no room at 1440 points). Not yet: outfit, hair and skin presets (none shipped) and a turntable. The round trip with the Game Editor is built (R10: Edit in Character Studio, Done or Cancel); Cancel discards the studio's changes without asking. The Walk and Run previews play in place: the character doesn't travel, so a planted foot slides back along the floor as on a treadmill.
 The realistic body has no clothes to wear yet beyond its underwear (boxer briefs, briefs, a sports bra, cut from the body itself) (CO10), and one hairstyle, a draft (below). Both families are offered (Paul's decision 7); since wave 3 the stylized one has presets too (four bodies a frame end, four faces, three characters), made with its parameters and checked only for soundness, not against references or captures.
 An installed editor offers it only when its payload has `assets/characters/` (`packaging/stage.py` stages it from this change on). A pack added to a project puts its characters in `assets/characters/descriptions/`, but a game script can't yet load a description by path: it sets a `HumanoidDescription`'s `Data` to the file's text.
- **The Hair and Clothing browsers (wave 4, CO25 and CO26) are built against drafts.** Every shipped item has no `quality`, so all are drafts and, with "Show drafts" off (the default, `asset-quality.md` §5), the stylized pages show only what is worn and the realistic body has nothing to browse beyond its underwear; the tests use their own items. Highlights and roots are declared by a hair material slot's `role` (`secondary`, `accent`), and no shipped style sets roles yet, so none shows colour controls; the natural hair colours assume a style made in a medium brown (`melanin` and `redness` are relative to the colour made), and of the Hair model's values the renderer draws only `tint`, `melanin`, `redness` and `roughness_scale` (dye, tip lightening and highlight wait for the Hair shading model). "Fits this body" knows only what an item's `limitations` say; nothing is measured. A tile's picture is the character being edited wearing the item, so a body change draws the page's tiles again on the picture thread (20 to 600 ms a tile in a debug build after the painter's first, which takes 1 to 3 s), never during a drag; tiles show the old picture until then. All tiles are drawn (no virtual list): fine for tens of items, not thousands. Accessories is still a list of chips (R8). The worn item's parameters use the shared slider row, so the project owner's drag rule of 2026-11-18 reaches them when that row takes it (ux-drag).
- **Editing on the character covers eleven features** (R2, `docs/editor/character-studio.md`). Every feature can be hovered and clicked, but only the face's outline, nose, eyes, cheeks, jaw, chin, mouth, forehead, shoulders, waist and hips have handles (brief §3.4's first seven, and the face's others since 2026-11-24; the brows, the ears and the rest of the body have none; the realistic family only, the stylized manifest has no features or handles). **Face shape is lit as the whole head**: its shapes move the whole face, and the studio shares the face's skin between the features whose shapes move it (`crates/editor/src/character_workspace/direct.rs`, `compete`), so as one of those it took the skin from the nose, the eyes and every other face feature, which lost their highlight. The manifest therefore names the `head` region on it (`faceparams.REGIONS`): it shares nothing, and a click on bare head skin still picks the smaller `head` feature, so Face shape is picked from the navigation. Once `compete` leaves a feature's skin alone against a much larger one, the feature can drop its `regions` and be lit as the face. A handle's whole range takes 110 points of drag along its direction on the screen, whatever the zoom, as every numeric control's does (RA1, Paul's rule of 2026-11-18), so the skin doesn't stay under the pointer: the face's parameters move the skin a few millimetres (about 10 points seen whole in the Face view), less than the pointer; the body's can move more. The waist handle moves the skin 19 mm over its range (the waist slider is weak, R3). The tint, its outline and the handles are drawn by egui over the preview, so what they may cover is found by a CPU depth buffer of the posed character (a texel per 2 points, rebuilt each frame something is selected or hovered; `direct/depth.rs`), not by the renderer's own depth. Where an arm or the nose crosses a feature, the tint stops a triangle short of the crossing (whole triangles are dropped, not cut at the occluder's edge), and a handle within about 6 points of skin that shows is drawn: at 70 degrees the far eye's lid handle sits at the nose's outline, where that lid peeks past it. Since 2026-11-24 (ADR-0045) a feature's tint and outline are drawn on the worn item over it (each of the item's corners takes the tint of the body corner nearest it at rest, within 12 cm: cloth farther off, a wide skirt's hem, takes none) and handles are hidden only by skin, so they show on clothes; a handle under thick cloth is drawn where the skin is, a little inside the cloth's outline. A face feature's tint stops short at the eyes' corners and inside the nostrils (hollows), the lips' reaches about 1.3 cm past the mouth's corners (its width shapes move that skin), and the jaw's, seen from the front, is a sliver at the near jaw angle. A body region's outline follows the region's border along the mesh's edges. The long-term route is the tint as draw data, depth-tested in `render`. Hovering casts a ray against the posed body's 25,000 triangles (after a CPU skinning of the frame's pose) each frame the pointer or the camera moves, and the overlay rasterizes the posed character each frame something is selected or hovered: in a release build, the studio's frame is 0.16 ms with editing on the character off, 3.56 ms with a feature selected and 4.61 ms with the pointer moving over the face (debug: about 20 ms for the ray alone); a still pointer keeps what it found. The handles drive the slider's values in a straight line: a parameter whose skin moves on a curve (a tilt) is followed only near its middle.
- **Edit mode shows a described character at rest** (R10, OPEN-21 fixed; `docs/editor/character-studio.md`). The Game Editor applies kept descriptions and poses described characters in the A-pose with their identity, items and materials; nothing plays and no expression shows until Play. Accessories on sockets aren't placed in edit mode (sockets are placed only by Play's Animators); characters without a description still draw their unposed model.
- **A project made before CO3 has no character library until a character is inserted or edited.** The library is written with the default character (a new project with a character, Insert > Character, Edit in Character Studio's Done, Character Studio's workspace folder); since R10 that writes both families (OPEN-22). An older project gets it the next time one of those is used; until then `Humanoid:ApplyDescription` fails with a message that says the project has no character library, and a packed game built from such a project has none. Its `Character` prefab (its players) is never changed: an older project's players stay stylized until the prefab is replaced by hand. The realistic family copies its 40 MB model into every project that gets it.
- **Some material values aren't drawn yet, and skin tone is one ratio per material.** Eye materials draw as Standard; skin has its own shading model since R5 (`docs/rendering/skin.md`), which draws `scatter_scale`, `definition` and `softness` too, and hair and cloth since CO7's wave 4 (`docs/rendering/hair.md`, `cloth.md`), which draw every Hair and Cloth value on a material its model file marks hair or cloth. Drawn since 2026-11-17 (CO7's first slice): `tint`, `iris_tint`, `sclera_tint`, `roughness_scale`, `detail_strength` (the normal map's strength) and the pigments `melanin`, `undertone` and `redness`, which tone the whole albedo by one colour ratio from the references' indicative tone table (`crates/scene/src/skin_tone.rs`). So lips, nails, palms and soles tone with the rest (a deep tone darkens the lips with the skin; per-texel masks wait for the Skin shading model), the table's light end leans lavender-pink and is not yet recomputed from the pigment spectra, and on a slot its file doesn't mark hair (the realistic brows and lashes, blended cards) Hair's `melanin` and `redness` take the skin's curve. Not drawn: `pupil_size`, `limbal_ring` and `iris_depth` (the Eye model; the pupil has the `Pupil_Size` shape meanwhile).
- **The realistic family's first hairstyle is a draft** (R6, 2026-11-18; `assets/characters/realistic/hair/`, `docs/development/character-studio/refinement/wave4/r6-hair-first.md`). A short textured crop of cards and a cap, built by script from a groom on the realistic scalp, on both frames, following the head's shapes; 27,600 / 16,000 / 470 triangles. It fails asset-quality §4 as measured: the hairline's ramp is 2 to 7 mm (8 required), 8% of the scalp has less than the declared 8 mm of volume, the masculine front shows 5.7% scalp (with the hair's shadow) against 2%, a vertex or two sits inside the head by the ear at some parameter ends and in Walk and Run, and close up it reads as brush strokes with pale flat patches (cards and cap lit by a broad grey sheen) and a pinwheel at the crown's whorl. `avatar::randomize` never draws an item marked `draft` (asset-quality §5), so Randomize doesn't wear it; the Hair page lists it until the browsers' "Show drafts" filter is merged. Stubble's density map is in the skin masks' spare channel; the Skin model doesn't draw it yet (its uniform is full). A hair material needs a normal map to get tangents: `assets` makes them only for materials with one.
- **Hair and cloth shading: limits** (2026-11-18, CO7 wave 4; `docs/rendering/hair.md`, `cloth.md`). No garment uses the Cloth model yet; the first hairstyle (a draft, above) uses Hair. Under TAA a hair card's hashed alpha leaves a little flicker: 3.38 of 255 root mean square over a card whose opacity ramps clear to whole, most of it at the hashed band's bottom (opacity 0.2 to 0.3), where TAA's neighbourhood clamp drops lone pixels; the limit (4.5) is provisional until the first hairstyle's sign-off. Without TAA or MSAA hair cards are cut hard. No strands or deep opacity shadows; no wet hair; ray traced reflections and GI see hair and cloth as made. Cloth reads `KHR_materials_sheen` and `KHR_materials_anisotropy`'s factors, not their textures; no fuzz layer or thin-fabric transmission. Only hair cards cast through their holes in the shadow maps: every other cut-out (leaf cards, fences, grates) casts its whole card, as before CO7, because casting foliage through its holes took the Shadows pass from 0.19 to 0.42 ms for 36,000 leaf cards at 1080p High (`docs/rendering/shadows.md`); a hair card with a texture tiled by its size casts its whole card too. Hair lit from behind: its light fades over the head's terminator only on a shell (single-sided hair, or a layer whose file sets `axcenity_hair_shell`); loose double-sided cards that close into a surface still show the shadow map's self-shadow edge as a torn band, so a hairstyle marks every layer lying on the head. Ray traced shadows and AO skip hair cards nearer the scalp than about 16 mm at 3 studs (`cut_out_near`), untested on a real hairstyle. A hair card's shadow is hashed over opacity 0.2 to 0.8 under every anti-aliasing, so without TAA or MSAA a fringe that isn't drawn still casts a faint stipple. **OPEN-19** (a cut-out card on a skinned, morphed part showing the backdrop through the skin) doesn't reproduce in the renderer since CO8's compute posing; a test pins it under Off, MSAA, TAA and TAA with AO.
- **Realistic skin: shading limits** (2026-11-17, R5; `docs/development/character-studio/refinement/r5/`). Fixed: the grain and the stippled terminator (the Skin shading model reads the normal map blurred for diffuse light, and the grain's cells are now 5 texels and half as steep), under TAA and MSAA alike; heavy bodies no longer show abdominal lines (a smooth and a defined normal map, blended by muscle and fat), and keep their navel (the smooth map keeps the sculpt's forms within 2 cm of it); since the review fix (2026-11-17) no saturated red fills an ear's fold, a nostril's corner, the crease under the eye or the nose's shadow on the upper lip, and a passing cloud doesn't redden skin; since the second review fix a normal map's slope past the triangles' own terminator (the navel's inner wall in a body's shadowed half) no longer glows. Left: under a hard light a few dozen pixels of a face (an ear's rim, the lips' edges) are still a little redder than before R5 (red over green up to about 2.4 in sRGB, against 2.0); there is no screen-space subsurface blur or transmission, so light doesn't bleed past shadow-casting geometry (ears and nostrils lit from behind stay opaque); the curvature that sets how far red spreads comes from screen-space derivatives, constant per triangle; close up the grain is now pores and fine lines rather than a network of cells (since the R5 makeup branch; it read as cracked skin), and since its review the Skin model draws pores and fine lines finer than the maps from the texture coordinates (about 100 pores to a square centimetre on the face); they fade out where a pore cell is under two pixels, so from a face-front distance down they leave no trace, and their pattern is the same on every character (seeded by the texture coordinates, not the description); the smooth normal map flattens the soft tissue's forms by 90% everywhere fat covers it, not by region. Without TAA (High and Ultra default to MSAA 4×, and Character Studio's viewport uses the editor's graphics settings) contact shadows keep their fixed pattern, so a limb in front of the body from the camera's view (not between the body and the sun) still casts a false screen-space contact shadow (it passes contact shadows' 0.5-stud thickness test); a fix is a thinner thickness for characters, or testing the blocker's depth gap against the march distance. The chest's dark band under the studio light (OPEN-09) is the light grazing the upper chest: the shadow terminator problem of a coarse mesh with smooth normals; not fixed.
- **Makeup and markings: limits** (R5 makeup branch, [ADR-0042](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0042-Makeup-And-Markings-Are-Layers-Over-Library-Masks-Drawn-Live-By-The-Skin-Model.md)). Nine fixed layers (foundation, blush, eyeshadow, eyeliner, lip colour, brow fill, freckles, moles, one scar on the left cheek): the masks' channel layout is fixed, so a new kind needs a fourth mask or the decal path, and tattoos, birthmarks and placed marks are not built (ADR-0042's decals). The masks are 2K like the skin maps, so eyeliner is two to three texels wide on the face and its wing (since the review, 6.6 mm along the lower lid's line at 22 degrees) is a short flick; brow fill barely shows round the realistic brows' dense card. Freckles and moles are a fixed population of 13,000 and 70 candidate spots chosen by amount and seed: a seed can't move a spot, only pick others. Ray-traced reflections and GI see the skin without makeup. A skin with a layer on loses its own emissive and opacity maps (none of the shipped skins has them).
- **Generated garments: the intake's limits** (2026-11-24, [ADR-0044](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0044-Garments-Not-Made-On-The-Body-Come-Through-An-Intake-Before-The-Fit.md); `avatar::fit::prepare`). A garment must stand upright, facing +Z, in a relaxed pose; a sleeve held level is placed but not turned to the arm. A garment made of loose sheets (a pleated skirt modelled as strips, a suit of overlapping pieces) has no trunk the reading can follow: 2 of the 32 fail this way. Its size is a guess from its shape (sleeves to the wrists, legs to the ankles, else the chest or seat with an ease) and can be given. A fitted item is 8 to 30 MB, mostly its carried shapes (the 30 in Character Studio's library: 446 MB, 591 MB before their corners were merged). **What the mending leaves** (2026-11-24 evening; `fit/mend.rs`, `intake/centre.rs`, `intake/weld.rs`): a top and a bottom worn together are their own entry, below ("Wardrobe pack 0.3.0"); a hole wider than 12 cm, or one a patch would close as a web, stays open (the turtleneck's chest by each sleeve; thin slits on the wide-leg jeans); a patch takes its rim's texture coordinates, so a large one smears the pattern; the cocktail dress's back fold stays crumpled and the biker jacket's shoulders pointed; a sleeve is centred on a straight arm, so one modelled much shorter or longer than the arm keeps its length. A generated garment is still one source for both frames.

- **Worn garments hide what is under them; what that leaves** (2026-11-24, [ADR-0045](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0045-What-Is-Worn-Over-Skin-Or-Cloth-Hides-It-By-A-Cover-Map.md); `crates/avatar/src/fit/cover.rs`, `crates/assets/src/hidden.rs`). Garments imported apart are still not pushed clear of each other: the outer one hides the skin and the inner one's cloth under it by its cover map, set back from its openings, so within the setback (3 mm to 4 cm from a hem) a thicker inner garment can still stand through a snug outer one. The cover map is made on the default body at rest: cloth that slides far over the skin in a pose can uncover hidden skin (on the test tee, a few corners of the side under the arm, up to 13 mm, at the widest swing of the walk and the run: recorded in `crates/player/src/fit_gate_tests.rs` and `garment_checks.rs`). Skin stays drawn within 2.5 cm of every opening and wherever the cloth comes off it in one of the fit's poses (since 2026-11-24 evening hidden skin is tried in those poses, not judged by matching weights: a median of 36% of the skin under a garment is hidden, 8% to 72%), so there the fit's 2 mm margin is still all that keeps it from showing. Items fitted by `shapes` (the realistic underwear) and items made before this borrow the body's texture coordinates when worn under something, and are hidden where they are well under it; a ring of their triangles stays at the cover's edge, so on a body far from the default one a triangle of a bra can still show through a tank top near its neckline (two on the heavy feminine body), and a strap shows at a wide neckline, as it would. Tops are always over the bottoms of their layer (no tucked-in tops). A generated garment whose sleeve was modelled joined to its side still tears, or shows a hole, under an arm held level or raised (`FitOptions::cut_webs` takes out only what a pose draws out five times; the cure is a source in an A-pose), and a coat's or dress's skirt, hung from the hips with part of each leg's motion, is crossed by a leg in a long stride. The intake's conforming draws a patch of cloth in by its mean clearance: it does not make a garment cut for one frame fit the other well, which is why such garments say who they are cut for and the Clothing page lists them by frame. The first realistic wardrobe (tee, joggers, sneakers) has cover maps only once rebuilt through the import, and its combined models don't carry the skin coordinates yet.
- **The online wardrobe is Character Studio's only, and one pack** (2026-11-24; `crates/editor/src/character_workspace/wardrobe.rs`, `crates/toolbox/src/character_pack.rs`): the Clothing page downloads garments from the Axcenity Wardrobe at a fixed address; no other pack can be added, its outfits aren't offered, and the Game Editor's Toolbox tab has no clothing kind. A downloaded garment's credit (CC BY 4.0 asks for one) isn't written to Build Game's `CREDITS.txt`: Copy credit line is the way. - **The first realistic wardrobe is three drafts** (2026-11-18, R7 wave 4; `assets/characters/realistic/clothing/`, `refinement/wave4/r7-wardrobe-first.md`): a crew-neck tee (FreeSewing Teagan), joggers (Paco) and court sneakers, made by a sewn-and-draped pipeline and fitted by `avatar::fit`, waiting for Paul's sign-off of each kind. Character Studio doesn't hide drafts yet (asset-quality §5's "Show drafts" toggle and "Draft" mark are not built; nothing in the editor reads `ItemManifest::is_draft`), so the three drafts, the broken sneakers among them, are listed in its wardrobe for the realistic base as ordinary items. Since 2026-11-24 the tee and the joggers have a **cover map** (made by `cover_map` from the shipped models; `wardrobe.py` carries the import's on a rebuild), so cloth worn under them is hidden as under any other garment: the boxer briefs through the joggers went from 9.0 to 4.6 mm, the bra through the tee in Idle from 16.0 to 7.1 mm, and the briefs no longer come through the joggers. Their maps' **red is empty**, so they hide no skin and the numbers above for skin stand: with it, the gate found hidden skin out from under the cloth in the walk and the run (up to 22 mm on the heavyset feminine body). The sneakers have none: the gate's check for holes doesn't count footwear as cover. The tee's file itself is at fault round the hips on the default body: of its corners at the height of the boxer briefs 6.8% are inside the skin (down to 29 mm, measured against the shipped body at rest) and 8.4% inside the briefs, which show through it as dark patches in Character Studio; it needs building again from its sources (`build_tee.py`, which needs the fabric tiles).
- **Binding-fitted items: the fitting core's limits** (CO9, `avatar::fit`: a garment imported or built takes the body's weights and identity and corrective shapes, pushed out of the body and lower layers). Its limits, measured on the test tee (`crates/avatar/tests/fixtures/test-tee`, a draft with a closed underarm, sleeves past the armpit and, since wave 4, an ordinary crew neck lying on the skin, the sports bra's straps having moved onto the shoulders; it fits at rest, in the T-pose and with the elbows or knees bent, and with the arms raised on all but two bodies, up to 5.1 mm): in the walk the chest at the front of the armpit and the side under it come out of it as the arm swings back (every one of the 54 gate bodies, up to 30 mm; the cloth bridging the arm and the side follows the arm), and the bra comes through it at the armpit (21 bodies, 17 mm). Hiding the skin it covers is no way round this: CO9 part 2's review fixes 1 hid the torso and the upper arm under the sleeve and the walk record fell to 50 bodies at 23.7 mm, but the hidden skin came out from under the cloth (holes: up to 38 mm in the walk on 38 bodies, the arm's edge bitten away below the sleeve's end in 4K captures, and 45 mm at rest under the arm on 25 heavy bodies), so since review fixes 2 an import hides only regions whose skin stays covered in every fit pose (`avatar::fit::pose_safe_hides`; the tee: the waist) and the gate counts holes in every pose. The cause is the arm hanging against the side, not its swing: the tee's underarm, made with the arm at 45 degrees, can't fold into the crease, and the arms hanging alone fail the same. Wave 4's swing correctives (`Corrective_Shoulder_Forward_*`, `_Back_*`) take the body's own fold out (the 95th percentile of its depth 10.6 to 4.9 mm) but not this; the import's posed fit (the garment's copies of the correctives made to follow the skin) barely changes it, and pushing the cloth out in the pose made every pose worse. The tee's armpit also jolts in the motion check (4.1 mm a frame in the walk, 11.7 in the run, `player` `garment_checks`). Loose garments need cloth proxies, deformer maps or a garment made with the arms down (ADR-0035). The import's fine alignment can't tell a size a little bigger from a more even ease (about 2%), and it assumes the same ease all round: a shirt lying on the shoulders with its collar on the skin (the wave-4 test tee) made 5% small and 3 cm high is only 13% more even at its true place, under the 20% and 2 mm the fine fit needs, so it isn't moved (the alignment test uses the wave-3 tee, `TestTeeEvenEase.obj`).
- **A character's parts pick their levels of detail one by one.** A small part (underwear, eyes, brows) switches to a simpler copy nearer the camera than the body does, and a simplified garment doesn't keep its clearance from the skin: from about 9 studs the stylized character's skin shows through its underwear in blotches (seen in CO2's in-engine captures, which record each part's level in `captures.json`; confirmed in CO9 part 2's review fixes: the capture draws the underwear at level 1 and the body at 0, and the masculine briefs forced to full detail with `AXCENITY_CAPTURE_FULL_DETAIL` show no skin; the feminine briefs at full detail still show skin on the right buttock in the walk, their own clearance, below). Fixing it is CO18's "character LOD sets switched together", with CO9's region hiding (the body under a garment isn't drawn) and CO4's constrained LODs; until then judge clothing fit in the captures' full-detail view and in the Blender harness.
- **Compute posing (CO8) has a ceiling and a few gaps** (2026-11-17, [ADR-0041](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0041-Shared-Base-Mesh-With-Live-Identity-Morphs-Baking-Is-Optional.md), `docs/rendering/animation.md`). Each animated character is now morphed (only shapes with a weight) and skinned once a frame, and every pass and ray tracing read that: 50 realistic characters of distinct live descriptions take 1.5 ms of GPU on an RX 9070 XT against 9.6 ms before, and 20 take 1.2 ms against 4.7 ms (`docs/development/character-studio/refinement/wave3/co8-compute-posing.md`). Left: the posing arena (every posed mesh's corners, joints and weights, and the moves of each shape that has been active) must fit one storage binding (128 MB by default; 50 distinct baked characters take 80 MB with its slack), and items past it are drawn at rest with a warning; the arena and the CPU copy each animated mesh keeps of its joints, weights and shapes cost memory the per-pass path didn't (7.5 MB of posing buffers for one live realistic character, 45 MB for 50); 20 live realistic characters (1.22 ms) are still slower than 20 stylized ones were (0.97 ms), the gap being the realistic skin material and the posing pass itself; and GI traced in software (`ray_soft.rs`, GPUs without ray queries) still leaves skinned items out.
- **Identities registered in a run are kept for the run** (audit D4). Each distinct identity the applier registers (`Assets::register_identity`) stays in `Assets` until the program ends. A realistic identity names about 150 shapes, even those at 0, plus its bone changes: about 8 to 10 KB each (estimated from its entries, not measured), and one more per part with item parameters. Since R2a removed Character Studio's 120 ms drag throttle, a slider drag registers one every frame: about 0.6 to 1.4 MB a second of continuous dragging at 60 to 144 Hz (about 80 KB a second before), so a long editing session can reach hundreds of MB. A prune of keys no MeshPart names, run when a drag ends, is the planned fix. `anim`'s cache of how each model wears each identity is bounded (1,024 entries, more only while a crowd wears them). A baked character's derived model is still made again in every run and whenever its base model is reloaded, never cooked.

## Editor

- Script completion and Go to Definition (F12) follow chains and declarations but doesn't type-check: a value it can't follow (a function's result, a table field, a module's return) offers nothing, luau-lsp ships in every package (a source build downloads it on request) and follows `require(game.ReplicatedStorage.X)` and `GetService` paths into modules (not `require(script.Parent.X)`, nor children's types such as `workspace.Door`); only open tabs are checked, and VS Code's set-up still checks each file alone.
- Panics while drawing the Play world aren't caught.
- **Group as Mesh** carries what a glTF material can hold. A Part's built-in Material texture (Wood, Brick, Concrete…, drawn from maps `scene` makes in memory) is grouped in its color; MaterialVariants, a Part's SurfaceAppearance, opacity and specular maps, DirectX normal maps and Decals aren't carried either (Output names each part). Hidden faces are only taken away when one solid part, or opposite faces in one plane, cover them; a face hidden by two overlapping parts together stays. Faces only partly pressed together stay whole (`docs/architecture/solid-modeling.md`).
- Model files aren't watched for changes while the editor runs (read again on open).
- **Scatter masks, ground clutter and the decal brush** (2026-11-23, `docs/editor/scatter.md`): masks act under the brush only (no fill of an area, no preview of a mask in the viewport), and moisture is the terrain's shape (hollows wet, rises dry), not water or rainfall. Ground clutter is painted foliage, so it counts toward a terrain's 32,768 instances (the Ground Clutter palette at its own density fills about a 190-stud square), and painted built-in props, its Rock included, have no cull distance (`scene::foliage`: only plants and models do), so a lot of gravel is drawn however far away it is: keep it to the places the camera gets close to. Scattered decals are ProjectedDecals: none is drawn at Low quality, the nearest 128 within 150 studs at Medium, 512 within 300 at High and 1,024 within 600 at Ultra, so they dress a place rather than cover a map; the brush's limit is a rule of thumb counted from each decal, not from the camera. The decal brush needs a Terrain to follow. A TerrainPath's shoulder is terrain paint, one weight a sample, so its ragged edge is no finer than the terrain's samples.
- **Render Movie** (`docs/editor/movie.md`): movies have no sound; videos need an `ffmpeg` on the machine (a PNG sequence doesn't); level sections aren't loaded; a hardware H.264 encoder that `ffmpeg` lists can still fail when the movie starts. A camera cut's **Dissolve** is drawn only in rendered movies: the game and the Sequencer's preview play it as a cut, because it draws the world twice a frame. In the game the cutscene overlay's subtitle is a fixed 28 pixels, small on a 4K screen (a movie scales it by its height).
- **Environment presets** (`docs/project/sequences.md`): a preset holds numbers, colors, vectors, switches and the `Precipitation` choice, not text (a `SkyImage`). Nothing is made at run time, so a preset's clouds, weather and haze need Clouds, a Weather and an Atmosphere in Lighting (the editor's apply adds the first two). The weather drawn still follows the Weather's `TransitionTime`, so a blend quicker than it lags unless it's 0. `Lighting:TransitionToPreset` fires no event when it starts or arrives and changes the device it's called on (call it from a Script). Preset files aren't Assets panel tiles and have no rename or delete in the editor. Asset Check doesn't check the presets a sequence names.
- Bakes (reflection probes, light probe volumes) go stale and must be rebaked by hand (flagged). A bake runs a little each frame with progress and Cancel, but each probe still reads its six faces back one at a time, so thousands of probes take minutes.

- **A SurfaceAppearance's normal map on a model whose own material has none is drawn without tangents** (every corner's tangent is `render::mesh::NO_TANGENT`, +X in the model), because `assets` works tangents out only for a material with a normal map of its own. Found by Group as Mesh's picture test on the `mesh` sample's ATM: the grouped model's material links the map itself, gets real tangents, and its bumps light differently (more correctly) than the separate MeshPart's.

## Content and platforms

- References may name an asset by path or by ID (PR #78); older projects still use paths.
- Built games ship cooked textures without their sources (G12); what the CPU reads of such a texture (terrain layers, color grading lookups, UI images) is decoded from BC7 or BC5, so it's close to the source, not equal. Model files (FBX, glTF) still ship beside their cooked copies.
- macOS is not verified on changes; mobile and web are not supported.
- No binary world format (S10k's 10 MB `world.json` loads in 0.19 s since C11).
