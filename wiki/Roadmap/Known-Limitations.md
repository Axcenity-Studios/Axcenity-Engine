# Known limitations

What the engine doesn't do, or does with a known flaw, today. Each entry names where it's documented. Performance issues are in [Known bottlenecks](../Performance/Known-Bottlenecks.md); structural problems in [Technical debt](Technical-Debt.md).

## Scripting

- No per-script **memory limit**; long C functions (`string.rep`, pathological patterns) aren't interrupted by the watchdog (`docs/scripting/sandbox.md`).
- `task.defer` runs at the next step (Roblox: later in the same frame); a RemoteEvent message with no handler is dropped (Roblox queues it); `ContextActionResult.Pass` and touch buttons not implemented (`docs/scripting/client-server.md`).
- Vector3 math allocates a userdata per result; `--!native` doesn't speed up engine calls.
- A breakpoint inside a C-called Lua function (a `table.sort` comparator), or in a script-made coroutine resumed from one, can't pause; a step goes on to the next line where it can. The same goes for a conditional breakpoint, a logpoint and Pause (Pause waits for the next script line that can pause, so a game whose scripts are all waiting pauses only when one wakes). Logpoints and conditions make their line yield on every pass, which slows a hot loop.

## Rendering

- Overlapping see-through items can blend in the wrong order (sorted by center).
- AMD/Vulkan/Windows: the sky clear isn't sRGB-encoded through an sRGB view (`docs/rendering/pipeline.md`).
- Lights are tiled in 2D, not clustered; rect lights use a representative point.
- No vertex colors or `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` from glTF; FBX uses the first UV set; glTF animations are read as bone takes, but their morph target weight channels aren't.
- Humanoid retargeting finds roles by bone names or by a rig definition of the project's (`assets/rigs/*.rig.json`); a retargeted take moves role bones only, so a bone without a role keeps its place (`docs/rendering/animation.md`).

## Physics

- MeshParts collide as convex hulls (hollows filled) unless already convex and small, their model has `_COL` collision pieces, or they're set to `PreciseConvexDecomposition` (R15.2). The Model Studio's Convex Pieces (R15.2b) runs on the UI thread and can take a few seconds on a big model.
- Setting `Position` stops a part (Roblox keeps its speed).
- Ragdolls can exceed joint limits briefly on hard landings.

## Networking

- **Game connections trust a typed address on first use** (MP4; a join link pins the fingerprint); a bare join code trusts the relay. Port mapping (MP5b) can't get past a provider's router (carrier-grade NAT; a relay can), and a killed host leaves its mapping until the 30-minute lease ends.
- No matchmaking or public relay (anyone can run `axcenity-relay`, MP5a), and prediction covers only characters (MP6a, under `Players.CharacterAuthority = "Server"`; physics bodies are MP6b; lag compensation and replays exist, Phase 81). Multiplayer has been measured with 16 bots across emulated home routers and 32 on one machine; a 16–32 client run on real machines (G13, MP7) is planned. All of it is planned in `docs/research/multiplayer-readiness.md` (MP1–MP7).
- **Server-moved characters** (MP6a): other players are drawn in the past on a device, so walking into a moving player costs a correction; moving platforms aren't rewound; seats, `MoveTo` and `MoveDirection` don't drive a remote player's character, and a device Script's `Jump` can be lost in a replay.
- Editor Play can't host or join a real network game.

## Characters

The Character Runtime (Character Studio overhaul CO3; [Characters](../Engine-Systems/Characters.md#applying-a-description-character-runtime), `docs/development/character-studio/architecture.md`):

- **Character Studio's interface is CO12's first part.** The workspace edits format 2 descriptions on any body of the library (the realistic one by default), previews them through the applier, and draws its Body, Face, Hair and Clothing pages from the manifest and catalogue (`docs/editor/character-studio.md`). R1 added presets from the library by kind (with a person's own and favourites), Randomize with scopes and locks, held versions (before and after, side by side) and autosave with recovery; wave 3 the asymmetry tools (mirror or reset a side) and a pose and motion preview (Still, Idle, Walk at the Walk take's own speed, Run, five gestures; the camera follows the head). Not yet: a wardrobe browser with thumbnails and search, outfit, hair and skin presets (none shipped), a turntable, and the round trip with the Game Editor (the rest of CO12). The Walk and Run previews play in place: the character doesn't travel, so a planted foot slides back along the floor as on a treadmill.
 The realistic body has no clothes or hair to wear yet beyond its underwear (boxer briefs, briefs, a sports bra, cut from the body itself), (CO10, CO11). Both families are offered (Paul's decision 7); since wave 3 the stylized one has presets too (four bodies a frame end, four faces, three characters), made with its parameters and checked only for soundness, not against references or captures.
 An installed editor offers it only when its payload has `assets/characters/` (`packaging/stage.py` stages it from this change on). A pack added to a project puts its characters in `assets/characters/descriptions/`, but a game script can't yet load a description by path: it sets a `HumanoidDescription`'s `Data` to the file's text.
- **Editing on the character covers seven features** (R2, `docs/editor/character-studio.md`). Every feature can be hovered and clicked, but only the nose, eyes, jaw, mouth, shoulders, waist and hips have handles (brief §3.4's first set; the realistic family only, the stylized manifest has no features or handles). The face's parameters move the skin a few millimetres over their whole range, so seen whole (the Face view) a range is about 10 points on the screen: the studio gives it 240 points instead (ux patterns' fallback), and the skin follows the pointer one to one only when zoomed in that far. The waist handle moves the skin 19 mm over its range (the waist slider is weak, R3). The tint, its outline and the handles are drawn by egui over the preview, so what they may cover is found by a CPU depth buffer of the posed character (a texel per 2 points, rebuilt each frame something is selected or hovered; `direct/depth.rs`), not by the renderer's own depth. Where an arm or the nose crosses a feature, the tint stops a triangle short of the crossing (whole triangles are dropped, not cut at the occluder's edge), and a handle within about 6 points of skin that shows is drawn: at 70 degrees the far eye's lid handle sits at the nose's outline, where that lid peeks past it. Hair and clothing hide only skin more than 1.5 cm behind them. A face feature's tint stops short at the eyes' corners and inside the nostrils (hollows), the lips' reaches about 1.3 cm past the mouth's corners (its width shapes move that skin), and the jaw's, seen from the front, is a sliver at the near jaw angle. A body region's outline follows the region's border along the mesh's edges. The long-term route is the tint as draw data, depth-tested in `render`. Hovering casts a ray against the posed body's 25,000 triangles (after a CPU skinning of the frame's pose) each frame the pointer or the camera moves, and the overlay rasterizes the posed character each frame something is selected or hovered: in a release build, the studio's frame is 0.16 ms with editing on the character off, 3.56 ms with a feature selected and 4.61 ms with the pointer moving over the face (debug: about 20 ms for the ray alone); a still pointer keeps what it found. The handles drive the slider's values in a straight line: a parameter whose skin moves on a curve (a tilt) is followed only near its middle.
- **The editor's edit mode doesn't apply kept descriptions.** `DescriptionSync` is stepped by the game systems, which run in Play, the player and the server. A saved character that had a description applied therefore shows its base meshes in the editor (the identity key its MeshParts name isn't registered in this run, so they wear nothing, and a baked character's derived `MeshId`s fall back to the base model) until Play (CO13).
- **A project made before CO3 has no character library until Insert > Character is used.** The library is written with the default character (`project::add_default_humanoid`: a new project with a character, Insert > Character, Character Studio's workspace folder). An older project gets it the next time Insert > Character is used; until then `Humanoid:ApplyDescription` fails with a message that says the project has no character library, and a packed game built from such a project has none.
- **Some material values aren't drawn yet, and skin tone is one ratio per material.** Eye materials draw as Standard; skin has its own shading model since R5 (`docs/rendering/skin.md`), which draws `scatter_scale`, `definition` and `softness` too, and hair and cloth since CO7's wave 4 (`docs/rendering/hair.md`, `cloth.md`), which draw every Hair and Cloth value on a material its model file marks hair or cloth. Drawn since 2026-11-17 (CO7's first slice): `tint`, `iris_tint`, `sclera_tint`, `roughness_scale`, `detail_strength` (the normal map's strength) and the pigments `melanin`, `undertone` and `redness`, which tone the whole albedo by one colour ratio from the references' indicative tone table (`crates/scene/src/skin_tone.rs`). So lips, nails, palms and soles tone with the rest (a deep tone darkens the lips with the skin; per-texel masks wait for the Skin shading model), the table's light end leans lavender-pink and is not yet recomputed from the pigment spectra, and on a slot its file doesn't mark hair (the realistic brows and lashes, blended cards) Hair's `melanin` and `redness` take the skin's curve. Not drawn: `pupil_size`, `limbal_ring` and `iris_depth` (the Eye model; the pupil has the `Pupil_Size` shape meanwhile).
- **Hair and cloth shading: limits** (2026-11-18, CO7 wave 4; `docs/rendering/hair.md`, `cloth.md`). No hairstyle or garment uses the models yet (the first are content's, to Paul's sign-off). Under TAA a hair card's hashed alpha leaves a little flicker: 3.38 of 255 root mean square over a card whose opacity ramps clear to whole, most of it at the hashed band's bottom (opacity 0.2 to 0.3), where TAA's neighbourhood clamp drops lone pixels; the limit (4.5) is provisional until the first hairstyle's sign-off. Without TAA or MSAA hair cards are cut hard. No strands or deep opacity shadows; no wet hair; ray traced reflections and GI see hair and cloth as made. Cloth reads `KHR_materials_sheen` and `KHR_materials_anisotropy`'s factors, not their textures; no fuzz layer or thin-fabric transmission. Only hair cards cast through their holes in the shadow maps: every other cut-out (leaf cards, fences, grates) casts its whole card, as before CO7, because casting foliage through its holes took the Shadows pass from 0.19 to 0.42 ms for 36,000 leaf cards at 1080p High (`docs/rendering/shadows.md`); a hair card with a texture tiled by its size casts its whole card too. Hair lit from behind: its light fades over the head's terminator only on a shell (single-sided hair, or a layer whose file sets `axcenity_hair_shell`); loose double-sided cards that close into a surface still show the shadow map's self-shadow edge as a torn band, so a hairstyle marks every layer lying on the head. Ray traced shadows and AO skip hair cards nearer the scalp than about 16 mm at 3 studs (`cut_out_near`), untested on a real hairstyle. A hair card's shadow is hashed over opacity 0.2 to 0.8 under every anti-aliasing, so without TAA or MSAA a fringe that isn't drawn still casts a faint stipple. **OPEN-19** (a cut-out card on a skinned, morphed part showing the backdrop through the skin) doesn't reproduce in the renderer since CO8's compute posing; a test pins it under Off, MSAA, TAA and TAA with AO.
- **Realistic skin: shading limits** (2026-11-17, R5; `docs/development/character-studio/refinement/r5/`). Fixed: the grain and the stippled terminator (the Skin shading model reads the normal map blurred for diffuse light, and the grain's cells are now 5 texels and half as steep), under TAA and MSAA alike; heavy bodies no longer show abdominal lines (a smooth and a defined normal map, blended by muscle and fat), and keep their navel (the smooth map keeps the sculpt's forms within 2 cm of it); since the review fix (2026-11-17) no saturated red fills an ear's fold, a nostril's corner, the crease under the eye or the nose's shadow on the upper lip, and a passing cloud doesn't redden skin; since the second review fix a normal map's slope past the triangles' own terminator (the navel's inner wall in a body's shadowed half) no longer glows. Left: under a hard light a few dozen pixels of a face (an ear's rim, the lips' edges) are still a little redder than before R5 (red over green up to about 2.4 in sRGB, against 2.0); there is no screen-space subsurface blur or transmission, so light doesn't bleed past shadow-casting geometry (ears and nostrils lit from behind stay opaque); the curvature that sets how far red spreads comes from screen-space derivatives, constant per triangle; close up the grain is now pores and fine lines rather than a network of cells (since the R5 makeup branch; it read as cracked skin), and since its review the Skin model draws pores and fine lines finer than the maps from the texture coordinates (about 100 pores to a square centimetre on the face); they fade out where a pore cell is under two pixels, so from a face-front distance down they leave no trace, and their pattern is the same on every character (seeded by the texture coordinates, not the description); the smooth normal map flattens the soft tissue's forms by 90% everywhere fat covers it, not by region. Without TAA (High and Ultra default to MSAA 4×, and Character Studio's viewport uses the editor's graphics settings) contact shadows keep their fixed pattern, so a limb in front of the body from the camera's view (not between the body and the sun) still casts a false screen-space contact shadow (it passes contact shadows' 0.5-stud thickness test); a fix is a thinner thickness for characters, or testing the blocker's depth gap against the march distance. The chest's dark band under the studio light (OPEN-09) is the light grazing the upper chest: the shadow terminator problem of a coarse mesh with smooth normals; not fixed.
- **Makeup and markings: limits** (R5 makeup branch, [ADR-0042](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0042-Makeup-And-Markings-Are-Layers-Over-Library-Masks-Drawn-Live-By-The-Skin-Model.md)). Nine fixed layers (foundation, blush, eyeshadow, eyeliner, lip colour, brow fill, freckles, moles, one scar on the left cheek): the masks' channel layout is fixed, so a new kind needs a fourth mask or the decal path, and tattoos, birthmarks and placed marks are not built (ADR-0042's decals). The masks are 2K like the skin maps, so eyeliner is two to three texels wide on the face and its wing (since the review, 6.6 mm along the lower lid's line at 22 degrees) is a short flick; brow fill barely shows round the realistic brows' dense card. Freckles and moles are a fixed population of 13,000 and 70 candidate spots chosen by amount and seed: a seed can't move a spot, only pick others. Ray-traced reflections and GI see the skin without makeup. A skin with a layer on loses its own emissive and opacity maps (none of the shipped skins has them).
- **The first realistic wardrobe is three drafts** (2026-11-18, R7 wave 4; `assets/characters/realistic/clothing/`, `refinement/wave4/r7-wardrobe-first.md`): a crew-neck tee (FreeSewing Teagan), joggers (Paco) and court sneakers, made by a sewn-and-draped pipeline and fitted by `avatar::fit`, waiting for Paul's sign-off of each kind. None passes asset-quality §2 and §3, so none is in a preset or drawn by Randomize: skin comes out of the joggers under the cuffs at the back of the ankles (53 of 54 bodies, up to 31 mm at rest) and of the tee round the waist over the joggers' waistband (38 bodies, 28 mm; every body in the walk and with the arms raised, up to 42 mm in Idle); the joggers' drawcord and waistband come through the tee's hem (every body, 18 mm), the boxer briefs through the joggers (33 bodies, 9 mm), the bra through the tee (21 bodies, 5.4 mm); the tee's crew neck is faceted (22 straight runs at LOD0, up to 39 mm) with tabs of the shell above the band at the shoulder seams, and its lower back bulges out over the seat (roughly 80 to 100 mm, read off the side views) with the hem flaring at the hips on heavyset bodies; the simpler copies cut into the skin (up to 31 mm); the tee's armpit jolts in motion (2.4 mm a frame in the walk, 5.1 in the run); the sneakers let the heel and toes out in the stride (up to 11.3 mm) and their tread tilts with the foot (up to 9.8 mm off flat); texture coordinates are well short of §2 on most materials (the tee's jersey 51.7% within 10% stretch, the sneakers' upper 3.4%; only the tee's rib and the shoes' lining have 8 px/cm on all of their area); two hem corners are turned less than 2 mm (the tee's side hem 1.4 mm, a joggers' cuff 1.92 mm) and drawn quads aren't folds the cloth had in the bent poses (binding has no compression folds at the elbows and knees). Every number is a record in `realistic_wardrobe.rs` and `wardrobe_tests.rs`. Randomize skips an item that says `quality: "draft"`; items made before the standard (no `quality`) are still drawn. Character Studio doesn't hide drafts yet (asset-quality §5's "Show drafts" toggle and "Draft" mark are not built; nothing in the editor reads `ItemManifest::is_draft`), so the three drafts, the broken sneakers among them, are listed in its wardrobe for the realistic base as ordinary items.
- **Binding-fitted items: the fitting core's limits** (CO9, `avatar::fit`: a garment imported or built takes the body's weights and identity and corrective shapes, pushed out of the body and lower layers). Its limits, measured on the test tee (`crates/avatar/tests/fixtures/test-tee`, a draft with a closed underarm, sleeves past the armpit and, since wave 4, an ordinary crew neck lying on the skin, the sports bra's straps having moved onto the shoulders; it fits at rest, in the T-pose and with the elbows or knees bent, and with the arms raised on all but two bodies, up to 5.1 mm): in the walk the chest at the front of the armpit and the inside of the upper arm come out of it (every one of the 54 gate bodies, up to 30 mm), and the bra comes through it at the armpit (21 bodies, 17 mm). The cause is the arm hanging against the side, not its swing: the tee's underarm, made with the arm at 45 degrees, can't fold into the crease, and the arms hanging alone fail the same. Wave 4's swing correctives (`Corrective_Shoulder_Forward_*`, `_Back_*`) take the body's own fold out (the 95th percentile of its depth 10.6 to 4.9 mm) but not this; the import's posed fit (the garment's copies of the correctives made to follow the skin) barely changes it, and pushing the cloth out in the pose made every pose worse. The tee's armpit also jolts in the motion check (4.1 mm a frame in the walk, 11.7 in the run, `player` `garment_checks`). Loose garments need cloth proxies, deformer maps or a garment made with the arms down (ADR-0035). The import's fine alignment can't tell a size a little bigger from a more even ease (about 2%), and it assumes the same ease all round: a shirt lying on the shoulders with its collar on the skin (the wave-4 test tee) made 5% small and 3 cm high is only 13% more even at its true place, under the 20% and 2 mm the fine fit needs, so it isn't moved (the alignment test uses the wave-3 tee, `TestTeeEvenEase.obj`).
- **A character's parts pick their levels of detail one by one.** A small part (underwear, eyes, brows) switches to a simpler copy nearer the camera than the body does, and a simplified garment doesn't keep its clearance from the skin: from about 9 studs the stylized character's skin shows through its underwear in blotches (seen in CO2's in-engine captures, which record each part's level in `captures.json`; confirmed in CO9 part 2's review fixes: the capture draws the underwear at level 1 and the body at 0, and the briefs forced to full detail with `AXCENITY_CAPTURE_FULL_DETAIL` show no skin at all). Fixing it is CO18's "character LOD sets switched together", with CO9's region hiding (the body under a garment isn't drawn) and CO4's constrained LODs; until then judge clothing fit in the captures' full-detail view and in the Blender harness.
- **Compute posing (CO8) has a ceiling and a few gaps** (2026-11-17, [ADR-0041](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0041-Shared-Base-Mesh-With-Live-Identity-Morphs-Baking-Is-Optional.md), `docs/rendering/animation.md`). Each animated character is now morphed (only shapes with a weight) and skinned once a frame, and every pass and ray tracing read that: 50 realistic characters of distinct live descriptions take 1.5 ms of GPU on an RX 9070 XT against 9.6 ms before, and 20 take 1.2 ms against 4.7 ms (`docs/development/character-studio/refinement/wave3/co8-compute-posing.md`). Left: the posing arena (every posed mesh's corners, joints and weights, and the moves of each shape that has been active) must fit one storage binding (128 MB by default; 50 distinct baked characters take 80 MB with its slack), and items past it are drawn at rest with a warning; the arena and the CPU copy each animated mesh keeps of its joints, weights and shapes cost memory the per-pass path didn't (7.5 MB of posing buffers for one live realistic character, 45 MB for 50); 20 live realistic characters (1.22 ms) are still slower than 20 stylized ones were (0.97 ms), the gap being the realistic skin material and the posing pass itself; and GI traced in software (`ray_soft.rs`, GPUs without ray queries) still leaves skinned items out.
- **Identities registered in a run are kept for the run** (audit D4). Each distinct identity the applier registers (`Assets::register_identity`) stays in `Assets` until the program ends. A realistic identity names about 150 shapes, even those at 0, plus its bone changes: about 8 to 10 KB each (estimated from its entries, not measured), and one more per part with item parameters. Since R2a removed Character Studio's 120 ms drag throttle, a slider drag registers one every frame: about 0.6 to 1.4 MB a second of continuous dragging at 60 to 144 Hz (about 80 KB a second before), so a long editing session can reach hundreds of MB. A prune of keys no MeshPart names, run when a drag ends, is the planned fix. `anim`'s cache of how each model wears each identity is bounded (1,024 entries, more only while a crowd wears them). A baked character's derived model is still made again in every run and whenever its base model is reloaded, never cooked.

The realistic base body (Character Studio overhaul CO4, built and waiting for review; [Characters](../Engine-Systems/Characters.md#the-realistic-base-body), `docs/development/character-studio/testing.md`). Numbers are **Measured** with the Blender QA harness (`measure.py --package realistic`; masculine / feminine frame) and kept in `docs/development/character-studio/co4/measurements.json`:

- **`axcenity/realistic` is not yet a full character family.** It has its body shapes (CO5), its head and face (CO6) and underwear, but no hair or other clothes, and the library written into a project is still the stylized package alone.
- **The realistic underwear is an interim fit** (2026-11-16): copied body shapes (37 to 53 per garment) and mask shapes that sink the skin under it 5 mm, an exception to [ADR-0035](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0035-Clothing-Fits-By-Surface-Binding-With-Region-Hiding.md) until CO9's region hiding; the briefs' back hem meets the gusset in a small crumple seen from below.
- **Map regions hide only what an item covers whole**, and the realistic regions are cut for a crew-neck T-shirt with short sleeves (since CO9 part 2's review fixes: the neck's skin is the neck's, and the upper arm has a third band to a short sleeve's end): such a shirt hides `torso`, `waist`, `upper_arms_top` and `upper_arms_mid`. Every map index is used (15 of 15), so a finer cut means a wider region layer. Hidden skin is not drawn even where it would show from below, up a sleeve into the armpit or under a hem: no check holds that (a ray test tried in review fixes 1 was not reliable and was dropped), and the standard capture views show no hole (`refinement/wave4/co9-fit-2.md`). Each distinct set of hidden regions is a model of its own (`…#h<key>`), so the body's mesh and shapes are uploaded once per outfit that hides something (CO9).
- **Fit defects the CO9 gate and checks found in shipped clothes** (all drafts; `player`'s `fit_gate_tests` and `garment_checks`, recorded per garment and pose or clip): the realistic boxer briefs let one skin corner by the gusset's edge through by 13.3 mm on the masculine heavy-soft body (17.9 mm in the walk), which the build's push-out, with the benchmark bodies among its states since wave 4, doesn't reach; on the stylized cast the tee counts the neck's side under its collar's rolled-in band (25 mm, a rule artefact) and the shoulder's top on the muscular feminine body (9 mm), the cargo pants the crotch (2.4 mm), the sports top comes through the tee at the sides of the chest (up to 8 mm in the T-pose), the sneakers let 1 to 4 foot corners out in the walk's stride (up to 18 mm) and their tread is not flat at rest (8.4 mm from end to end, 3.6 mm under the floor), the cargo pants' crotch jolts in the walk (5.6 mm a frame) and four of the six stylized hairstyles have corners up to 11 mm inside the head. The stylized clothes were not refitted in wave 4; the stylized tee's back hem crumpling into the cargo pants' waistband is not measured by the gate at rest or in the T-pose.
- **The realistic face is short of a photograph** (CO6, judged in 4K engine captures against reference photographs): no creases anywhere (no wrinkle maps); the eye's shade is a film between the lids that does not know where the light is, so the whites are lit alike in every light (eye shading is CO7); a closed lid under side light shows the head's own shadow edge as a lit blotch; a closed smile is milder than a real one; an open mouth's corners are points and its lower back teeth can be made out close up; the jaw fully open is bell-shaped; the U and WQ visemes show the front teeth; lashes are a card, and brows are cards drawn hair by hair (wave 3), which read as hair from the front, three-quarter and (the masculine tail) in profile but lie flat on the skin (no hair stands off it), and are at the short end of F-29's 3D length (59 and 51 mm against 69.5 and 61.4); `nose_tip_rotation`'s drooped end is held at about 2 SD of the nasolabial angle (-20 and -22 degrees), because a larger droop draws a thin blue-grey line under the columella (a grazing reflection of the backdrop on the lobule's lower edge once it turns down to subnasale's height; the skin has no specular occlusion in narrow creases); the pupil has no slider; some face parameters at their ends still cross the head's own triangles (the masculine `eye_size` -1 shows as a lit streak on the upper lid). The package's sliders check is red on one line from three false counts in its eye measure, which signs a point by one face where two tie at an edge; the measure is unchanged until the project owner decides. Details: `docs/development/character-studio/co6/README.md`.
- **The raised shoulder keeps less local volume than once targeted (decided, 2026-11-16).** With the arm raised 150 degrees and CO5's corrective shapes the shoulder keeps 0.463 / 0.513 of its local volume (the old target was 0.8; plain skinning gave 0.562 / 0.561). The local volume rewards an inflated cap (a shape that passed it showed a step and a ball in the engine), so the project owner decided the 150 degree shoulder is held by its ring area instead: 0.929 / 0.82, with a floor of 0.92 / 0.81 in the package's thresholds; the local volume is still measured and reported. A collarbone that lifts with the arm, as a real one does, would raise both and is left for the second wave. The arm lowered to hang, as the starter clips hold it, has its own corrective shapes since 2026-11-16: before them the cap squared off and a ridge rose beside the neck (the shoulder gained 12.5 / 9.3% of its local volume); with them it keeps within 2.4% of rest. At 90 degrees the shoulder keeps 0.847 / 0.88 against targets of 0.853 / 0.992 (the stylized body's), printed as not met. The other CO4 targets are met: the elbow at 135 degrees keeps 0.757 / 0.758 and the knee 0.74 / 0.75 of the ring area (0.6 or more), no face vertex above the head joint carries `Neck` weight (the stylized body has 911), and eyes and teeth don't interpenetrate at rest or at any face parameter's end (`docs/development/character-studio/stage3/`).
- **Still short elsewhere on that body:** the fist self-intersects (247 / 199 triangle pairs). The teeth touching the lower lip with the jaw open and the lips resting 2 mm apart were fixed in CO6.
- **The realistic body's sliders stop where deformation or the population says.** Since R3 each proportion's ends are found by a sweep (deformation checks and ANSUR II's 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles); some are nearer 1 than CO5's 0.85 to 1.15 (arms 0.95 to 1.08, feet 0.96 to 1.21), and the waist slider stops at 0.8 and 1.175, where its change steps in the trunk's outline (it moves the waist's breadth by 0.82 to 0.88 of its value since R3's wave 3; **Measured**: `docs/development/character-studio/refinement/r3/ranges.md`). One range serves both frames. The stylized body's hips narrow to 0.7 by a shape, as the realistic body's do, and its head scales 0.87 to 1.125 (its build shapes are kept apart with the head scaled); its waist slider is still the bone alone (about a quarter of its value, as the realistic one was before R3's wave 3); its elbow and knee keep 0.37 to 0.45 of their ring area at 135 degrees (no twist or helper bones), held where they are. The bone sliders' own steps (a pinched waist on the slight masculine body when the legs lengthen) set several ends. corrective shapes exist for the arm raised sideways and lowered, and since wave 4 for the hanging arm swung forward and back (the walk); the corrective rules are constants in `crates/anim/src/correctives.rs`, with no manifest field yet.
- **Twist and helper bones are found by name.** No manifest field describes the driver rule yet, so only a skeleton that uses the names in `crates/anim/src/twist.rs` is driven ([Animation](../Engine-Systems/Animation.md)).

## Editor

- Script completion and Go to Definition (F12) follow chains and declarations but doesn't type-check: a value it can't follow (a function's result, a table field, a module's return) offers nothing, luau-lsp ships in every package (a source build downloads it on request) and follows `require(game.ReplicatedStorage.X)` and `GetService` paths into modules (not `require(script.Parent.X)`, nor children's types such as `workspace.Door`); only open tabs are checked, and VS Code's set-up still checks each file alone.
- Panics while drawing the Play world aren't caught.
- **Group as Mesh** carries what a glTF material can hold. A Part's built-in Material texture (Wood, Brick, Concrete…, drawn from maps `scene` makes in memory) is grouped in its color; MaterialVariants, a Part's SurfaceAppearance, opacity and specular maps, DirectX normal maps and Decals aren't carried either (Output names each part). Hidden faces are only taken away when one solid part, or opposite faces in one plane, cover them; a face hidden by two overlapping parts together stays. Faces only partly pressed together stay whole (`docs/architecture/solid-modeling.md`).
- Model files aren't watched for changes while the editor runs (read again on open).
- Bakes (reflection probes, light probe volumes) go stale and must be rebaked by hand (flagged). A bake runs a little each frame with progress and Cancel, but each probe still reads its six faces back one at a time, so thousands of probes take minutes.

- **A SurfaceAppearance's normal map on a model whose own material has none is drawn without tangents** (every corner's tangent is `render::mesh::NO_TANGENT`, +X in the model), because `assets` works tangents out only for a material with a normal map of its own. Found by Group as Mesh's picture test on hello-mesh's ATM: the grouped model's material links the map itself, gets real tangents, and its bumps light differently (more correctly) than the separate MeshPart's.

## Content and platforms

- References may name an asset by path or by ID (PR #78); older projects still use paths.
- Built games ship cooked textures without their sources (G12); what the CPU reads of such a texture (terrain layers, color grading lookups, UI images) is decoded from BC7 or BC5, so it's close to the source, not equal. Model files (FBX, glTF) still ship beside their cooked copies.
- macOS is not verified on changes; mobile and web are not supported.
- No binary world format (S10k's 10 MB `world.json` loads in 0.19 s since C11).
