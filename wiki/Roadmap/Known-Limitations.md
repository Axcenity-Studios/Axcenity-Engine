# Known limitations

What the engine doesn't do, or does with a known flaw, today. Each entry names where it's documented. Performance issues are in [Known bottlenecks](../Performance/Known-Bottlenecks.md); structural problems in [Technical debt](Technical-Debt.md).

## Scripting

- No per-script **memory limit**; long C functions (`string.rep`, pathological patterns) aren't interrupted by the watchdog (`docs/scripting/sandbox.md`).
- `task.defer` runs at the next step (Roblox: later in the same frame); a RemoteEvent message with no handler is dropped (Roblox queues it); `ContextActionResult.Pass` and touch buttons not implemented (`docs/scripting/client-server.md`).
- Vector3 math allocates a userdata per result; `--!native` doesn't speed up engine calls.
- A breakpoint inside a C-called Lua function (a `table.sort` comparator), or in a script-made coroutine resumed from one, can't pause; a step goes on to the next line where it can. The same goes for a conditional breakpoint, a logpoint and Pause (Pause waits for the next script line that can pause, so a game whose scripts are all waiting pauses only when one wakes). Logpoints and conditions make their line yield on every pass, which slows a hot loop.

## Rendering

- Overlapping see-through items can blend in the wrong order (sorted by center).
- AMD/Vulkan/Windows: the sky clear isn't sRGB-encoded through an sRGB view (`docs/rendering/pipeline.md`).
- Lights are tiled in 2D, not clustered; rect lights use a representative point.
- Foliage is lit by where a point is in its item's box (a ball for the crown, its height for the foot), since glTF vertex colours aren't read: a plant can't carry its own baked occlusion, and a crown that isn't round is shaded as if it were. The ground round a tuft of grass that flares from a narrow foot is darkened only a little by screen-space occlusion (the tuft's own foot shade grounds it), and nothing lays dirt or a darker skirt where a plant or a prop meets the ground: that is content (ProjectedDecals) for now (2026-11-26, `docs/rendering/models.md`, `docs/rendering/shadows.md`).
- Water's waves calm at the shore by the depth the scene's copy shows (2026-11-26, `docs/rendering/water.md`): only from Medium reflection quality up (Low has no copy, and its waves keep their height over shallow banks), and a corner with something in front of it on the screen keeps its waves (its depth isn't known), as does one behind the camera; a corner off the screen reads the screen's nearest edge. Some fine parallel ripple lines still show on Desert Homestead's far river.
- Ray traced shadows, ambient occlusion and GI pass through cut-outs' holes only within 60 studs of the camera (2026-11-27, `docs/rendering/ray-tracing.md`): further off every hit on a leaf card counts, which darkens far canopies and the ground under them, with an edge where it starts; small plants cast traced shadows only to 55 studs.
- A plant whose ScatterRule has `CastShadow` off (2026-11-27, `docs/terrain.md`) casts nothing under ray traced shadows: contact shadows are skipped where rays trace, so only ambient occlusion grounds it. Without ray tracing contact shadows still do. Half-resolution traced shadows (High and below, `docs/rendering/ray-tracing.md`) fill three pixels of four from neighbours on the same plane, so a shadow's edge on thin casters (wires, grass blades) is softer than at Full.
- Screen-space reflections (2026-11-26, `docs/rendering/reflections.md`) see only what's on the screen, a frame late (they read last frame's picture: the first frame after a cut or a change of targets reflects nothing on screen, and a moving camera's newly uncovered spots can show what stood in front last frame), mirror along the surface's own normal (not its normal map), and blur rough reflections with five reads, not a color pyramid; leaves and grass (cut-outs) aren't marched. Where they miss, the sky's reflection is shaded by the sun's shadow maps along it (`sky_open_along`): a heuristic that also shades a way the sun's shadow crosses though the sky above it is open, and does nothing without sun shadows. Not yet a `UserGameSettings` property.
- No vertex colors or `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` from glTF; FBX uses the first UV set; glTF animations are read as bone takes, but their morph target weight channels aren't.
- Humanoid retargeting finds roles by bone names or by a rig definition of the project's (`assets/rigs/*.rig.json`); a retargeted take moves role bones only, so a bone without a role keeps its place (`docs/rendering/animation.md`).

## Physics

- MeshParts collide as convex hulls (hollows filled) unless already convex and small, their model has `_COL` collision pieces, or they're set to `PreciseConvexDecomposition` (R15.2). The Model Studio's Convex Pieces (R15.2b) runs on the UI thread and can take a few seconds on a big model.
- Setting `Position` stops a part (Roblox keeps its speed).
- Ragdolls can exceed joint limits briefly on hard landings.
- **A physics step stopped for good once, cause not found** (2026-11-25; `crates/physics/src/threads.rs`, `vendor/rapier3d/src/pipeline/physics_pipeline/mod.rs`). In one run of the whole `player` suite (the release build, its tests side by side) the test program did nothing for 14 minutes until it was killed. **Measured** from its stacks: one thread of physics' pool was inside a step, waiting in `join_deferred_bvh_optimize` for the broad phase's tree optimization that an earlier step had handed to the pool; the pool's other eleven threads were asleep with no work, and thirty test threads waited for their turn to step (`Threads::run`). Where the task went is not known: it was on no thread's stack. Steps taking turns (the fix for the first deadlock of this kind) doesn't prevent it. Seen once; the suite's other runs that day, one of them straight after with nothing changed, finished in 45 to 150 seconds. Nothing says a game can't meet it: a game steps one world on the same pool. The stacks were lost with the session's scratch folder; to look again, attach `gdb` to a run that stops (`thread apply all bt`).

## Networking

- **Game connections trust a typed address on first use** (MP4; a join link pins the fingerprint); a bare join code trusts the relay. Port mapping (MP5b) can't get past a provider's router (carrier-grade NAT; a relay can), and a killed host leaves its mapping until the 30-minute lease ends.
- No matchmaking or public relay (anyone can run `axcenity-relay`, MP5a), and prediction covers only characters (MP6a, under `Players.CharacterAuthority = "Server"`; physics bodies are MP6b; lag compensation and replays exist, Phase 81). Multiplayer has been measured with 16 bots across emulated home routers and 32 on one machine; a 16–32 client run on real machines (G13, MP7) is planned. All of it is planned in `docs/research/multiplayer-readiness.md` (MP1–MP7).
- **Server-moved characters** (MP6a): other players are drawn in the past on a device, so walking into a moving player costs a correction; moving platforms aren't rewound; seats, `MoveTo` and `MoveDirection` don't drive a remote player's character, and a device Script's `Jump` can be lost in a replay.
- Editor Play can't host or join a real network game.

## Characters

The Character Runtime (Character Studio overhaul CO3; [Characters](../Engine-Systems/Characters.md#applying-a-description-character-runtime), `docs/development/character-studio/architecture.md`). Since 2026-11-25 the engine ships one family, `axcenity/human` ([ADR-0050](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0050-The-Engine-Ships-One-Character-Family-MakeHumans-Body-With-Quaterniuss-Animations.md)); entries about the realistic and stylized bases, the generated wardrobe and the realistic test tee's failing record tests were removed with them (this page's history has them).

- **One character family: what went with the other two** (2026-11-25, ADR-0050). What is open:
  - [ ] **Engine features with no shipped asset.** Only the realistic base had them, so nothing the engine ships exercises them now and only their synthetic tests remain: corrective shapes and helper bones (`crates/anim/src/correctives.rs`, `twist.rs`'s helpers), makeup and marking layers (ADR-0042), the Skin model's normal maps (smooth and defined), eye and iris values, per-corner body regions and region hiding (`avatar::fit::pose_safe_hides`), a character's levels of detail, body features and handles, and `avatar::fit` end to end on a shipped body. A fault in one of them would be found only by a project that brings its own base.
  - [ ] **The fitting code's last known state is a regression nobody fixed.** After [ADR-0046](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0046-Non-Destructive-Proxy-Fitting-And-Biharmonic-Inpainting.md)'s rewrite the realistic test tee fitted worse in three poses (arms out: 2 bodies, 7.1 mm; arms raised: 6 bodies, 18.9 mm; walk: 35.7 mm) and six record tests failed. Those tests went with the base; the code is as it was. The human base's clothes are not fitted by it (they follow the body by their own copies of its shapes).
  - [ ] **Motions the library lacks are gone**: walking and running backward and sideways, waving, pointing, using, pulling, climbing a ladder, cheering, clapping, shrugging. A script that plays one of those states by name finds none.
  - [ ] **A program run away from its files has no family.** The default humanoid's model is built in (the default body in its boxer briefs, 4.75 MB, 30 face control shapes), but the base's package is not: without `assets/characters/` a character can't be given a description. Before, the stylized package was built in.
  - [ ] **An older project keeps what it has and is not brought up to date.** Its own copies of the stylized or realistic packages still resolve its characters, and Character Studio offers such a body only when the library it opens has it. A format 1 character file still migrates to `axcenity/stylized`; where that package is missing it opens as the default character with a notice, and saving then writes the default character over the old file.
  - [ ] **The repository is 96 MB larger in its working tree** (`assets/characters`: 213 MB with three families, 309 MB with one: the base's 31 MB model, 11 MB of skins, 51 garments and 10 hairstyles), and the installed program's library with it.
  - [ ] **Garments generated with Meshy fit nothing the engine ships.** They and the wardrobe pack published from them were made for the realistic base. The copies outside the repository (the wardrobe repository, Character Studio's own folder on a person's machine) are untouched; Character Studio's online wardrobe still downloads that pack and offers only items made for the body being edited, so on the human base it offers none of them.
- **Character Studio's interface.** The workspace edits format 2 descriptions on the library's body (`docs/editor/character-studio.md`): Body, Face, Hair, Clothing, Accessories and Expressions pages drawn from the manifest and catalogue, presets by kind (with a person's own and favourites), Randomize with scopes and locks, held versions, autosave with recovery, the asymmetry tools, and a pose and motion preview (Still, Idle, Walk at the take's own speed, Run, five of the library's gestures; the camera follows the head). The face controls move left and right together (no one-sided preview), the expression tiles have no pictures, and the neutral light is not in the bottom bar (no room at 1440 points). Not yet: outfit, hair and skin presets (none shipped) and a turntable. The round trip with the Game Editor is built (Edit in Character Studio, Done or Cancel); Cancel discards the studio's changes without asking. The Walk and Run previews play in place: the character doesn't travel, so a planted foot slides back along the floor as on a treadmill. The row that picks a family is drawn only when the library has more than one base, so it is never seen with the shipped library. An installed editor offers Character Studio only when its payload has `assets/characters/`. A pack added to a project puts its characters in `assets/characters/descriptions/`, but a game script can't yet load a description by path: it sets a `HumanoidDescription`'s `Data` to the file's text.
- **The Hair and Clothing browsers show drafts only.** Every shipped garment and hairstyle is a `draft`; a page with no finished item shows its drafts whether or not "Show drafts" is on. Highlights and roots are declared by a hair material slot's `role` (`secondary`, `accent`), and no shipped style has a second material, so none shows those controls. "Fits this body" knows only what an item's `limitations` say; nothing is measured. Who an item is made for (the Male, Female and All row, 2026-11-25) is a tag set by hand in `assets/characters/human/cut.py`, from each item seen on both bodies and MakeHuman's own tags, not from a measured fit: 14 masculine, 31 feminine, 16 unisex. The fisherman sweater is masculine because the female chest comes through it (two or three patches of skin, at any distance). The fit gate still measures a garment on both body types unless MakeHuman's own tag says `male` or `female`, so its records hold bodies the pages no longer offer the garment to. The 35 CC BY garments outside the repository are not in the table: converted again they take MakeHuman's tag, or are unisex. A tile's picture is the character being edited wearing the item, so a body change draws the page's tiles again on the picture thread, never during a drag; tiles show the old picture until then. All tiles are drawn (no virtual list): fine for tens of items, not thousands. A tile is previewed only once the pointer rests on it and its models are read on the loader's thread (2026-11-25; before, each tile crossed stalled the frame by 24 to 147 ms); what is left on the interface's thread at a first preview is making the body's copy with its hidden skin and uploading it with the garment (about 47 ms for a garment and 22 ms for a hairstyle in a debug build, measured with `timing_tests.rs` `browser_costs`; not measured in the running editor). The picture thread draws on the GPU the viewport uses: after a change to the character a page's tiles are drawn again there (about 14 ms each once their models are loaded), which a slow GPU would show as dropped frames.
- **Editing on the character covers the face only** (`docs/editor/character-studio.md`). The human base has ten face features with 24 handles; its body has no features or handles, so nothing on the body can be hovered, clicked or dragged. Face shape and Neck are lit whole and share their skin with no feature (`direct.rs` `WHOLE`, a feature lighting more than five times the usual skin; the forehead, at 3.7 times, is the nearest to that line), so under the chin the neck is lit where a click picks the chin, and Face shape can be picked on the skin only where no smaller feature is lit (the temples, the side of the jaw): it is picked from the navigation. Fixed 2026-11-25: the nose, the eyes and the cheeks were hardly lit or picked (1.2 cm², 2.6 cm² and nothing: they shared their skin with Face shape), and a click on an eyeball picked nothing. A handle's whole range takes 110 points of drag along its direction on the screen, whatever the zoom, as every numeric control's does (Paul's rule of 2026-11-18), so the skin doesn't stay under the pointer. The tint, its outline and the handles are drawn by egui over the preview, so what they may cover is found by a CPU depth buffer of the posed character (a texel per 2 points, rebuilt each frame something is selected or hovered; `direct/depth.rs`), not by the renderer's own depth: where the nose crosses a feature the tint stops a triangle short of the crossing, and a handle within about 6 points of skin that shows is drawn. Since ADR-0045 a feature's tint is drawn on the worn item over it and handles are hidden only by skin. The long-term route is the tint as draw data, depth-tested in `render`. Hovering casts a ray against the posed body's triangles (after a CPU skinning of the frame's pose) each frame the pointer or the camera moves; the frame costs measured for this (3.6 ms with a feature selected, 4.6 ms with the pointer moving, release build) are the retired realistic body's and were not measured again on the human one. The handles drive the slider's values in a straight line: a parameter whose skin moves on a curve (a tilt) is followed only near its middle. A handle's `travel` in the manifest is the measured surface travel to within 5 mm (the worst, the chin's, is 4.1 mm off; `player` `handle_tests`): until 2026-11-25 every one was half the contract, which the human base's own tests had not caught.
- **Edit mode shows a described character at rest** (`docs/editor/character-studio.md`). The Game Editor applies kept descriptions and poses described characters at rest with their identity, items and materials; nothing plays and no expression shows until Play. Accessories on sockets aren't placed in edit mode (sockets are placed only by Play's Animators); characters without a description still draw their unposed model.
- **A project's character library holds what its characters name** (ADR-0050; `project` `add_character_packages_for`). New Project, Insert > Character, Character Studio's Done and Add to Project copy a description's base (its 31 MB model, region map and 11 MB of skins), the items it wears and the underwear of both body types: about 64 MB for the default game character (by file sizes, not measured in a project). A script that wears an item no character of the project names finds it missing: add it through Character Studio. A project made before the library existed gets one the next time one of those is used; until then `Humanoid:ApplyDescription` fails with a message that says so. A project's `Character` prefab (its players) is never changed.
- **Some material values aren't drawn yet, and skin tone is one ratio per material.** Eye materials draw as Standard; skin, hair and cloth have their own shading models (`docs/rendering/skin.md`, `hair.md`, `cloth.md`). The pigments `melanin`, `undertone` and `redness` tone the whole albedo by one colour ratio from the references' indicative tone table (`crates/scene/src/skin_tone.rs`), so lips, nails, palms and soles tone with the rest, and the table's light end leans lavender-pink. Not drawn: `pupil_size`, `limbal_ring` and `iris_depth` (the Eye model).
- **Hair and cloth shading: limits** (CO7 wave 4; `docs/rendering/hair.md`, `cloth.md`). No shipped garment uses the Cloth model (MakeHuman's are Standard); the ten hairstyles, the brows and the lashes use Hair. Under TAA a hair card's hashed alpha leaves a little flicker (3.38 of 255 root mean square over a card whose opacity ramps clear to whole; the limit, 4.5, is provisional). Without TAA or MSAA hair cards are cut hard. No strands or deep opacity shadows; no wet hair; ray traced reflections and GI see hair and cloth as made. Cloth reads `KHR_materials_sheen` and `KHR_materials_anisotropy`'s factors, not their textures; no fuzz layer or thin-fabric transmission. Only hair cards cast through their holes in the shadow maps: every other cut-out (leaf cards, fences, grates) casts its whole card (`docs/rendering/shadows.md`). Hair lit from behind fades over the head's terminator only on a shell (single-sided hair, or a layer whose file sets `axcenity_hair_shell`). A hair material needs a normal map to get tangents: `assets` makes them only for materials with one. Of the Hair model's values the renderer draws `tint`, `melanin`, `redness` and `roughness_scale`.
- **Skin shading: limits** (`docs/rendering/skin.md`). There is no screen-space subsurface blur or transmission, so light doesn't bleed past shadow-casting geometry (ears and nostrils lit from behind stay opaque); the curvature that sets how far red spreads comes from screen-space derivatives, constant per triangle; the pores and fine lines the model draws are the same pattern on every character and fade out where a pore cell is under two pixels. Without TAA contact shadows keep their fixed pattern, so a limb in front of the body from the camera's view still casts a false screen-space contact shadow. The human base's skins have no normal maps, so the model's `definition` and `softness` change nothing on it.
- **Makeup and markings: limits** ([ADR-0042](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0042-Makeup-And-Markings-Are-Layers-Over-Library-Masks-Drawn-Live-By-The-Skin-Model.md)). The human base has no masks, so Character Studio shows no makeup on it. In the engine: nine fixed layers whose masks' channel layout is fixed (a new kind needs a fourth mask or the decal path; tattoos, birthmarks and placed marks are not built); freckles and moles are a fixed population chosen by amount and seed; ray-traced reflections and GI see the skin without makeup; a skin with a layer on loses its own emissive and opacity maps.
- **The starter animations** (2026-11-25, [ADR-0049](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0049-The-Starter-Animations-Are-Quaterniuss-Library-Baked-Onto-The-Humanoid-Skeleton.md), Proposed until Paul has watched them; ADR-0050; `assets/characters/human/mocap.py`, `animations.py`). The 84 takes of Quaternius's Universal Animation Library are baked onto the human base's own skeleton, and the default state machine has a state for each and no other. What is open:
  - [ ] **The takes were looked at as film strips, not at speed, and not by Paul.** On 2026-11-25 every state was drawn at ten moments over its length on the dressed male and female default characters, from the front and the side, beside the library's own mannequin at the same moments (`AXCENITY_CAPTURE_FILM`, `docs/development/character-studio/visual-qa.md`; the sheets are in `Quaternius 2026-11-25/Review/2026-11-25 motion review/` beside the sources), and every take was measured against the library frame by frame: each bone's turn between frames, the ends of the loops, the takes' lengths, the hips' correction and the feet's height. What that found is fixed (next two items). What it can't judge is left: timing and weight at real speed, the walk with the controller moving the body over ground, and what Paul thinks of them. What he watched in Character Studio that day was mostly not these takes (the item after next).
  - [x] **The collarbones were turned against the world, not the chest** (found by numbers, seen in the sword takes' spins and the crouch; fixed 2026-11-25, `mocap.SWUNG`, `CARRIER`). A collarbone takes only where the library's points, from where it points standing at ease; that was measured in the world's axes, so a body that leaned, lay down or turned round left its collarbones behind, twisted about their own length by as much as the chest had turned. Measured before: every one of the 84 takes had a collarbone more than 10 degrees past the library's own turn, 48 of them more than 30 (78 degrees crouching, 51 in the jog, 58 in the sprint, 22 talking, 180 in the roll), and in 12 takes a collarbone turned over 30 degrees more in one frame than the library's did (165 degrees in one frame of the sword combo, where the spin passed the point the shortest way round changes sides: a shoulder dropped for the rest of the spin). After: none is past the library's by 10 degrees, and no bone of any take turns more than 4 degrees further in a frame than its counterpart. The fit records measured in the jog and the idle followed (ADR-0049, decision 5): hidden skin showing fell on thirteen garments (the men's first casual suit 46.31 to 16.69 mm), skin through the cloth rose on seven women's tops (the camisole 4.92 to 12.61 mm), the idle's holes behind the men's armpit 44.6 to 51.7 mm.
  - [x] **Character Studio played an earlier build's animation file** (found and fixed 2026-11-25, `project::refresh_default_humanoid`). The studio's own folder got the default humanoid's files once and kept them, so on Paul's machine it still held the keyed animation file of 21 November under this build's clips: `IdleTalking`, `Yes` and `No` played nothing (the file has no such takes), and `Walk`, `Run`, `Dance`, `Interact` and Idle played the keyed motion made for the stylized skeleton, at the new takes' lengths. The workspace now writes this build's model, animation file, clips and state machine over whatever its folder holds.
  - [ ] **A project keeps the animation file it got.** `add_default_humanoid` never writes over a project's files, which is right for a creator's own. A project that took the default character from an earlier build therefore plays that build's takes, and Insert > Character then adds clips for states its file has no takes for (they play nothing). Deleting the project's `assets/models/Humanoid/HumanoidAnimations.glb` and `assets/animations/Humanoid/` and inserting a character again brings this build's. Nothing tells a creator.
  - [ ] **A state a script plays over the Humanoid's goes back to Idle, not to what the body is doing.** The Humanoid asks its Animator for a state only when its own changes, so `PickUp` played while walking ends in Idle while the body still moves, until it stops and starts again. The library's `SlideEnd` and `ClimbUp` end in a running pose for the same reason. Not changed: the fix is in the Humanoid, and a state machine of a creator's own may rely on it.
  - [ ] **The aim takes are offsets, played whole.** `PistolAimUp` and `PistolAimDown` lean the whole body back to aim at the sky and bend it to aim at the floor: the library made them to be mixed with the pistol stance by where a player aims, and the default state machine plays each as a state. `HighJumpFall` holds the body 55 cm above where it stands, as the library's does.
  - [ ] **Only the hips are corrected for the ground**: the lower foot is put at the library's height (and the take lifted where a hip, hand, knee, elbow, shoulder or the head would otherwise be under the floor), the other foot is where the turns leave it, and neither is held in place on the ground. A take lifted for a hand has its feet that much above the floor in those frames. On a character with other proportions (height, leg length) a planted foot can slide or sink a little.
  - [ ] **`Walk` and `Run` are the jog and the sprint sped up** (by 4% and 36%) to keep up with the controller's 16 and 32 studs a second; the library's walk (0.97 m a second) is `Stroll`, which no movement state plays. The sprint at 136% is fast in the legs, and `CrouchWalk` plays at 187% (the library's crouched walk covers 0.75 m a second, the controller's 1.4): two steps a second, a walking pace on bent legs.
  - [ ] **Takes made for a prop have none**: the sword, shield, pistol, torch, lantern, phone and farming takes hold empty hands; the rail takes lean on nothing. The seated takes sit 54 cm high and a Seat puts the feet on its top.
  - [ ] **The animation file is 5.3 MB**, embedded in the editor and copied into each project that adds the default character. Rebuilding it needs the two packs from quaternius.com and Blender.
- **The human base** (2026-11-24 and 25, [ADR-0047](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0047-The-Realistic-Family-Is-Rebuilt-On-MakeHumans-Body-And-Wardrobe.md), [ADR-0048](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0048-A-Base-Models-Material-Draws-A-Library-Colour-Map-Chosen-By-The-Characters-Parameters.md), ADR-0050; `assets/characters/human/`). `axcenity/human` (MakeHuman's body) has body type with a frame slider between Male and Female, muscle, fat and age, the proportion sliders with hand and foot size, 149 face sliders with hand-written names in three levels, ten face features with 24 handles, 30 presets, 30 expression shapes, MakeHuman's 22 skins on the Skin shading model with tone controls and a fade between ages, brows and lashes on the Hair model, ten hairstyles with colour variants, underwear cut from the body, and 51 garments: every CC0 item of the converted MakeHuman library (35 CC BY garments stay outside the repository). It has no normal maps or makeup on the skin, levels of detail, correctives and helper bones, or body features and handles. Every garment and hairstyle is a `draft`. What is open, as measured or seen in captures:
  - [ ] **13 of the 48 standard facial controls play nothing** (`face.UNMAPPED`): MakeHuman has no expression unit for the brows drawn together (`Brow_Inward_*`), the mouth sideways (`Mouth_Left`, `Mouth_Right`), the jaw forward or sideways (`Jaw_Forward`, `Jaw_Left`, `Jaw_Right`), cheek raise and puff (`Cheek_Raise_*`, `Cheek_Puff`) or the tongue (`Tongue_Out`, `Tongue_Up`, `Tongue_Down`).
  - [ ] **The face's names, handles and presets were made without Paul looking at them in Character Studio.** The names are written from what each MakeHuman target was measured to move (`face.NAMES`), not from use; a few are still guesses at what the target is for ("Lower face slope", "Septum angle", "Bridge length"). The handles sit on the corner their shape moves most and their directions come from the shapes; every one of the 24 shows in the Face view or with the camera turned an eighth round, and only the nose's was dragged, by a test, none by hand. Every slider's name fits the panel uncut (22 characters), at the cost of a few terse ones ("Left inner lid gap", "Nose set, depth"). The presets' values were set by eye from captures. Two face presets change little of the picture: the narrow face is no different in profile (2.3% of the face's pixels) and the weathered one is lines and lids more than shape (2.5% in each view; `player` `face_preset_tests`).
  - [ ] **The nose bridge's handle doesn't show from the front** (`nose_bridge`, driving `nose_hump`): the hump moves toward the camera, so there is nothing to drag in the Face or Front view; it shows with the camera turned to three quarters or the side. The slider works in any view.
  - [ ] **Skin tone lightening goes grey on the dark skins**: `melanin` below 0 on the African skins gives a cool grey-brown (the tone curve was made for another albedo). Darker, warmer and redder work on every skin. A lighter skin is the `skin` choice.
  - [ ] **The fade between ages is a mix of two photographs**, so halfway between two ages wrinkles are at half strength rather than half as many. It is drawn by the Skin model alone (in the occlusion map's place: a skin with an occlusion map would snap), ray traced reflections see the nearer skin only, and foundation makeup would take its tone from the nearer one.
  - [ ] **Hair is painted on the scalp of every skin**: ginger stubble on the Caucasian skins, a black cap on the African ones, black slicked hair on the bodysuit pair. A hairstyle covers it; a bald character shows it.

- **A character's parts pick their levels of detail one by one.** A small part switches to a simpler copy nearer the camera than the body does, and a simplified garment doesn't keep its clearance from the skin. Fixed 2026-11-25 (Paul: "strange visual holes or artifacts that appear at a distance but disappear when moving closer"): the human base's files have no simpler copies, but the model loader made them for every opaque primitive of 256 triangles or more (half, then a quarter of its triangles, the surface free to move 2% and 5% of its size: `crates/assets/src/model/lod.rs`), from the rest shape alone. Below 30% of the picture's height a garment was drawn from one, cut through the skin under it, and where that skin was hidden left holes; the hair lost patches the same way. A primitive with blend shapes is no longer simplified when loaded (the body, every garment and hairstyle; a file's own `_LOD1` and `_LOD2` copies are still used), so the human base is drawn at full detail at every distance: 50 of them are 50 full bodies. A custom base's garment without blend shapes is still simplified apart from its body. Making character copies that keep their clearance and switch together is CO18's "character LOD sets switched together".
- **Compute posing (CO8) has a ceiling and a few gaps** ([ADR-0041](../Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0041-Shared-Base-Mesh-With-Live-Identity-Morphs-Baking-Is-Optional.md), `docs/rendering/animation.md`). Each animated character is morphed (only shapes with a weight) and skinned once a frame, and every pass and ray tracing read that. Its measurements (50 characters of distinct live descriptions in 1.5 ms of GPU on an RX 9070 XT) were made with the retired realistic body and not again with the human one, which has more shapes (339) and no simpler copies. Left: the posing arena (every posed mesh's corners, joints and weights, and the moves of each shape that has been active) must fit one storage binding (128 MB by default), and items past it are drawn at rest with a warning; the arena and the CPU copy each animated mesh keeps cost memory the per-pass path didn't; GI traced in software (`ray_soft.rs`, GPUs without ray queries) still leaves skinned items out.
- **Identities registered in a run are kept for the run** (audit D4). Each distinct identity the applier registers (`Assets::register_identity`) stays in `Assets` until the program ends: about 8 to 10 KB each on the retired realistic body (estimated; the human base names more shapes), and one more per part with item parameters. A slider drag in Character Studio registers one every frame, so a long editing session can reach hundreds of MB. A prune of keys no MeshPart names, run when a drag ends, is the planned fix. `anim`'s cache of how each model wears each identity is bounded (1,024 entries). A baked character's derived model is still made again in every run and whenever its base model is reloaded, never cooked.
- **Correctives, twist and helper bones are found by name.** No manifest field describes the driver rule, so only a skeleton that uses the names in `crates/anim/src/twist.rs` is driven, and the corrective rules are constants in `crates/anim/src/correctives.rs` ([Animation](../Engine-Systems/Animation.md)). The human base's eight twist bones are driven; it has no correctives or helpers.
- **Character Studio's tests fill the temporary folder** (found 2026-11-25). Each of the editor's Character Studio tests copies the whole shipped library (300 MB) into the system's temporary folder and removes it when it passes; a failing test leaves its copy. On a machine whose `/tmp` is memory with a quota, a run with many failures stops later runs with "Disk quota exceeded" until the dead `axcenity-*-<pid>` folders are removed by hand. The tests should take what they wear (`add_character_packages_for`) or share one read-only copy.

## Editor

- Script completion and Go to Definition (F12) follow chains and declarations but doesn't type-check: a value it can't follow (a function's result, a table field, a module's return) offers nothing, luau-lsp ships in every package (a source build downloads it on request) and follows `require(game.ReplicatedStorage.X)` and `GetService` paths into modules (not `require(script.Parent.X)`, nor children's types such as `workspace.Door`); only open tabs are checked, and VS Code's set-up still checks each file alone.
- Panics while drawing the Play world aren't caught.
- **Group as Mesh** carries what a glTF material can hold. A Part's built-in Material texture (Wood, Brick, Concrete…, drawn from maps `scene` makes in memory) is grouped in its color; MaterialVariants, a Part's SurfaceAppearance, opacity and specular maps, DirectX normal maps and Decals aren't carried either (Output names each part). Hidden faces are only taken away when one solid part, or opposite faces in one plane, cover them; a face hidden by two overlapping parts together stays. Faces only partly pressed together stay whole (`docs/architecture/solid-modeling.md`).
- Model files aren't watched for changes while the editor runs (read again on open).
- **An untouched editor does not slow down for moving scenery** (found 2026-11-26; `docs/editor/shell.md`, "Steady frames"). `viewport::shader_time` means to draw swaying plants and drifting clouds 30 times a second while nobody touches the editor (`SCENERY_FPS`); egui takes a sixtieth of a second off the wait, so the frames come 55 to 60 a second (or as fast as the scene draws), and active particles ask for every frame. The GPU is not spared as the code intends. Whether to make the pace real is the owner's call: at 30 a second the sway is visibly coarser.
- **Scatter masks, ground clutter and the decal brush** (2026-11-23, `docs/editor/scatter.md`): masks act under the brush only (no fill of an area, no preview of a mask in the viewport), and moisture is the terrain's shape (hollows wet, rises dry), not water or rainfall. Ground clutter is painted foliage, so it counts toward a terrain's 32,768 instances (the Ground Clutter palette at its own density fills about a 190-stud square), and painted built-in props, its Rock included, have no cull distance (`scene::foliage`: only plants and models do), so a lot of gravel is drawn however far away it is: keep it to the places the camera gets close to. Scattered decals are ProjectedDecals: none is drawn at Low quality, the nearest 128 within 150 studs at Medium, 512 within 300 at High and 1,024 within 600 at Ultra, so they dress a place rather than cover a map; the brush's limit is a rule of thumb counted from each decal, not from the camera. The decal brush needs a Terrain to follow. A TerrainPath's shoulder is terrain paint, one weight a sample, so its ragged edge is no finer than the terrain's samples.
- **Render Movie** (`docs/editor/movie.md`): movies have no sound; videos need an `ffmpeg` on the machine (a PNG sequence doesn't); level sections aren't loaded; a hardware H.264 encoder that `ffmpeg` lists can still fail when the movie starts. A camera cut's **Dissolve** is drawn only in rendered movies: the game and the Sequencer's preview play it as a cut, because it draws the world twice a frame. In the game the cutscene overlay's subtitle is a fixed 28 pixels, small on a 4K screen (a movie scales it by its height).
- **Environment presets** (`docs/project/sequences.md`): a preset holds numbers, colors, vectors, switches and the `Precipitation` choice, not text (a `SkyImage`). Nothing is made at run time, so a preset's clouds, weather and haze need Clouds, a Weather and an Atmosphere in Lighting (the editor's apply adds the first two). The weather drawn still follows the Weather's `TransitionTime`, so a blend quicker than it lags unless it's 0. `Lighting:TransitionToPreset` fires no event when it starts or arrives and changes the device it's called on (call it from a Script). Preset files aren't Assets panel tiles and have no rename or delete in the editor. Asset Check doesn't check the presets a sequence names.
- Bakes (reflection probes, light probe volumes) go stale and must be rebaked by hand (flagged). A bake runs a little each frame with progress and Cancel, but each probe still reads its six faces back one at a time, so thousands of probes take minutes.

- **A SurfaceAppearance's normal map on a model whose own material has none is drawn without tangents** (every corner's tangent is `render::mesh::NO_TANGENT`, +X in the model), because `assets` works tangents out only for a material with a normal map of its own. Found by Group as Mesh's picture test on the `mesh` sample's ATM: the grouped model's material links the map itself, gets real tangents, and its bumps light differently (more correctly) than the separate MeshPart's.

## Content and platforms

- References may name an asset by path or by ID (PR #78); older projects still use paths.
- Built games ship cooked textures without their sources (G12); what the CPU reads of such a texture (terrain layers, color grading lookups, UI images) is decoded from BC7 or BC5, so it's close to the source, not equal. Model files (FBX, glTF) still ship beside their cooked copies.
- macOS is not verified on changes; mobile and web are not supported.
- No binary world format (S10k's 10 MB `world.json` loads in 0.19 s since C11).
