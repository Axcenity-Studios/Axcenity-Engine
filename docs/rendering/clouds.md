# Clouds

A layer of cloud high in the sky, lit by the sun, whose shadows fall on the world (graphics Phase 26). `DrawList::clouds` (`render::Clouds`, `render/src/draw_list.rs`) comes from the world (`scene/src/clouds.rs`); the renderer packs it into the frame (`renderer/clouds.rs`, `CloudsRaw`, `Clouds` in `clouds.wgsl`, compiled after `fog.wgsl` into every shader that includes `lighting.wgsl`, followed by `cloud_fill.wgsl`). The sky's clouds are traced in passes of their own before the scene (`renderer/cloud_trace.rs`, `cloud_trace.wgsl`; [below](#the-traced-clouds)).

## The world's clouds

A `Clouds` inside Lighting (the Lighting ribbon's **Clouds**, beside Atmosphere, or the Explorer's Insert menu; it always goes into Lighting, like an Atmosphere). The first one is drawn. Without one there are none.

- `Cover` (0 to 1, default 0.5): how much of the sky is cloud. 0 is a clear sky, drawn exactly as without clouds; 1 is overcast.
- `Density` (0 to 1, default 0.7): how much light the cloud takes away (0.05 per stud at 1, `EXTINCTION`), so thicker clouds are whiter on top, darker below, and cast deeper shadows. 0 is none.
- `Color` (default white): the clouds' own color, lit by the sun and the sky.
- `Speed` (studs a second, default 20; 0 holds them still, below 0 counts as 0) and `Direction` (degrees from +X, east, toward +Z, south; default 0): how they drift on their own, so they move out of the box. The Workspace's `GlobalWind` (studs a second; its level part) adds to that drift, and their shadows move with them. The renderer keeps how far they've gone (`CloudDrift`), moving them on by the wind times the time since the last frame (`DrawList::time`), so a new wind changes their speed, not where they are; when the time counts back (a host starting again) they stay put. Each renderer has its own drift (a probe bake starts from none). The editor keeps drawing frames while clouds are in the wind (`viewport::shader_time`).

An environment preset sets `Cover`, `Density`, `Color` and `Speed` with the sky and the weather, and a sequence's Environment track blends them ([../project/sequences.md](../project/sequences.md#environment-presets-and-the-environment-track)).

## How they're drawn

The layer lies between `Clouds::BOTTOM` (800) and `Clouds::TOP` (1200) studs up. Above each level point, five octaves of value noise (shapes about 1600 studs across, `CLOUD_SCALE`), cut at a level set by `Cover`, say how thick the cloud is (`cloud_cover`, 0 to 1). The shapes are made to look torn by the air, not round (before 2026-11-23 they were smooth lens-shaped puffs):

- They're drawn out one way, half again as long as wide (`CLOUD_STRETCH`), and pulled about by a slower noise before they're cut (`CLOUD_PULL`), so no edge runs straight or round.
- The three finest octaves are ridged (`1 − |2n − 1|`), which gives strands and tufts along the edges.
- The cut is soft and wide (`CLOUD_EDGE`, 0.42 of the noise's range) and eased, so a cloud is mostly thin veil round a thicker middle.
- `Cover` is about the share of the sky that's cloud: 0.3 is scattered wisps, 0.5 to 0.6 broken cloud, from 0.85 an overcast, and at 1 there are no holes (its thin places are thin cloud, lighter from below).

A cloud `cover` thick lies about a third of the way up the layer and grows both ways until it fills it (`cloud_span`), thickest through its middle; before, it filled the layer from the bottom up with a rounded top. With fine detail (High and Ultra), two octaves of 3D noise drawn out the same way eat the thin parts away to wisps and leave the thick middle whole.

- **In the sky** (`cloud_layer`, which gives the light the clouds add and the share of the sky behind them that shows; `clouded_sky` is the sky with it laid over; from the traced clouds' passes, or from `fs_sky` where they aren't traced): the sky behind is dimmed by the cloud in front of it and the cloud's light is added, step by step through the layer (integrated exactly over each step), each step lit by the sun through a dual-lobe phase (a silver lining toward the sun) and by the sky's colors. Deep in cloud, where none of the sun's light arrives straight, a share still arrives scattered many times from every side (`cloud_glow`: one part in 1 + 0.075 × the cloud's depth, the two-stream answer for droplets, times `CLOUD_GLOW`), so the underside of an overcast or storm sky is grey, lighter where the cloud is thinner, and not the dimmed blue of the sky behind it. Where the noise stands well above the cut the cloud more than fills its layer (`cloud_fill` above 1; `cloud_cover` is `cloud_thick` of it), and its underside shows less of that scattered light and of the sky's, down to half where it's heaviest (`cloud_belly`, `CLOUD_BELLY`): every part of an overcast is too deep to see through, so without it the sky was one flat grey from horizon to horizon. Broken and fair-weather cloud never fills that far and is drawn as before. Far clouds fade into the sky from 5000 to 20000 studs (`CLOUD_FAR`), and a ray is marched through at most 4000 studs of the layer (`CLOUD_MARCH`). The fog then fades the clouded sky like the sky. Clouds are part of the sky: they're drawn behind everything, so from above them the ground isn't hidden, and they aren't in the environment maps' sky.
- **Shadows** (`cloud_shadow`, in `light_surface`, so on parts, models and terrain, and in the volumetric fog's sun): the sun's light at a point is dimmed by the cloud where its path crosses a quarter of the way up the layer, its thickness there over the sun's height. A share of the light gets through even thick cloud (`cloud_light_through`: light scattered many times inside), so the ground under a cloud is dim, not black. Every cloud quality casts the same shadows.

- **The sky's light under them** (`under_clouds` in `lighting.wgsl`, inside `sky_diffuse` and `sky_specular`, so on every surface, in ray traced GI's and reflections' misses, in the fog's own light and the Path Traced view): the environment maps and the sky's harmonics hold the clear sky, since clouds aren't baked into them, so until 2026-11-23 an overcast world was still lit, and its puddles still mirrored, a clear blue sky the clouds hid. From broken cloud on (`OVERCAST_FROM`: none at a `Cover` of 0.4, all of it from 0.9) the sky's light turns the grey of its own brightness, tinted half-way to the clouds' `Color`, and a thick layer keeps 0.8 of it (`OVERCAST_LIGHT`). How thick is `1 − exp(−¼ × Density's extinction × the layer's depth)`, so a veil of little `Density` changes nothing however much sky it covers. It's by the world's `Cover`, not by the cloud over each point: the sky's light comes from all of it. Fair-weather cloud (under 0.4) draws exactly as before. Still not there: the clouds' own shapes in reflections.

The **cloud quality** graphics setting (`CloudQuality`, [settings.md](settings.md); `UserGameSettings.CloudQuality`) sets the sky's cost: Low a flat sheet a quarter of the way up (one look-up per pixel), Medium 16 steps lit by the cloud above each step, High 32 steps with fine 3D detail eroding the edges and 3 steps toward the sun, Ultra 64 and 6. Steps start at a per-pixel offset (`pixel_noise`) so they don't band.

## The traced clouds

`clouded_sky` in every pixel of the sky was the largest single cost of an outdoor frame: 1.3 to 2.6 ms of GPU time at 1920 × 1080 on Ultra in Desert Homestead, against 0.3 ms for the volumetric clouds of Unity's HDRP in the same views ([../benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md#against-unity-hdrp-desert-homestead)). Since 2026-11-26 the sky's clouds are worked out before the scene pass (`renderer/cloud_trace.rs`, `cloud_trace.wgsl`, the `Clouds` GPU pass), with the same steps through the layer and toward the sun at every quality:

- **Shapes from a texture.** `cloud_sum`, the five octaves of noise `cloud_fill` is cut from, depends only on the place. It was worked out at every step and at each step toward the sun (up to seven times a step). It is now kept in a texture: 4096² of `R16Float` (32 MB) over 52,428.8 studs (`CLOUD_FILL_SPAN`, 12.8 studs a texel) round the camera, carried with the clouds by the wind. It covers the furthest a ray is marched (`CLOUD_FAR` + `CLOUD_MARCH`, 24,000 studs) and its steps toward the sun. It follows the camera in whole texels, so drawing it again changes no sample, and it is drawn again only when the camera is 1,200 studs from its middle (0.47 ms of GPU time when it is; in still air at the default drift, once a minute). At 2048² thin veils came out visibly different from the noise's; at 4096² they don't.
- **Half size first** (`fs_trace`). The light the clouds add and the sky they hide are worked out for every other pixel each way, and only for texels whose surrounding pixels show sky (the prepass's depth). The sky behind, the sun's disc and every silhouette in front stay at full size: the sky pass still draws only the pixels its depth test leaves and multiplies its own sky in.
- **Then each pixel** (`fs_resolve`, at the frame's size). A pixel that shows sky reads between the four half-size texels round it where they agree (within `CLOUD_REFINE`, about three of 255 levels). Where they don't (a cloud's edge, or toward the horizon, where each step through the layer is long) it is worked out for that pixel exactly as the sky pass did, from the same per-pixel offset. Reading between texels everywhere made a busy horizon up to 10 levels brighter than a picture drawn at four times the size and brought down; with this step the traced clouds are as close to that picture as the sky pass's own.
- `fs_sky` then reads its pixel's clouds from the resolved texture. A pixel left out there (one the prepass covered and the scene pass didn't) is worked out in the sky pass as before.

Nothing is kept from one frame to the next, so there is nothing to trail behind a turning camera or a cut. Only while the prepass is drawn (ambient occlusion, contact shadows, volumetric fog, TAA and others keep it; its depth is what says where sky is) and the cloud quality steps through the layer (Medium and up). Otherwise, and with `Renderer::set_trace_clouds(false)` or `AXCENITY_NO_CLOUD_TRACE` set when the renderer is made (for comparing the two), the sky pass draws them itself.

Measured in Desert Homestead, Ultra, 1920 × 1080, RX 9070 XT (`benchmarks/cross-engine/homestead/results.md`): the `Clouds` pass takes 0.14 to 0.30 ms and the scene pass loses 1.1 to 2.7 ms. Against the sky pass's own clouds the stills differ by 0.01 to 0.14 of 255 levels on average, and at most 0.04% of pixels by more than 8.

Lit surfaces' cloud shadows and the fog's (`cloud_shadow`) still work `cloud_fill` out from the noise in every lit pixel (about 0.25 ms of the scene pass in Homestead's hero view). The item shader has no texture binding left for the shapes' texture.

## Cost

Without clouds: nothing. Every lit pixel skips them on one uniform test (`Clouds.color.w`), and no pass, target or texture exists. With them, traced: two passes before the scene, the shapes' texture (32 MB), a half-size and a frame-size `Rgba16Float` target (20 MB at 1920 × 1080), all let go when the clouds go. Not traced: they're worked out in the sky pass's pixels. The sky pass is drawn after solid items and before see-through ones, with a depth test that passes only where no solid item was drawn, so the sky (and its clouds) is worked out only for the pixels that show it.

## Tests

- `render/src/renderer/tests/clouds.rs` (GPU): the clouds overhead and their shadows on a floor move by the wind's distance (the middle row's best-matching shift is the wind's 100 studs in pixels), and stay still without wind; Cover 0 draws exactly the clear frame, traced clouds add their three textures' bytes and no draw calls, the sky pass's own add nothing, and taking them out restores the frame and the bytes; every cloud quality draws clouds and shades the ground the same.
- `render/src/renderer/tests/clouds.rs` (GPU), `an_overcast_turns_the_skys_light_grey`: a floor lit by a blue sky alone stays as blue under fair-weather cloud and under a veil of no density, and turns grey and a little dimmer under a thick overcast.
- `render/src/renderer/tests/clouds.rs` (GPU), the traced clouds against the sky pass's own: the same picture at Medium, High and Ultra, overhead and toward the horizon behind posts with a low sun (under 0.4 of a level apart on average, under 0.6% of pixels more than 8 apart); as bright square by square; unchanged when the shapes' texture is kept from 1,000 studs away or drawn again from 3,000; and under TAA changing no more from frame to frame. `AXCENITY_CLOUD_PICTURES=<folder>` writes the two pictures and one at four times the size.
- `render/src/renderer/tests.rs`, `stats_time_each_pass_the_frame_draws`: the `Clouds` pass is timed while clouds are traced and not for a flat sheet.
- `render/src/renderer/clouds.rs`: none by default or without density, each quality's steps and detail, the drift following the wind (level part only), keeping its place when the wind changes or time counts back.
- `scene/src/clouds.rs`: a new world has none; Lighting's Clouds take Cover (held to 0 to 1), Density and Color, drift east at 20 studs a second by default, turn with Direction and scale with Speed (not below 0), add the GlobalWind, and Cover 0 or removing it is none.
- `player/src/clouds_tests.rs` (GPU): a world's Clouds shade part of the floor, its GlobalWind moves the shadows, still air at Speed 0 keeps them, their own Speed moves them, and destroying the Clouds gives the frame without them.
- `render-types/src/graphics.rs`: each preset's cloud quality.
