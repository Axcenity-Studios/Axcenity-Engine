# Weather

Rain and snow, wet and snow-covered surfaces, sky-visibility occlusion, manual interior/exterior masking, weather volumes, and what's pressed into the snow (footprints, ruts, the hollows things lie in; graphics Phase 27 + Section 4). One state holds it all: Lighting's `Weather`. `scene/src/weather.rs` reads it, eases toward it, updates a 64×64 world-space `WeatherField` around the camera and the fine print map in it ([Prints in the snow](#prints-in-the-snow)), and `render::Weather` (`DrawList::weather`) + `DrawList::weather_field` drive the shaders (`Frame::weather`, `Frame::weather_bounds`, `Frame::precipitation`, `Frame::weather_prints`, `@binding(19) weather_field`, `weathered` in `lighting.wgsl`, and the snow's depth in `terrain.wgsl`).

`render::Weather` holds how wet surfaces are (`wetness`), how much snow lies (`snow`, `snow_thickness`), and what falls now: `rain` and `snowfall` (0 to 1, `Frame::precipitation`). Surfaces stay wet after the rain stops; the rings drops leave on the water don't.

## The world's weather

A `Weather` inside Lighting (the Lighting ribbon's **Weather**, the Explorer's Insert menu, or the Properties panel's **Weather & Surface Detection** section; it always goes into Lighting). The first one is used. Without one, surfaces are dry and nothing falls.

- `Enabled` (bool, default `true`): quick toggle for the weather system. When `false`, precipitation stops and surfaces dry and clear over `TransitionTime`.
- `Precipitation` (`Enum.Precipitation`: None, Rain or Snow; default Rain): what falls around the camera.
- `Intensity` (0 to 1, default 0.5): how hard it falls. Rain wets surfaces at least this much, and falling snow covers them at least this much.
- `Wetness` and `SnowCover` (0 to 1, default 0): how wet surfaces are and how much snow lies on them, whatever is falling, such as puddles after the rain or old snow.
- `SnowThickness` (0 to 2 studs, default 0.35): how deep the snow lies. Snow on terrain rises that far (in drifts), burying the foot of what stands on the ground; it also sets how far the snow's normal is smoothed and how deep prints go (a share of it).
- `Lightning` (0 to 1, default 0): how often lightning flashes: the chance of a flash every 2.6 seconds. See [Lightning](#lightning).
- `TransitionTime` (seconds, default 10): how long a change takes to ease in. Rain starting and stopping, surfaces wetting and drying, and snow settling and melting each move at most one whole change per `TransitionTime`. 0 changes at once.
- `OcclusionEnabled` (bool, default `true`): enables precipitation collision occlusion and automatic top-down sky-visibility surface detection.
- `FootprintsEnabled` (bool, default `true`) and `FootprintFadeTime` (seconds, default 30, at least 0.5): what touches the snow presses its own shape into it, as deep as it has sunk, and a print fills in by one whole depth of the snow each `FootprintFadeTime` (two and a half times as fast in snowfall at `Intensity` 1). See [Prints in the snow](#prints-in-the-snow).
- `SurfaceMask` (string, default `""`): manual interior/exterior cell overrides painted in the editor (`"x,z:I;x,z:E"`).
- The Workspace's `GlobalWind` blows the rain and snow, drives lying snow onto what faces it and runs spindrift along the ground.

**Environment presets** set the weather together with the sky, the clouds and the wind under one name (Clear, Storm, SnowDay and the project's own): a sequence's Environment track blends from one to the next, `Lighting:TransitionToPreset(name, seconds)` eases to one from a script, and the Properties panel applies one or saves the world's look as one. They set this Weather's `Precipitation`, `Intensity`, `Wetness`, `SnowCover` and `SnowThickness` and never its `TransitionTime`, which still limits how fast the weather drawn follows: [../project/sequences.md](../project/sequences.md#environment-presets-and-the-environment-track).

## Easing & Surface Detection

`settled` is the weather once eased in. `build_draw_list` puts it into the list, so a world drawn on its own (a still, a test, a probe bake) shows it settled. The host's `Particles` (player and editor viewport) owns a `WeatherState`. `Particles::update_against` eases it toward the world's weather, incrementally casts up to 512 top-down sky-visibility rays per frame via `Surfaces` (`physics::surface_hits`) across a 64×64 stud grid around the camera, presses what touches the snow into the print map (`weather/prints.rs`), and builds `WeatherField`. `Particles::add_weather` puts the eased weather, `WeatherField`, and occluded rain/snow sprites into the draw list.

## Surfaces & Exterior Masking

`light_surface` (and `toon_surface`, `cloth_surface`, `skin_surface`) runs `weathered` first, so parts, models, surface shaders and terrain all get it. See-through items get it too. Emission isn't changed. Leaves take it by their plant's shape (`weathered_lying`, below).

- **Exterior Masking**: `weather_sample` combines `Interior` `GraphicsVolume` boxes (`FogEffect.Interior == true`), automatic sky-visibility roof heights (`weather_field.x`), and manual `SurfaceMask` overrides (`weather_field.y`: `-1` Interior, `0` Auto, `+1` Exterior) into one exposure, from 0 (sheltered) to 1 (open sky), which is the share of the world's wetness and of its snow a surface has. Surfaces under roofs, inside `Interior` volumes, or painted Interior stay dry and bare. A `WeatherVolume` then sets the two shares in its box by hand, in place of all three ([Weather volumes](#weather-volumes)). The roof heights come from top-down rays against the world's solid surfaces (`physics::surface_hits`), so a MeshPart shelters by how it collides: as its convex hull unless its model has collision pieces (`_COL` objects, [models.md](models.md)). A house whose hull covers its terrace keeps the terrace dry; give it pieces for its roof, walls and floor.
- **Wet** (`weather_wet`): a porous surface's color is darkened toward 0.45 of itself (`WET_DARKENING`: water fills its pores) and deepened (`WET_SATURATION`); a smooth, sealed one (paint, glass: roughness under about 0.25) keeps `WET_SEALED` (0.4) of that change, and a metal none. A film of water lowers the roughness (`weather_film`) to 0.45 of its own (`WET_FILM`), but for being wet never under 0.2 (`WET_FILM_ROUGHNESS`), so the sun's reflection is sharper. A coarse surface (roughness from 0.6, whole by 0.95) keeps 0.68 of its own instead (`WET_FILM_COARSE`: the water stands between its grains), and the ground (the terrain) is never smoother than 0.38 for a film (`WET_FILM_GROUND`): packed earth is smooth enough dry for the film to make a mirror of it, and the sky it mirrors comes from the sky's image, which nothing stands in front of, so a wet yard shone pale as ice up to the foot of the house. The film's shine fades out from 250 to 900 studs from the camera (`WET_SHEEN_REACH`), where the surface is as rough as dry and only darker: shining, every ridge and gully of a far mountain range drew as a bright line at dusk. Undersides stay drier (by the surface's own normal), and water runs down walls in streaks (noise stretched along the height: between streaks a wall is half as wet, `WET_WALL_DRY`). Rain is blown in under an eave (`weather_blown`): a wall is as wet as the open ground 3 studs out from it, times 0.85 (`WET_BLOWN`), where what's straight above would have kept it dry; a porch deeper than that keeps its wall dry, and so does a room, since out from an inside wall is still inside. Before, every wall under an overhang stayed bone dry in a storm.
- **Puddles**: water stands only on level ground (the surface's own normal within about 20 degrees of up), in patches a few studs across (`puddle_at`: two octaves of the clouds' value noise over the world's x and z, so they stay put). The first appear at `Wetness` 0.35 (`PUDDLE_START`) and they spread to about a third of the level ground at 1 (`PUDDLE_COVER`, 0.4 of the noise; at 0.55 a soaked yard was one sheet of water with islands in it). A puddle is as smooth as water (roughness 0.08, `WET_ROUGHNESS`) and level whatever the normal map under it says, so it mirrors the sky. They thin out from 90 studs from the camera and are gone by 260 (`PUDDLE_REACH`), where each is smaller than a pixel. Until 2026-11-23 every wet surface was as smooth as a puddle: walls, hillsides and bumpy ground mirrored the sky at glancing angles and came out paler wet than dry, and distant mountains looked veined with snow.
- **Rain's rings** (`weather_dimples`, `rain_dimples`): while rain falls (`Frame::precipitation.x`), drops ring the water on surfaces facing up. The ground is cut into three sheets of squares (1.1, 0.7 and 0.45 studs across, each with its own beat of 1.7 to 3.1 drops a second); in each square a drop lands somewhere new each beat, as often as the rain's intensity says, and its ring spreads to 0.3 of the square and dies away, tipping the water's normal as the slope of a ridge (at most 0.55, `DIMPLE_SLOPE`). Whole in a puddle, 0.3 of that in a film of water (`DIMPLE_FILM`); none on slopes, and they fade out from 28 to 70 studs from the camera (`DIMPLE_REACH`). They move with `DrawList::time`. Nothing rings water once the rain has stopped.
- **Reflections in the wet** (`weathered_prepass`): the depth and normal prepass keeps the same roughness and ringed normal `weathered` makes (`fs_prepass`, `fs_terrain_prepass`), so ray traced reflections ([ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md)) trace wet surfaces as they're drawn. Terrain is marked too rough to trace, but for its puddles (smoother than `TERRAIN_MIRRORS`), which mirror what stands over them.
- **Snow in the wind** (`snow_driven`): the wind (`Frame::clock.yzw`, the `GlobalWind`) drives snow onto what faces it, so `snow_lying` takes a windward slope or wall as flatter than it is: by up to 0.55 of the way to level in a gale (`SNOW_DRIVEN`: none in air stiller than 0.5 studs a second, all of it from 14), and a face in the lee as a little steeper (0.35 of that). In a gale a windward wall is plastered in patches and a lee wall stays bare; in still air nothing changes. Before 2026-11-23 snow lay by the normal's height alone, the same on every side of a house.
- **Snow** (`snow_lying`): covers exposed surfaces by their own normal's height, `clamp(1.5 × cover − 2.5 × (1 − up))`, with a ragged edge (two octaves of noise add or take up to 0.45 of the cover where it's thin, `SNOW_RAGGED`, so light cover lies in patches and whole cover has no gaps on level ground). Thin snow is see-through until it's a few grains deep, and the ground under its melting edge is damp (`SNOW_EDGE_DAMP`). Its surface has a grain that tips the normal (`snow_grain`, fading out from 18 to 55 studs), a few grains that glint in the sun (`SNOW_GLINTS`: one cell in 28 is a flat crystal lying its own way, as smooth as 0.1; the cells double in size with each doubling of the distance, so a glint stays a few pixels across, and they fade out from 30 to 90 studs), a darker cast in the grain's hollows, and it's a little brighter while snow falls (`Frame::precipitation.y`).
- **Snow's depth**: `SnowThickness` lifts exposed upward-facing terrain vertices by the whole thickness (`vs_terrain`; until 2026-11-23 by 35% of it), heaped into long drifts from 0.7 to 1.3 of it (`snow_drift`), and by less under thin cover (none below a cover of 0.2, whole from 0.9). What stands on the ground is buried that deep: stones go under, tyres and pots sink in. Where shelter begins the depth runs out over a stud (the field's cells), and the snow's normal follows that bank (`weather_snow_gradient`), on terrain and on any other surface. The ground counts as sheltered only by what stands well over it (none of it under 2.5 studs, all from 4.5, `SHELTER_LOW`): snow blows in under a bench, a truck or a bush, where the field's roof height alone left a bare, flat patch the shape of the thing. And it drifts against what stands in it (`SNOW_BANK`): each cell of the field keeps how far its top stands over the ground within 3 cells of it (the field's fourth channel, `bank`, at most 3 studs; `bank_heights` in `scene/src/weather.rs`), and the terrain's snow is heaped there by 0.4 of that, up to 0.65 of the thickness, smoothed across cells, so a wall, a tyre or a crate has a bank at its foot and not a flat sheet cut off square.
- **Snow on what stands in it** (`snow_cap`, `snow_cap_cover`, `renderer/snow_caps.rs`): on a solid item snow is a slab too, not a coat of white. `item_vertex` lifts every corner that faces up (its normal's height from 0.45, whole from 0.8, `SNOW_CAP_SLOPE`) straight up by 0.7 of the thickness (`SNOW_CAP_DEPTH`), times the snow's share there, so sheltered corners stay and a weather box's deeper snow lifts further, and times a share that differs from place to place (`snow_cap_uneven`: 0.6 to 1.15, two octaves of noise over x and z, so corners that meet still rise together): a slab of one depth on every chair, pot and sill looked cut from a sheet. Lifting a face leaves a slit at every hard edge, where the top's corners rise and the side's stay, so each mesh is uploaded with a skirt: for every edge whose two faces have corners of their own facing different ways, two more triangles between the one pair of corners and the other, as indices only after the mesh's own (`GpuMesh::cap_index_count`). Unlifted they have no area; lifted they are the slab's sides. Only the prepass, the scene pass and the depth fill draw them, and only while snow with a thickness lies (`drawn_indices`), so shadows, ray tracing and triangle counts never see them. A mesh whose skirt would be over four times its own indices gets none. `pixel_surface` then makes a lifted pixel snow (`snow_cap_cover`, by the lift it was given, in `VertexOut::rise`): all of the top, the side down to a ragged line (up to 0.55 of the depth, `SNOW_CAP_RAGGED`) so the slab doesn't end in a ruled edge, and none where the pixel itself is sheltered though its corners weren't (a long beam half under a roof). Not lifted: cut-outs and leaves (they take snow by their crown, below), swaying, posed and decal items, and see-through ones.
- **Snow in shade** (`snow_glow`): snow passes light on inside itself, so its shaded side isn't grey but takes a cool cast from the sky. Added to snow-covered pixels: 0.12 (`SNOW_GLOW`) of the light from overhead and the ambient, tinted blue (`SNOW_GLOW_COLOR`), by how much of the pixel is snow.
- **Prints**: what's pressed into the snow shows as it's shaped, on terrain and on any other snow-covered surface ([Prints in the snow](#prints-in-the-snow)).
- **Leaves** (`lit_leaf`, `weathered_lying`): rain and snow lie on a plant, not on each card of it. A leaf takes the weather as a surface facing the way its place in the plant's crown faces (65% `leaf_canopy`'s direction, 35% its own shading normal, lifted toward up on the outside of the crown: `LEAF_SNOW_CROWN`, `LEAF_SNOW_LIFT`), so snow caps a bush and thins down its sides though every card stands on edge. Snow on a leaf stops light passing through it. No puddles on leaves. Give a model's leaf cards normals that point out and up from each clump and the caps follow the clumps.

## Lightning

`Weather.Lightning` above 0 flashes (`lightning` in `lighting.wgsl`; `render::Weather::lightning`, `Frame::noise.z`). Time is cut into beats of 2.6 seconds (`LIGHTNING_BEAT`); in each, with a chance of `Lightning`, a flash starts at a moment of its own: a stroke that dies away in about a tenth of a second, a second one a tenth of a second later and, by chance, a weaker third within half a second. It's worked out in the shaders from `DrawList::time` alone, so every pixel and pass sees the same flash, a film rendered twice flashes on the same frames, and it costs nothing between flashes but two hashes a pixel (nothing at all at 0). It needs no rain and no clouds to light the ground, and isn't eased by `TransitionTime` any differently from the rest.

- **On surfaces** (`lightning_arrival` and `lightning_fill`; every kind of surface takes them: `shade_surface` for parts, models, terrain and leaves, and `toon_surface`, `skin_surface`, `hair_surface` and `cloth_surface`, each lighting itself by the flash as it does by a lamp): a cold light (`LIGHTNING_COLOR`) of up to 3.4 at a flash's brightest (`LIGHTNING_LIGHT`; the sun's intensity is 3), whole within 250 studs of the camera and fading to 0.22 of itself by 2200 (`LIGHTNING_REACH`, `LIGHTNING_FAR`: the stroke is taken as near the viewer, or a flash lit a far mountain range as brightly as the yard). 70% of it comes the sun's (or moon's) way and is shadowed as the sun is, by the shadow maps or the rays but not by the clouds (`ArrivalParts::shadow`): a flash throws the hard shadows the overcast hides and comes in through windows. The stroke isn't somewhere of its own because nothing could shadow light from another side. The other 30% (`LIGHTNING_FILL`) is the whole lit cloud overhead: unshadowed, by how far the surface faces up, and only a tenth of it under a roof or in an interior (`LIGHTNING_INDOORS`, the weather field's shelter and `Interior` volumes).
- **In the sky** (`lightning_sky`, added in `fs_sky` after `clouded_sky`): where there are Clouds, the cloud glows round the stroke (within about 700 studs of where the sun's way crosses the layer, moved up to 1400 studs each way by the flash's own offset) and faintly all over, by how much cloud is there.
- Not lit: fog and volumetric fog, rain and snow sprites, water's own shading and the environment maps. No thunder: sound is the game's.

## Weather volumes

A `WeatherVolume` is a Part that sets the weather inside its box by hand, wherever the camera is: an interior the roof rays get wrong, a porch that should stay dry, an overhang the wind blows snow under, a drift that lies deeper than the rest. Insert one from the World ribbon tab's **Post FX ▾ ▸ Weather Volume** (with the other volumes, `Insertable::VOLUMES`), the Explorer's Insert menu or the command palette; it goes into the Workspace with its bottom on the ground. It isn't a `GraphicsVolume`: it changes the world in its box, not the look the camera takes on, and it holds no effects.

Everything a Part has, with its own defaults: anchored, `CanCollide` and `CanQuery` off (so it's no roof to the sky-visibility rays), `Transparency` 1, no shadow, 20 × 12 × 20 studs. Its box is its `Position`, `Orientation` and `Size`, turned with it; its `Shape` is ignored. As made it's a shelter: no drops, dry, no snow.

| Property | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `Enabled` | true | Off, it changes nothing. |
| `Precipitation` | false | Whether rain and snow fall inside it. False keeps drops, splashes and new drips out even under open sky; true lets drops fall inside even under a roof the engine found (an open-sided shed, an overhang the wind blows under). |
| `Snow` | 0 | How much of the world's snow lies inside, from 0 to 2: 0 none, 1 as outside, 2 twice as deep. It replaces the automatic shelter there, so 1 puts snow under a roof. Above 1 the snow is deeper (the terrain's lift and the snow standing on things scale with it); the cover's whiteness is held at 1. |
| `Wetness` | 0 | How much of the world's wetness is inside, from 0 to 2: 0 dry, 1 as outside, replacing the automatic shelter. Above 1 it's wetter than outside, the result held to soaked (`Weather.Wetness` 1). |
| `BlendDistance` | 1 | Studs over which its `Snow` and `Wetness` fade across its faces, half outside them and half inside (as `dust_inside` does for dust). 0 switches at the faces. Drops stop at the faces whatever it is. |
| `Priority` | 0 | Where volumes overlap, the higher wins; equal ones follow Explorer order, the later winning. |

It only scales the world's weather: with no `Weather` in Lighting, or one that's dry and bare, a volume shows nothing.

- **Reading** (`scene/src/weather.rs`, `weather_volumes`): the enabled WeatherVolumes in the Workspace (not the ones kept inside a UnionOperation), sorted by `Priority` (a stable sort, so Explorer order breaks ties), each as a `render::WeatherBox` (`to_box`, `half_size`, `blend`, `wetness`, `snow`; the shares held to 0 to 2) and its `Precipitation`. `build_draw_list` puts the boxes into `DrawList::weather_boxes` in that order.
- **Surfaces** (`lighting.wgsl`): `frame_uniform` packs up to 8 boxes (`MAX_WEATHER_BOXES`) into `Frame::weather_boxes` near the end of the frame's uniform (only `Frame::weather_prints` follows), their count in `Frame::precipitation.z`. With more than 8 in the world the 8 nearest the camera are kept (inside one counts as 0 away), still in their order; none are packed while the weather is clear, and a box with a place or size that isn't finite is left out. `weather_sample` returns the wet share (x), the footprint's depth (y) and the snow share (z): both shares start as the exposure above, then each box in turn takes their place by how far inside it the point is (`weather_box_inside`, `mix(share, box's, weight)`), so the last box at a point wins. `weathered_lying` and `weathered_prepass` wet by the wet share (`weather_wet`; above 1 the world's wetness is scaled up to 1) and lay snow by the snow share held to 1; `vs_terrain`'s lift and `snow_cap` take the snow share whole, so 2 is twice the depth. The snow's normal follows the step in depth at a box's faces (`weather_snow_gradient`, `weather_snow_depth`), as it follows the bank where shelter begins.
- **Drops** (`WeatherState`): the volumes are read again each update while there's weather. `is_drop_occluded` asks them first: the last volume (the one that wins) a drop is inside decides, occluded if its `Precipitation` is off and not occluded if it's on, whatever the roof heights, the painted cells and `Interior` volumes say; `OcclusionEnabled` off doesn't turn that off. Splashes and new drips don't start in a volume whose `Precipitation` is off.

What it doesn't do: snow on a part is lifted at the part's corners (`snow_cap` in `item_vertex`), so a part that reaches from inside a volume to outside it keeps one depth of snow across its top, as it does where it reaches under a roof; terrain is lifted at every vertex and follows the box. Splashes land on what the top-down rays hit, so in a volume under a roof with `Precipitation` on the drops fall but splash on the roof, not the floor. Things press the snow in a volume by the volume's `Snow` (none where it's 0, deeper snow where it's above 1; its `BlendDistance` isn't counted). Sequences don't key a WeatherVolume's properties.

## Prints in the snow

What touches the snow presses its own shape into it, as deep as it has sunk: a foot prints a foot turned the way it points, a plank its rectangle, a ball a bowl as wide as it has sunk, and a thing dragged or rolled leaves a rut as wide as what touches. `scene/src/weather/prints.rs` (`Prints`, inside the host's `WeatherState`) does it while `FootprintsEnabled` is on and the eased `SnowCover` is over 0.05. Until 2026-11-23 a print was a disc of a guessed size at full depth, drawn into the field's one-stud cells.

**What presses** (within 48 studs of the camera, level):

- **Parts and MeshParts** in the Workspace that collide (`CanCollide`) and aren't a character's, kept in a tool or inside a UnionOperation. An unanchored one presses where it lies; an anchored one only while something moves it (a sliding gate, a lift), so a house stands in the snow without a print under it. A part is looked at when its `motion_revision` changes, and 256 of the rest an update in turn (for ones that lie still, and ones the camera left). The list of parts is read again at most every 0.25 s while parts come and go.
- **A character's feet** (a Model with a Humanoid). If a skinned MeshPart in it has foot sockets (Attachments whose `Bone` attribute is `LeftFoot` and `RightFoot`, as the default humanoid's `LeftFootSocket` and `RightFootSocket`, which `anim` moves to the posed bones each frame), each foot presses where its bone is, when it's down: a box 15.5% of the body's height long, 6.5% wide and rounded, turned as the bone is, its sole as far under the bone as the bone has been lowest over the ground. Otherwise a print each stride, left and right in turn, at the character's real leg parts (`Left Leg`/`Right Leg`, or `…LowerLeg`, `…Foot`), sized and turned as the leg and as deep as its foot is in the snow; a character with only a body's box steps either side of it, sized by its height (the block rig's legs and a bare `HumanoidRootPart` only follow the body: their walk isn't in the world).

**Where it touches** (`Contact`):

1. The ground under the thing: a ray straight down from its top through its lowest point that passes the thing itself by (`Surfaces` with the thing as `skip`, so it lands on the terrain or the part the thing stands on; a ray from the sky would land on the thing). Kept while the thing stays within 0.75 studs of where it was found, the plane the ground lies in standing in for it, so a dragged crate asks about every 0.75 studs and not every frame. At most 32 such queries an update; the rest wait (the trail still joins up).
2. The snow's surface: that ground lifted by `SnowThickness` × the cover's easing the shaders use (`smoothstep(0.2, 0.9, cover)`) × the share of the world's snow there (`snow_share`: a WeatherVolume's `Snow`, none in a cell painted Interior, in an Interior volume or under a roof that stands over the thing), lying as the ground does. An approximation: the long drifts (`snow_drift`, 0.7 to 1.3 of the thickness on terrain) and the 0.7 of it that lies on parts aren't counted, the shelter is read from one field cell (the shaders blend four), and the snow counts as at least 0.03 studs deep, so a dusting takes the print of what stands on the ground.
3. The thing's shape under that surface, seen from above, as a convex outline:
   - a block: the part of its box below the surface (its sunk corners and where its edges cross the surface), so a plank lying turned 30° prints a rectangle turned 30° and one that leans prints only the end that's in;
   - a ball: the circle its sunk part cuts in the surface, pressed into a bowl;
   - a cylinder: lying, a strip along it as wide as it has sunk, with a round floor; standing on its end, the end's disc; tipped between the two, the box round it. A capsule: the same strip with round ends, or its lower end's ball;
   - a wedge, a corner wedge and a **MeshPart: the box round it** (`Size`), not the mesh's own shape.
4. **How deep**: how far the thing's lowest point is under the snow's surface, as a share of the snow's depth there. On the ground it presses the snow right through; held half-way in, half as deep; lying on top, not at all (under 3% leaves nothing).

A thing that moved while touching presses everything between where it was and where it is (the outline round both), unless it moved more than 6 studs in the update (it was put there, not dragged).

**No roof to its own ground**: the sky-visibility rays land on whatever collides, a character or a crate included, which made the ground under it sheltered (bare, with snow banked round it, and no rain) until the field looked at that cell again, about 32 updates later: patches that trailed whatever moved. While prints are on, a ray that lands on a character or on a part touching the snow (`Prints::passers`, each one's box and half a stud round it) is cast again past it, for at most 4 such things an update (`PASSERS_AN_UPDATE`). With `FootprintsEnabled` off, and for an unanchored part that isn't in the snow, it's as before.

**The print map** (`render::WeatherPrintCells`, in `WeatherField::prints`): 512 × 512 cells of an eighth of a stud, 64 studs round the camera, moved in steps of 8 studs. Each outline is stamped by its signed distance: nothing outside, sloping down over 0.15 studs to a flat floor (a bowl or a trough for round things, deepest in the middle), and, round a print deeper than 30% (whole from 80%), a rim of pushed-out snow 0.3 studs wide and up to 14% of the snow's depth high (`PRINT_RIM`). A cell keeps the deeper of what it has and what's stamped.

- A cell is one `f32` holding two 12-bit numbers, `press + 4096 × rim`, in 1024 levels to a whole depth. Both are stamped as their value plus how far everything had filled in then (`Prints::fill`), and the shader takes away how far it has filled in now (`Frame::precipitation.w`). So **cells change only where something presses**, though every print fills in every frame. When `fill` passes 2.5 the cells are counted down once.
- It lives in the weather field's storage buffer after the field's 4,096 cells, four cells to a `vec4` (1 MiB), described by `Frame::weather_prints` (its corner, cells to a stud, side), the last field of the frame's uniform. No new binding: the item shader is at wgpu's default limits.
- The renderer writes only the rows whose `row_versions` are newer than the version it holds (`upload_weather_prints`); all of it when the map moves, is counted down, or is another host's.
- The outlines are kept too (`Stamp`, at most 4,096, a trail as one outline per 3 straight studs), so when the map moves, what it newly covers is stamped from them: prints the camera left are there when it comes back, as filled in as they should be. They last until they've filled in.
- The print under a thing that lies still stays whole (stamped again when other prints' filling in has taken a quarter of it) and doesn't count as filling in, so a world of crates at rest asks for no frames.

**Shown** (`weather_print`, `weathered_lying` in lighting.wgsl; `vs_terrain`):

- The field's channel 2 is each stud's average depth, fading out over the map's last 4 studs. The terrain's corners (a stud apart) are pressed down by it, up to 85% of the snow's depth, and it tells a pixel whether any print is near, so untrodden snow reads nothing more.
- Near one, the pixel reads the print map through a smooth 3 × 3 kernel (a quadratic B-spline: 9 cells for the depth and its slope). The slope tips the snow's normal (`PRINT_RELIEF`, held to a tangent of 2.6), so a print reads as a hollow with a lit and a shaded wall; its floor takes the packed snow's color (`SNOW_COMPACTED_COLOR`, bluer) and is smoother; its walls and floor are a little shaded (`PRINT_WALL_SHADE`, `PRINT_FLOOR_SHADE`).
- On any snow-covered surface, a floor Part's as well as the terrain's (a Part's snow isn't pressed down, only shaded).

**Limits**: the map is by X and Z only, so a print under a bridge also shows on the snow on the bridge, and one print's depth is one number (a leaning plank's is its lowest end's). An eighth of a stud is about 4 cm: a foot is two or three cells wide. Prints are gone 28 to 36 studs from the camera (the map's edge, by where its 8-stud step has put it), fading out over the 4 studs before. A step in the ground within 0.75 studs of where it was last found is missed. In the editor a part dragged through snow leaves a rut until it fills in (it isn't saved).

## Rain and snow

They fall in a 64 × 48 × 64 stud box around the camera (`BOX`). Each drop wraps from one side to the other, so the camera never runs out of them. There are up to 7000 raindrops and 8000 snowflakes (`MOST_DROPS`, `MOST_FLAKES`). As many are drawn as the eased intensity says, as sprites that fade toward the box's sides and right at the camera (`RAIN_NEAR`: none within 2 studs, full from 10; `SNOW_NEAR`: 2.5 and 9), since a drop a hand's width from the lens would cross the whole picture. When `OcclusionEnabled` is on, drops below the cell's sky-visibility `roof_y`, inside painted `Interior` cells, or inside `Interior` `GraphicsVolume` boxes are culled so precipitation does not pass through roofs. A `WeatherVolume` a drop is inside decides before any of them ([Weather volumes](#weather-volumes)). Raindrops fall at 60 studs a second (±20% each). Rain is drawn as a veil of many faint streaks, not as lines: each streak (`rain_streak`, soft across, with a tail above and a heavier foot) is about a stud long, longer the faster its drop falls and by its own share (0.7 to 1.3), and each has its own strength (0.35 to 1 of an opacity of 0.45), so no two look alike and none reads as a white bar. It lies along the drop's fall as the camera sees it, wind included, so wind slants the rain (`WeatherState::sprites` takes the list's camera; before 2026-11-23 every streak was upright whatever the wind, and all were one length and strength). Snowflakes are small irregular clumps, not discs: one of four pictures (`snow_flakes`, a 2 × 2 sheet of clumps made of a few soft lobes each) chosen by the flake, 0.12 to 0.36 studs across with most small (`FLAKE_SIZE`), turned along the way it drifts. They fall at 5 studs a second, sway 1.5 studs a second and eddy (`flake_velocity`). Nearer the lens than 14 studs a flake is out of focus: up to 2.6 times as wide and as much fainter (`FLAKE_BLUR`), so the nearest pass as soft blurs. **Spindrift** (`WeatherState::sprites`): where snow lies (`SnowCover` over 0.35, whole from 0.85) and the level wind is over 2 studs a second (`SPINDRIFT_WIND`, all of it from 13), streaks of blown snow run along the ground the way the wind goes: up to 2,600 (`MOST_SPINDRIFT`) faint rain-streak sprites 1.1 to 2.8 studs long, lying along the wind as the camera sees it, 0.06 to 0.6 studs over the snow. They ride on the snowflakes' own places (from the far end of the list, which keep moving while spindrift blows though no snow falls) set down on what's under them: the sky-visibility grid's height there plus the snow's depth, so they cross roofs and the ground alike and need `OcclusionEnabled`. None in an `Interior` volume or a WeatherVolume that keeps drops out.

Both are carried by `GlobalWind`, and both take the scene's light (`WEATHER_LIGHT`, 0.75 of `Sprite::light_influence`, [particles.md](particles.md)): they dim with the day and under cloud and keep a quarter of their own color, where before they were as bright at midnight as at noon.

**Splashes** (`WeatherState::update_splashes`): while rain falls, up to 900 drops a second at Intensity 1 (`SPLASHES_A_SECOND`, at most 64 an update) land on what's under them: a top-down ray each against the world's solid surfaces (the same `Surfaces` the roof heights use), at random within 34 studs ahead of the camera (`SPLASH_REACH`; nearer more often, within about 60 degrees of where it looks, all round when it looks straight up or down). Where one lands (not inside an Interior volume, a cell painted Interior or a `WeatherVolume` whose `Precipitation` is off) a crown of spray stands for about 0.16 seconds (`SPLASH_LIFE`): a sprite with the `rain_splash` picture, widening to about 0.24 studs and thinning as it goes. So rain splashes on the ground, roofs, steps and whatever else collides; a MeshPart is hit as it collides (its hull unless it has collision pieces).

**Drips** (`WeatherState::update_drips`): rain runs off the edges of roofs and ledges. Where the sky-visibility grid shows one cell's top standing 1.5 studs or more over the next's (`DRIP_LEDGE`: an eave, the edge of a porch roof, a truck's cab over its bed, a cliff), drips start along the side the two cells share, up to 7 a second from each stud of edge at Intensity 1 (`DRIPS_A_SECOND`; at most 48 start an update and 900 fall at once). Each falls faster as it goes (28 studs a second each second, gravity on a 0.35 m stud), carried a little by the wind, drawn as a short upright streak that lengthens with its speed, and splashes where it lands on the lower cell's top. They stop starting when the rain does, and follow the grid's cells, so an edge is found to within about half a stud.

## Cost

Weather adds no render pass, render target, or draw call apart from the precipitation and splash sprites while something falls. Every lit pixel skips `weathered` on one uniform test while surfaces are dry and bare. Wet, a pixel reads the field once and two or three noise values; while rain falls, a level wet pixel within 70 studs adds three sheets of rings (a dozen hashes); a snow-covered pixel near the camera adds the grain (nine noise values within 55 studs) and one hash for glints, four more field reads only where shelter begins, and 9 print cells where a print is within about a stud (none in untrodden snow). Each weather box drawn adds a box test to every weathered pixel and lifted corner (none while there are no boxes: the loop runs `Frame::precipitation.z` times). The prepass works out the wet roughness and the rings again (`weathered_prepass`) only while surfaces are wet. Splashes cost up to 64 rays an update on the CPU while it rains.

Lightning, the wind on the snow and the overcast's grey light add nothing that shows in a frame's time. Measured 2026-11-23 on the Desktop of the wiki's Hardware notes (Radeon RX 9070 XT through radv on Linux; the ignored test `weather_looks_cost`, `--release`, a 1920 × 1080 frame of a thousand blocks on a floor, median GPU time of 300 frames): clear 1.32 ms; under an overcast 2.73 ms (the cloud layer itself, marched in the sky); a storm 3.87 ms and the same storm with a flash in every beat 3.85 ms (its slowest frame in a hundred 4.20 ms against 4.12); snow in still air 3.71 ms and in a gale 3.75 ms. Spindrift is up to 2,600 more sprites while it blows.

Prints cost nothing with `FootprintsEnabled` off or under 5% cover, and no upload and no 1 MiB of buffer until something presses. Measured 2026-11-23 on the Desktop of the wiki's Hardware notes (Ryzen 9 9950X3D, its Radeon through radv on Linux, other builds running at the time; `scene` and `physics` at opt-level 3; the ignored tests `prints_cost`, `prints_cost_against_the_world` and `print_map_upload_cost`): with 20 parts dragged through the snow and 4 characters walking, the prints add about 0.10 to 0.12 ms to an update against a flat test ground (about 2 ground queries an update) and 0.2 to 0.27 ms against physics in a world of 2,020 parts (20 dragged), on top of the 0.5 to 0.9 ms the weather's update takes there without them; about 14 of the map's 512 rows (29 KiB) change an update. Writing rows to the GPU's queue takes about 3 µs for a print's 16 rows (32 KiB), 8 to 15 µs for 64 rows and 40 to 70 µs for the whole map (1 MiB), which happens each 8 studs the camera travels over trodden snow.
