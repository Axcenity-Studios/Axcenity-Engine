# Fog

What's far from the camera fading into the air (graphics Phase 25). `DrawList::fog` (`render::Fog`, `render/src/draw_list.rs`) comes from the world (`scene/src/fog.rs`); the renderer packs it into the frame (`renderer/fog.rs`, `FogRaw`, `Fog` in `fog.wgsl`), and every scene shader fades into it as it draws: parts and models (`fs_main`), terrain (`fs_terrain`), sprites (`sprite.wgsl`, premultiplied: the fog's light goes only where a sprite hides what's behind it) and the sky (`fs_sky`, as if it were `Fog::SKY_DISTANCE`, 5000 studs, away). Each surface fogs itself by its own distance, so see-through items, sprites and MSAA need no depth copy; reflections and the environment maps' sky aren't fogged.

## The world's fog

- **Lighting's distance fog**: `FogStart` (default 0), `FogEnd` (default 100000) and `FogColor` (default grey 192). Nothing is fogged nearer than `FogStart` studs, everything is `FogColor` from `FogEnd` on (the sky too, when `FogEnd` is under 5000), straight between. Fogged light is `FogColor` as an unlit color: it doesn't dim at night. A `FogEnd` of 100000 or more is no fog, so worlds made before fog look the same. An end before the start is whole from the start. Properties panel: Lighting's **Fog** section.
- **An Atmosphere's height fog** (exponential height fog), in place of Lighting's (whose fog is then left out). It takes away `density` of the light per stud at and below its height and thins by `e` every `FogFalloff` studs above (default 50, `FOG_FALLOFF`; 0.1 to 10000), worked out exactly along each ray (`fog_transmittance`: the fog's column below a height, `fog_column`, differenced between the two ends). `Density` 0.395 (Earth's) hides half of what's 500 studs away at the bottom of the air, and it scales with `Density`, `1 + Haze` and `FogDensity` (default 1, 0 to 100: the fog alone, leaving the sky and the sun's color as they are); `Density` 0 or `FogDensity` 0 is no fog. Its height is `FogHeight` (world Y in studs, default 0); `Offset` (0 to 1) lifts the world above it, by up to 250 studs (`OFFSET_DEPTH`), so the fog thins. **`FogStart`** (studs, default 0; `Fog::height_start`, `Fog.sun_direction.w` in the shaders) keeps the way nearer the camera clear of it: each ray's fog is taken only from that distance on, so the foreground keeps its contrast and there's no edge where the fog starts (it builds up from nothing there). The defaults are the fog worlds had before these four properties (2026-11-26). Far things fade into the sky's color around the horizon (the gradient's horizon, dimmed by night, or an atmosphere's horizon averaged around it: `SkyLook::colors[1]`; where a creator's `SkyImage` is drawn, above Low reflection quality, the picture's band from the horizon to 6° above it, averaged and dimmed as the picture is: `SkyLook::fog_horizon`, worked out once per picture), so they melt into the sky behind them. Before 2026-11-26 a sky image beside an Atmosphere left the fog the atmosphere's own clear-sky horizon: a blue haze under Overgrown Street's grey overcast picture. The sun (or moon) scatters 80% of its light in it (Henyey-Greenstein, `fog_phase`, forward by 0.6 + 0.03 × `Glare`), so the fog glows toward the sun and reddens with it at sunset.

## Fog volumes, dust in a box, and interiors

A GraphicsVolume's `FogEffect` with a `Density` above 0 ([volumes.md](volumes.md)) is dust, mist or haze filling the volume's box (`render::Dust`, `Fog::dust`), such as a valley's haze or a room's air: light passing through it is lost at `Density` per stud, and `Color` of what's lost is scattered, forward by 0.6 + 0.03 × `Glare` (Henyey-Greenstein). Unlike the volume's other effects it's in the air, not a look the camera takes on, so it's drawn wherever the camera is. It thins to nothing across the box's faces over the volume's `BlendDistance` (half outside, half inside), and, with a `HeightFalloff` above 0, by `e` every `HeightFalloff` studs above the box's bottom (haze lying low). It's separate from an Atmosphere: thick dust doesn't thicken the sky or redden the sun, as an Atmosphere's `Density` and `Haze` do.

- **Many at once** (LE2, `docs/research/lighting-engine-audit.md`): up to 8 boxes (`MAX_DUST_BOXES`, `DustBoxes`), the 8 nearest the camera (`scene/src/graphics_volumes.rs`'s `dust_boxes`), applied lowest `Priority` first, then in Explorer order. Before LE2 only the nearest box was drawn.
- **Interiors.** A `FogEffect` with `Interior` on makes its volume an interior: inside the box the air outside it is cleared (the world's height fog, Lighting's distance fog and the boxes applied before it), and only the effect's own `Density` fills it (0 for clear air). So a house in a misty valley has clear air, or its own dust, inside. In the grid each cell's outdoor fog is kept by `1 − inside` for each interior (`dust_at`); along a surface's way (`fogged_along`) the share of the way inside interiors gives back what the outdoor fog took there, taking the fog as even along the way.
- **In the editor.** Lighting ribbon > Volumes > **Fog Volume** inserts a `FogVolume`: a 200 × 60 × 200 stud GraphicsVolume, its bottom a little under the ground, `BlendDistance` 20, with a FogEffect of Density 0.02, Glare 2, HeightFalloff 20 and a pale blue-grey. **Interior** inserts a room-sized `Interior` (`Priority` 1, `BlendDistance` 1) with a FogEffect marked Interior, Density 0.015 and a warm dust color, so light through its windows shows as shafts. Resize either to fit.
- **Surfaces** behind it (`fogged_along`) lose the light of the stretch of the way inside each box (`dust_span`, a ray-box intersection, its density taken at the stretch's middle), the dust taken as nearer than the rest of the fog.
- **Its light**: with volumetric fog on, the grid scatters the sun's light through it with its shadows (shafts through windows and gaps), the lamps', and the light around each cell (`surrounding_light`: light probe volumes' or ray traced GI's probes, the sky's where there are none), so a closed room's dust is dark where the sun doesn't reach it. Past the grid's reach (200 studs), and everywhere without volumetric fog, it's lit flatly by the sky's average and the unshadowed sun (`DustBox::ambient`, `sun`), so a big fog volume still shows far away.
- Dust alone turns the volumetric fog on at Ultra (`volumetric_drawn`), with no Atmosphere needed.
- **Reading a box in a shader.** fog.wgsl has no bindings, so its functions take the `Fog` as a value, but they read each box through `fog_dust(i)`, which whoever includes the file defines over its own uniform (`frame.fog.dust[i]` in lighting.wgsl, `sprites.fog.dust[i]` in sprite.wgsl). Indexing the array on the value (`fog.dust[i]` with `fog` an argument) copies all eight boxes to pick one, for every box looked at, on every pixel: with Desert Homestead's two boxes (a valley's haze and the house's air) that was 50 ms of GPU time a box at 2560 × 1440 on an RX 9070 XT under radv, a scene pass of 110 ms on High in place of 10 (2026-11-25, [performance.md](performance.md#fog-boxes-read-one-at-a-time-desert-homestead)). A new array in a struct the shaders pass around by value needs the same care.

## Volumetric fog

The machine's `volumetric_fog` setting (on at Ultra only; `UserGameSettings.VolumetricFog`; [settings.md](settings.md)) lights an Atmosphere's fog, and dust, with the sun's shadows (sun shafts) and with lamps (`renderer/fog.rs`, `volumetric_fog.wgsl`). Each frame, after the shadow maps, two compute passes fill a grid of 160 × 90 cells across the view by 64 slices along each ray, out to `VOLUMETRIC_REACH` (200 studs; slices about 0.15 studs deep at the camera and 15 at the far end): `cs_scatter` gives each cell its fog and dust and the light they scatter toward the camera: the sun's by how much of the cell it reaches (`cell_sun_visibility`: the nearest cascade's comparison at 8 points spread through the cell, the first of the Halton (2, 3, 5) sequence, the same in every cell; one point in the middle made shafts step from cell to cell and the steps crawl as the view moved, and shifting the points at random per cell left a speckle TAA's clamp can't average; with ray traced shadows on, cells within their reach use rays toward the sun instead, `traced_air`, traced by ray_shadows.wgsl's `cs_air` (LE3, [ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md))), each lamp listed for the cell's screen tile, with the lamp's own falloff, cone and shadows, and height fog's own color by how open the air is to the sky (`openness`: the light probes' light around the cell over the open sky's, 1 with no probes; before, a closed room's air glowed with the open sky's color, which hid every shaft); Each frame `cs_scatter` looks at each cell through different points (`cell_shift`: the fixed points shifted, wrapping, by the frame's turn of the Halton (2, 3, 5) sequence plus a shift of the cell's own; turn 0, on the grid's first frame or after a camera cut, shifts nothing) and blends what it finds with the grid's history: last frame's grid, looked up where the cell's middle was in last frame's view (`with_history`, last frame's camera in `FogHistoryRaw`), keeping 90% of it (`HISTORY_KEEP`), so about the last 10 frames are averaged and a still view settles on many points per cell. The two scatter grids take turns being written and being the history. Before this (2026-11-14), every cell used the same 8 (shadow maps) or 4 (rays) points every frame: a fine shadow, such as vines over a window, came out as cell-sized blocks, worst with ray traced shadows, which have no shadow map filtering to soften them, and a thin lit layer (a dust box poking out of a roof) as stair steps. A camera moving more than 25 studs or turning more than about 40° in a frame (`CUT_DISTANCE`, `CUT_TURN`) is a cut: the grid starts again. Moving lamps and items leave a short trail in the fog as the history catches up (about a sixth of a second at 60 frames a second). `cs_gather` adds them up along each ray, front to back, each slice dimmed by the fog in front of it (Hillaire's energy-conserving step). After the scene pass (MSAA resolved), `fs_composite` adds the light gathered up to each pixel's solid surface (the prepass's depth) or all of it in front of the sky, smoothed across the screen by a cubic B-spline over the 4 × 4 cells around (`smooth_gathered`, 4 filtered looks; straight interpolation showed each cell as a soft diamond). A cell's height fog is taken only past the Atmosphere's `FogStart`, by the share of its slice beyond it (`started` in `cell_scatter`), so the grid and the surfaces' own fog agree where the fog starts. Within the reach, the surfaces' own shaders leave the sun's scattered light out (`Fog.height.w`), and height fog's own color too (`Fog.distance.w`, unless there's distance fog, which keeps its flat color), so open air looks the same with it on or off; only shadowed or closed-in air (and lamp light) changes. See-through items and sprites take the light up to the solid surface behind them. Lighting's distance fog is a flat color and isn't lit.

It costs nothing below Ultra or without an Atmosphere's fog: no grids, no passes, and the prepass only if ambient occlusion or contact shadows want it. While on, it needs the prepass's depth (the setting keeps the prepass), three 160×90×64 `Rgba16Float` grids (two scatter grids that take turns, and the gathered one: 22 MB, in `RenderStats::target_bytes`) and the `VolumetricFog` pass in the profiler (its composite counts toward Scene). Its compute shaders see the frame's bindings through a copy of the frame's layout visible to compute (`frame_layout_entries`). On a GPU without compute shaders it stays off with a note.

## Tests

- `render/src/renderer/tests/fog.rs` (GPU): parts nearer than `FogStart` are clear, halfway is half fog, and past `FogEnd` parts, sprites and the sky are all fog; far parts fade into height fog, a part as far but higher is less fogged, and raising the fog's height fogs it again; a part nearer than the height fog's start is exactly as without fog, one past it less fogged than with no start, and a short falloff leaves a low part as fogged while clearing one 24 studs up (`height_fog_starts_at_its_start_distance_and_keeps_low_by_its_falloff`); with a start, a short falloff and the sun scattering, parts near and far look the same with volumetric fog on or off (`shaped_height_fog_looks_the_same_with_or_without_volumetric_fog`, within 8 of 255; without the start in the grid, the part inside it glowed at 65 in place of 0); at Ultra the air in front of a wall that shadows it loses the sun's light while open air looks the same as with volumetric fog off, and grey height fog's own light looks the same too (`height_fogs_own_light_looks_the_same_in_the_open_with_volumetric_fog`); dust glows only inside its box, with volumetric fog on or off, and at Ultra loses the sun's light in a wall's shadow; two boxes glow at once; an interior clears thick height fog along the way inside it, with volumetric fog on or off, and its own dust fills it again; below Ultra or without fog no grids exist and fog adds no draws or target bytes, and at Ultra the grids are freed when the fog goes. With ray traced shadows and no shadow map at all, the wall's shadow still darkens the air before it while open air is unchanged, a see-through wall lets the sun through, and turning them off frees the traced air (`ray_traced_shadows_trace_the_suns_shafts_in_the_air_without_shadow_maps`, ray queries only). A still view of shafts through a fine grille settles smoother than its first frame and then holds still, with and without ray traced shadows (`a_still_view_settles_on_a_smoother_grille_shadow_and_holds_still`).
- `render/src/renderer/atmosphere_sky.rs`: height fog fades into a creator's sky image's band above the horizon (not its sky or ground), dimmed with it at dusk, and into the colors at Low or with an atmosphere's own sky (`height_fog_fades_into_a_creators_sky_image_around_the_horizon`).
- `render/src/renderer/fog.rs`: the grid's first frame has no history and shifts nothing, later frames keep last frame's camera and a turn that never comes back to 0, and a cut to another view starts the grid again; no fog by default, distance fog keeps its color and range and scatters no sun, height fog takes the horizon's color and its share of the sun and packs its start distance (0 for one below 0 or not a number), height fog's own light moves into the grid only without distance fog, and dust packs its box, color and the sun's light it scatters. `render/src/renderer/tests.rs`: the setting-switching test checks the grids exist only while volumetric fog is on.
- `scene/src/graphics_volumes.rs`: a FogEffect with a Density fills its volume's box with dust seen from anywhere, keeping Lighting's fog; every fog volume is drawn at once, an interior applying after lower priorities with its HeightFalloff.
- `scene/src/fog.rs`: a new world has none; Lighting's fog takes its start, end and color; an Atmosphere's replaces it, thicker with Haze, tighter with Glare, lifted by Offset, and none at Density 0; it takes its `FogHeight`, `FogFalloff`, `FogStart` and `FogDensity` (Offset still lowering the height, a falloff of 0 a thin sheet, a start below 0 at the camera, `FogDensity` 0 no fog).
- `render-types/src/graphics.rs`: volumetric fog is on only at Ultra, and off with a note without compute shaders.
