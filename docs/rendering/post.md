# Post-processing

HDR, bloom, tone mapping, color grading and lens effects.

- The scene draws into an `Rgba16Float` texture. A bloom chain (prefilter above `BloomThreshold`, downsample, additive upsample) feeds a composite pass that adds bloom, applies `Exposure` and tone mapping into the real target (or, with FXAA on, into an LDR target that FXAA then smooths into the real one), then the UI draws on top. With MSAA on, the scene pass draws multisampled and resolves into the HDR texture before bloom. Anti-aliasing is a graphics setting: [shadows.md](shadows.md). The machine's post-processing setting is a ceiling on `PostProcessingEnabled` (off on the Low preset; [settings.md](settings.md)), while `Exposure` and `ToneMapping` are the world's look, and no setting changes them.
- Broken pixels don't spread: every post pass reads the scene and bloom through `finite_light` (post.wgsl), which counts a pixel that isn't a number as black and one brighter than 16 bits hold (or infinite) as 65,504, and one pixel gives bloom at most 2,048 above the threshold, so bloom's added-up levels stay within 16 bits. Without it one such pixel (a sky image's sun past 65,504, or 0 / 0 in a shader) grew into a flashing square as bloom blurred it.
- Lighting properties `PostProcessingEnabled`, `Exposure`, `ToneMapping`, `BloomEnabled`, `BloomIntensity` and `BloomThreshold` configure it. Off means a plain clamp with no bloom, whatever the curve.
- `ToneMapping` (`Enum.ToneMapping`, `render::ToneMapping`) picks the curve, after exposure:
  - `Classic`: each channel under 0.6 unchanged, brighter ones roll off toward 1 (it shifts the hue of bright colors). The default, so a world saved without the property looks as it did.
  - `Neutral`: Khronos PBR Neutral. Takes off the 4% a non-metal reflects (less in the darkest colors), so a lit part shows about its picked sRGB color, then compresses highlights without shifting hue. The editor's templates (`project::Template`) write it into new worlds.
  - `Filmic`: Stephen Hill's ACES fit, after scaling light by 1/0.6 so a mid-grey stays about as bright. More contrast; very bright colors wash toward white.
  - `render/src/tone_mapping.rs` is a CPU copy of the curves (`ToneMapping::apply`) that must match `post.wgsl`; a GPU test checks they agree. Scripts set it with `game.Lighting.ToneMapping = Enum.ToneMapping.Filmic` (or the name), and blocks with "set tone mapping to …".
- `scene` draws Neon parts 2.5× brighter than their color when bloom is on, so they pass the threshold and glow.
- **Dither** (`dithered` in post.wgsl): the finished picture is kept in 8 bits a color, which shows a smooth sky or a dim wall as bands of single shades. While tone mapping is on, the composite pass adds noise of under one shade to each pixel's sRGB encoding before it's rounded (two hashed numbers' sum, so it averages to nothing; fixed per output pixel), which breaks the bands up. Pure black and white get none, and with tone mapping off (`PostProcessingEnabled` false, flat colors drawn as they are) nothing is added. It comes before FXAA, which sees no edge in it.
- **Upscaling** (roadmap G9; `upscale.wgsl`, `renderer/post.rs`): while the render scale is below 100%, the composite pass (and FXAA, if on) draws the finished picture at the scene's size into an 8-bit target (`FrameTargets::upscale`, written through an sRGB view and read through a plain one, so the next passes see sRGB-encoded values). An edge-adaptive upscale after AMD FSR 1.0's EASU (12 texels per pixel, a Lanczos-like kernel stretched along the edge it finds, kept within the four nearest texels' range so it never rings) takes it to the output's size, and a contrast-adaptive sharpen after FSR's RCAS (0.2 stops below its strongest, less where a pixel looks like noise) writes the output. With TAA on, TAA upscales instead ([temporal.md](temporal.md#upscaling-inside-taa)): bloom and the composite pass run at the output's size on TAA's result, and only the sharpen runs after them (`UpscaleTarget::upscaled` is `None`). It's automatic: no setting, nothing at 100% or above (a scale above 100% is still scaled down by the composite pass's linear sampler). The UI is drawn after, at the output's size as before. Vignette, grain and grading are applied before the upscale, so film grain is at the scene's pixel size (with TAA's upscale, the composite pass and its grain are at the output's). The two passes count as post-processing in the GPU timings. On an RX 9070 XT (`bench_upscaling`, ignored, `--release`), they cost about 0.13 ms at 1080p and 0.2 to 0.25 ms at 1440p and 4K. On that bench's light scene of blocks, half scale took the GPU frame from 1.9 to 1.0 ms at 4K and from 1.0 to 0.64 ms at 1080p; at 1440p the gain was within run-to-run noise. Heavier scenes save more, because the scene's cost shrinks with the pixel count and the upscale's doesn't. At half scale on the test block, the upscaled frame is about a third closer to the full-size frame than a bilinear stretch, and TAA's upscale after 48 frames is closer still (`renderer/tests/upscaling.rs`; `bench_upscaling` also times TAA's upscale, not yet measured on the RX 9070 XT). FSR's MIT notice is in `NOTICE`.

## Color grading

Graphics Phase 32. A `ColorCorrectionEffect` inside Lighting changes the finished picture's colors. The composite pass (`fs_composite` in `post.wgsl`) applies it after tone mapping and the clamp, on sRGB-encoded values (as the screen shows them), then turns them linear again for the sRGB target. With FXAA on, FXAA smooths the graded picture. It is the world's look. No graphics setting changes it, and it applies with post-processing off too (it costs one full-screen pass's arithmetic, not a target).

| Property | Default | What it does, in order |
|---|---|---|
| `Enabled` | true | Off, or with no effect in Lighting, nothing changes and nothing is bound. |
| `Brightness` | 0 | Added to each channel (-1 black, 1 white). |
| `Contrast` | 0 | `(c − 0.5) × (1 + Contrast) + 0.5`; -1 is flat grey. |
| `Curve`, `CurvePivot` | 0, 0.5 | An S-curve on each channel (`s_curve`), clamped to 0–1 first: below the pivot `p` it's `p × (c / p)^k`, above it the same mirrored into white, meeting at the pivot with slope `k` both sides, so the pivot stays, black and white stay, and nothing clips (slope 0 at both ends). `k` is `1 + Curve` above 0 (deeper shadows, lifted highlights; 2 at most) and `1 / (1 − Curve)` below (flatter, ½ at least). 0 is no curve. The pivot is kept 0.05 from black and white. |
| `Saturation` | 0 | Mixed away from the Rec. 709 grey by `1 + Saturation`; -1 is black and white. |
| `ShadowTint`, `HighlightTint`, `Balance` | white, white, 0 | Split toning (`split_toned`): each tint is first divided by its own Rec. 709 brightness (`split_tint`, on the CPU), so it shifts the hue and keeps the brightness; the color is multiplied by a mix of the two by `smoothstep(0, 1, brightness + Balance / 2)`, so dark colors take the shadow tint and light ones the highlight tint. `Balance` (-1 to 1) above 0 gives more of the picture the highlight tint. White tints are off, whatever the balance. |
| `TintColor` | white | Multiplies each color, then the result is clamped to 0–1. |
| `LookupTable` | "" | A `.cube` file or a strip image the color is looked up in. |
| `Posterize` | 0 | 2 or more cuts each channel into that many even levels (`min(floor(c × n), n − 1) / (n − 1)`). |
| `Palette` | "" | An image; each pixel becomes the nearest of its colors (straight distance in sRGB). |

Several enabled effects in Lighting add up: brightness, contrast, saturation, `Balance` and `Curve` add, and tints (`TintColor`, `ShadowTint`, `HighlightTint`) multiply. The first in Explorer order with a `Curve` gives its `CurvePivot`, and the first with a lookup table, a `Posterize` level or a palette gives that one (`scene/src/color_grading.rs`, into `DrawList::grading`). An effect anywhere else does nothing. `workspace.CurrentCamera` isn't an Instance, so effects can't go inside it; a `GraphicsVolume` gives a place its own look, and the effects inside one replace Lighting's while the camera is there ([volumes.md](volumes.md)).

**Files** (`assets/src/color_grading.rs`, read by `Assets::color_lookup` and `Assets::palette`, cached, reloaded when changed, and each failure reported once while colors stay as they are). The editor's pickers copy a file from outside the project into `assets/grading`.

- `.cube` (Adobe/Resolve): `LUT_3D_SIZE N` (2 to 128), then N³ lines of `r g b` with red changing fastest. `TITLE`, `#` comments and other keywords are skipped. `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX` must be 0 and 1. 1D tables are refused.
- A strip image: N squares of N × N pixels side by side (256 × 16, 1024 × 32). Square `b` is blue `b/(N−1)`. In it red grows to the right and green downward. Its values are sRGB as stored.
- A palette: any PNG, JPEG, TGA or BMP image. Its different opaque colors, row by row, are the palette, up to 64 (`MAX_PALETTE_COLORS`). With more, a warning says only the first 64 are used.

**Split toning and the curve** (2026-11-26, for Overgrown Street's foreground road against a hazy treeline) are applied before the lookup table, so a fitted `.cube` sees the graded picture. They were picked over lift/gamma/gain: a creator asking for "deeper shadows, cool shadows, neutral highlights" gets one control for each, the curve can't move black or white (lift and gain do, and clip), and both stay inert at their defaults. With them off (white tints, `Curve` 0) the shader skips them (`highlights.w`, `curve.x` 0), so a world's picture is bit for bit what it was. **Filmic's contrast**: the ACES fit has no toe or contrast setting of its own, and none is added. The curve after it does the same job in display space. Checked numerically on grey from 8 stops under mid-grey to 5 over (2026-11-26): Filmic given 15% more contrast in log exposure about mid-grey (`0.18 × (x / 0.18)^1.15` before the curve, the usual "contrast" of a filmic curve) is matched by plain Filmic with `Curve` 0.2 about `CurvePivot` 0.5 within 1 of 255 (without the curve they differ by up to 8), and 10% and 20% by `Curve` 0.13 and 0.27 within 1.3. It works under every tone mapping curve and with tone mapping off too.

**Drawing** (`renderer/grading.rs`). The composite pipelines have a second bind group: the lookup table as an `Rgba32Float` 3D texture and `GradingUniform` (matching `Grading` in `post.wgsl`: values, tint and on flag, counts, split toning's tints with the balance and on flag, the curve's power and pivot, 64 palette colors). The shader reads the table's eight nearest entries with `textureLoad` and blends them itself, so a neutral table gives back exactly what it got and no float filtering is needed. The table's texture is made when its key changes and dropped when none is used (a 1×1×1 placeholder stays bound). A table with the wrong number of entries, or bigger than the GPU's 3D limit, is left out.

`crates/samples/projects/grading` grades a colorful scene with a warm `.cube` table. Its LocalScript steps through looks with G: warm, cold night (tint), black and white, posterized, a retro palette, and none.

Tests: `render` `renderer/tests/color_grading.rs` (`each_control_changes_the_frame_as_it_says`; `the_curve_deepens_shadows_and_lifts_highlights_about_its_pivot_without_clipping`; `split_toning_tints_shadows_and_highlights_apart_keeping_their_brightness`, with the balance moving where shadows end; `the_new_controls_at_their_defaults_give_back_the_picture_exactly`, byte for byte with Off and MSAA; `a_neutral_lookup_table_changes_nothing`, with tables of 2, 17 and 33 and with Off, FXAA and MSAA; `a_lookup_tables_texture_lives_only_while_one_is_used`). `assets` `color_grading::tests` (`.cube` parsing and errors, strips, palettes). `renderer/grading.rs` (tints scaled to white's brightness, white exact; the curve's power; defaults off). `scene` `color_grading::tests` (effects combine, split toning and the curve too, and files load from the project).

## Lens effects

Graphics Phase 34. Five effect Instances inside Lighting, each off until one is added: `DepthOfFieldEffect`, `VignetteEffect`, `FilmGrainEffect`, `ChromaticAberrationEffect` and `SharpenEffect`. Of each kind, the first enabled one in Lighting (Explorer order) is used (`scene/src/lens.rs`, into `DrawList::lens`, a `render::LensEffects` of five options). They are the world's look. Each also has its own graphics setting ([settings.md](settings.md)), so a machine can turn any of them off, and Low turns them all off. `PostProcessingEnabled` doesn't affect them. An effect inside a `GraphicsVolume` does nothing ([volumes.md](volumes.md)).

| Effect | Properties (defaults) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `DepthOfFieldEffect` | `FocusDistance` 20, `InFocusRadius` 10, `NearIntensity` 0.75, `FarIntensity` 0.75 | Sharp from `FocusDistance − InFocusRadius` to `FocusDistance + InFocusRadius` studs. Nearer, the blur grows to `NearIntensity` at the camera; farther, to `FarIntensity` at twice the sharp range's far edge (the sky is fully far). Full blur is a radius of 1/72 of the scene's height (15 pixels at 1080). |
| `VignetteEffect` | `Intensity` 0.5, `Size` 0.5, `Color` black | From `Size` (0 the middle, 1 the corners) out to the corners, the picture turns toward `Color`, up to `Intensity`. Round on any screen. |
| `FilmGrainEffect` | `Intensity` 0.3, `Size` 1 | Noise of ±0.2 × `Intensity` in sRGB, a new pattern every frame, in grains `Size` pixels of a 1080-pixel-tall view across (never under one pixel). The average brightness stays. |
| `ChromaticAberrationEffect` | `Intensity` 0.5 | Red is read from farther out and blue from farther in, by 2% × `Intensity` of the distance from the middle (1% of the width at the side edges for 1): nothing in the middle, colored fringes toward the edges. |
| `SharpenEffect` | `Intensity` 0.5 | Each pixel moves away from the average of its four neighbors by 2 × `Intensity` of the difference: flat areas stay, edges steepen. Below 100% render scale the upscale already sharpens; this adds to it. |
| `MotionBlurEffect` (graphics Phase 35) | `Intensity` 0.5 | What moves across the view streaks along `Intensity` of the way it moved in a frame; what moves with the camera stays sharp. Drawn from the motion vectors: [temporal.md](temporal.md). |
| `SunRaysEffect` | `Intensity` 0.25, `Spread` 0.5 | Shafts of light streaming from the sun past what's in front of it (bright sky between leaves, a window). Each pixel counts the sky on its way toward the sun on screen, so gaps in leaves or a window frame become beams; `Spread` is how far beams reach. Worked out on screen, so they show only while the sun is in or near the view. Its own setting is `UserGameSettings.SunRays`. |

Each also has `Enabled` (true). Disabled, absent, or turned off in the settings, an effect costs nothing.

**Drawing.**

- **Depth of field** (`renderer/lens.rs`, `depth_of_field.wgsl`) is a pass of its own after the scene pass (and volumetric fog's light), before bloom. It needs the prepass's depth, so while it's drawn it keeps the prepass alive (like screen-space outlines). It draws into a blurred copy of the scene (`FrameTargets::depth_of_field`, an HDR target at the scene's size) that bloom and the composite pass read in the scene's place. Each pixel averages the pixels under a spiral of 16 (Low) or 32 (High) points on a disc as wide as its blur, each counting only as far as its own blur reaches back (`saturate(its blur − distance + 1)`). Color and depth come from the same whole pixel, so a sharp surface never smears into a blurred one beside it. A blurred surface in front of a sharp one doesn't spill over it either: near blur stops at its own silhouette. Depth of field that blurs nothing (both intensities 0) makes no target. See-through items aren't in the prepass, so they blur by what's behind them.
- **The other four** are in the composite pass (`fs_composite` in `post.wgsl`), from `PostUniform`'s `lens`, `vignette` and `vignette_color` (`lens::add_lens`; 0 is off and the shader skips it). Chromatic aberration and sharpening change how the scene (or its blurred copy) is read, before bloom and exposure. Sharpening is judged on the unparted scene, so fringes aren't sharpened. The vignette and grain are applied after tone mapping, on sRGB-encoded values, before color grading. Probe bakes draw without lens effects.
- **Sun rays** (`renderer/sun_rays.rs`, `sun_rays.wgsl`) are a pass of their own after the scene pass and volumetric fog's light, before TAA and bloom, added to the scene. They need the prepass's depth, so while a world has them and the setting is on they keep the prepass alive (`sun_rays::wanted`). `sun_rays::uniform` projects the sun's direction onto the screen; they're off with the sun behind the camera, fade out as it goes past the screen's edge (gone 0.6 half-screens past it) and below the horizon. Each pixel takes 48 points toward the sun, as far as the beams reach (0.15 to 1 screen height by `Spread`), starting a little apart per pixel (interleaved gradient noise) so the steps don't band. Under TAA the start turns each frame (`SunRaysUniform::noise`, the frame number as ray traced shadows have it), so TAA averages the noise away instead of leaving a fixed dither over walls in the glow; without TAA it stays still. Rays only show while the sun is on or near the screen: for light through windows with the sun out of view, use volumetric fog with dust ([fog.md](fog.md)). A point counts if it's sky in the prepass (nothing solid drawn there, so a glass window counts as open), less the farther it is from the pixel and from the sun. The sun's light (color × intensity) times that, times 1.5 × `Intensity`, is added. A gap in a canopy so becomes a beam, and the leaves around it cast dark streaks through the glow. Tests: `renderer/tests/sun_rays.rs`.

`crates/samples/projects/lens` looks down two rows of pillars to a striped wall through all five. Its LocalScript steps through them one at a time, all together and none, with L.
