# Character Studio

Character Studio has two places: a **workspace** of its own, opened from the start screen with no project (`docs/research/character-studio.md` CS2, decision D9), for making and dressing characters; and the **tab** in the Game Editor, for a character's face.

## The workspace

Code: `crates/editor/src/character_workspace.rs` (the description, starting a character, the preview World, the library, saving) and `character_workspace/`, split by area (refinement R1, [audit-studio-ui.md](../development/character-studio/refinement/audit-studio-ui.md) section 3): `shell.rs` (the layout, shortcuts, notices; the one place that applies an `Intent`), `nav.rs`, `panel.rs` and `panel/` (parameters, the slider row, the skin materials), `toolbar.rs`, `bottom_bar.rs`, `compare.rs` (held versions and the strip over the viewport), `presets.rs` (presets by ID, a person's own, favourites), `random.rs` (Randomize and its locks), `autosave.rs` (the recovery file); the contracts they share, `intent.rs` (everything a panel can ask), `selection.rs` (category, feature, level: editor state, never saved) and `edit.rs` (undo with transactions, reset by scope); `stage.rs` (camera, lighting, backdrop). Each area draws from `&CharacterWorkspace` and returns `Vec<Intent>`. The editor's side, which opens it and asks before leaving, is `app/character_workspace.rs`; the start page is `start/characters.rs`. The contract is [architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) (sections 1, 5 to 7 and 15); Character Studio overhaul CO12 moved the workspace onto it.

- **Opening it.** The start screen's sidebar (or top bar) has **Character Studio**, after Projects (its page: [the start page](#the-start-page)). `axcenity-editor --character-studio` opens straight into it. It needs no project; while it's open the Game Editor's menus aren't shown, and the window is titled with the character's name (`*` while unsaved).
- **Its files** are in the editor's data folder, `Character Studio/` (on Windows `%APPDATA%\Axcenity Editor\data\Character Studio`): the default humanoid's files (`project::add_default_humanoid`), the **character library** in `assets/characters/` and the saved characters, `characters/<name>.character.json`. The library is the built-in stylized package (`add_character_library`) plus every package the engine ships (`add_character_packages`, which copies a package's manifests, the files they name and its presets when they're missing or differ from the shipped ones, in size or bytes, so an older copy is brought up to date (audit D2), leaves the folder's own files and IDs alone, and writes the merged catalogue). The shipped library is found by `find_character_library`: `assets/characters/` (with a `catalogue.json`) in the folder the editor was started from, in a folder above the editor's program (an installed editor has it beside itself: `packaging/stage.py` puts it in every payload) or in a debug build's source tree. Without it only the stylized body is offered, with a notice. Beside them: a person's own presets (`presets/*.preset.json`), the favourites (`favourites.json`) and the autosave's `recovery/character.json`.
- **The layout** (brief §3.1). A toolbar (Home, the character's name to type in, "Not saved", Undo, Redo, Randomize, New and Open menus, Reset with a scope menu, Save), the categories down the left in the brief's order (Body, Face, Hair, Clothing, Skin and Makeup, Accessories, Expressions, Presets; each with the count of its values that differ from the default, and from the Detailed level the open category's features: the manifest's groups, such as Eyes, Nose, Jaw), the viewport in the middle with the comparison strip over it and the bottom bar under it (views Full body, Face, Front, Three-quarter, Side, Back, the one the camera is at lit; lighting Studio, Daylight, Sunset, Night; backdrop Grey, Light, Dark, Sky: none of it saved), the contextual panel on the right, and a status line (the camera's mouse hints or the last notice, which clears itself after 8 s; where characters are saved). The side panels narrow before the viewport does.
- **The panel** shows only the open category, or the feature picked in it ("Face › Nose", with a close mark). Its header has the **level** (Quick, Detailed, Advanced: one flat filter; Quick shows presets and the body type, Detailed every feature's sliders, Advanced adds the manifest's advanced parameters and typed numbers) and **Symmetry** (on by default; not shown at Quick, which always moves both sides, nor on a page without pairs). A changed value the level hides is said ("1 more change in Advanced", a link to that level). Until the manifest has `level`, `feature` and `about` (R1 step 1), `selection.rs` reads them from what it has: `advanced` is Advanced, a parameter with choices Quick, everything else Detailed; the feature is the group; the tooltip is the label. A slider row has its label (tooltip), its value (typed at Advanced), a reset to the default when it differs (also a double click) and the inspectors' changed bar.
- **Families** (plan section 0, decision 7): both the realistic and the stylized family are offered (`offers_base` is every base the library has; `presets::families` lists realistic first). New lists each family with its whole-character presets, or its default character where it has none (the stylized one); the Body page starts with **Family** chips (the one the character is on lit; another starts again on that body, one undo step). A stylized character's controls come from its own manifest: its groups stand in for features in the navigation, Randomize's feature scope and the levels (`selection.rs`'s fallbacks).
- **Presets** are the library's `*.preset.json` files and a person's own (architecture §15a), addressed by ID (`Start::Preset`, `Intent::ApplyPreset(id)`), applied by `avatar::apply_preset` (only what the kind owns; the name kept; one undo step). The Body and Face pages start with their kind's presets as **Start from** chips (a body preset tagged `male` or `female` shows only on that body type), the applied one lit and **Custom** lit when none matches (`matches_preset`). The **Presets** page groups the character's base model's presets by kind (Characters, Bodies, Faces, then Hair, Outfits, Skin when there are any) as picture tiles drawn by the thumbnails painter (`thumbnails::preset_subject`: a part preset on its base's default character, a face preset framed on the face), favourites first with a star, and ends with **Save as preset**: a kind and a name write `presets/<name>.preset.json`, a format 2 description with a `preset` block (`my/<name>` IDs, only what the kind owns; a body preset leaves out the body type and is tagged with it; an existing file is never written over). Favourites are editor state in `favourites.json`, never in a character or the library.
- **Randomize** (brief §11, `random.rs`): the toolbar's menu draws Everything, the open category or the picked feature again with `avatar::randomize`, as one undo step, saying how many locked values it keeps. Every slider row and the body type have a lock; the body type (`frame`) is locked by default. Locks are editor state. The seed is a counter mixed with the character (not the clock), so the same presses on the same character give the same result.
- **Comparison** (CO19, brief §12, `compare.rs`): the strip over the viewport holds the current character as A, B or C (up to three; never an undo step, never saved), shows one in the character's place with the same camera and light (editing is off and the viewport says so; an edit asked anyway goes back to the current one first), or **Side by side**: a second character on a floor of its own in the preview World, 30 studs to the side (both go when side by side is turned off), applied by the same applier, drawn in the left half with the same camera moved over to it. The strip and the viewport's labels ("A (held)", "Current", "Showing A") sit on small cards of the panel's colour, so they read on every backdrop. **Keep** makes the shown version the character (one undo step, the name kept); the trash discards it; starting another character drops them all.
- **Autosave** (CO20, `autosave.rs`): unsaved work (a change since the save, or since a new character started) is written to `recovery/character.json`, outside `characters/`, at most every 3 s and never during a drag; Save and a clean close remove it. When the studio opens on a folder with one it asks **Recover the unsaved character?**: Recover brings it back unsaved, to its own file if it had one; Discard removes it. The start page's picture painter never writes it.
- **Body**: the manifest's `body` groups, a section each with a slider per parameter, except a parameter with `choices`, which is a chip per choice: the realistic body's **Body type** is Male or Female (`frame` 0 or 1), not a slider. Picking one is one undo step and wears that choice's `items` (Male: boxer briefs; Female: briefs and a sports bra). What a choice wears is always on (`chosen_slots`, `tidy`): the slots it fills aren't on the Clothing page, so the underwear can't be taken off or swapped, and a saved character without it gets it when it opens. The same rule holds outside the studio (`avatar`'s `wear_choices`, run by `resolve` and the Character Runtime's applier): a script's `SetItem` or `RemoveItem` on those slots is an error, and setting `frame` dresses for it. A value between the choices (from a script or an older file) is taken to the nearest when the character is edited. **Face**: the manifest's `face` groups, folded (nine of them) until one is opened or picked in the navigation. Face and Expressions frame the face (the Face view aims at the head bone as posed, so a tall or short character is framed too); leaving them for another page frames the whole character again, unless the camera was moved. With Symmetry a mirrored pair is one slider ("Left ear protrusion" is "Ear protrusion") that sets both, while its sides agree; a pair whose sides differ shows both. A drag is one undo step. **Hair**, **Clothing** and **Accessories**: the manifest's slots of that category, each a row with the items of the catalogue that fit this body and the slot's empty choice ("Bald", "None"); a slot nothing fits isn't shown, nor one a choice fills (the realistic body's underwear, which comes with the body type; the page says so); with nothing to show, a short empty state. The realistic body's items so far are its underwear (`assets/characters/realistic/underwear.py`); its hair and clothes are CO10 and CO11. The Face page's Brows group has brow height, arch, length (the brows' card drawn in or carried down the temple) and ridge. **Skin and Makeup**: every value of the base's skin-shaded materials (the realistic body's `Skin` slot: tint as a colour button; melanin, redness, undertone, roughness scale, detail strength and scatter scale as sliders), written to `description.materials` (a value at its neutral setting is left out); until the skin shading model lands (CO7) the renderer draws only the tint and roughness, and the others say "not drawn yet". **Expressions**: the 15 built-in expressions as chips; one shows on the preview's face (a FaceControls of the preview's own on the body, as the capture tool does), never in the description and never an undo step, and leaving the page takes it off.
- **Undo** is the workspace's own (`edit.rs`): each change of the description is a step; a drag is one transaction (Esc cancels it back to where it started), and a change of nothing (a slider clicked where it is, the name field visited) makes no step and keeps redo. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z redo (redo is matched first: egui ignores an extra Shift). Reset offers Everything (the body's default character, keeping the name), the open category (its sliders, the items of its slots, for Skin its material values; the body type stays) and the picked feature, each one step. **Save** (Ctrl+S) writes `characters/<name>.character.json`; another character's file is never written over: the character is saved under the first free "<name> 2" and the status line says so. After a rename the old file is moved, with its Recent entry, pictures and pack places, not left beside the new one. **New** (a family or a preset) and **Open** (a saved character) ask Save, Don't Save or Cancel when there are unsaved changes, as Home does; so does closing the editor's window (the character is in the editor's list of unsaved documents).
- **The character file** is the engine-wide description, format 2 ([architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) §5): `name`, `base`, `parameters` by the manifest's IDs, `items`, `materials`. A format 1 file (CS2's `Look`: build, muscle and a part per slot) opens on the stylized body wearing what it wore and saves as format 2. Values out of a parameter's range are brought in and parameters the body hasn't are dropped; a file on a body the library hasn't opens the default character with a notice; a newer format is refused with a message.

## The start page

Code: `start/characters.rs` (the page and what its buttons do) with `start/characters/cards.rs` (the cards) and `dialogs.rs` (the pack editor, the questions before deleting); the files behind it are `character_workspace/shelf.rs` (listing, overview, delete), `recent.rs`, `packs.rs` and `thumbnails.rs`.

- **From the top:** New Character (the realistic default character); **Recent**, the characters last opened or saved, the first four as cards and "Show N more" / "Show less" for the rest; **Your characters**, every saved one, newest first; **Packs**, with New Pack… and Import Pack…; and **Start from a preset** (each family's whole-character presets, realistic first, and the stylized family's default character). The files are listed again every 3 s while the page shows. Cards are at least 150 points wide and a row fills the page (two columns in a narrow window, where New Character goes under the title).
- **A card** shows the character's picture, its name and an overview (`shelf::overview`): the body type (each parameter with choices, such as Male or Female), the base model's label and how many other values are set, then the hair (or the hair slot's empty label, "Bald") and what it wears (the clothes, or the underwear its body type wears when that's all). The overview is of the character as shown, with what its body type wears put on. A saved character's card has **Edit** (opens it in the workspace; clicking the picture does too) and **Delete…** (a trash button), which asks first in the editor's standard dialog and then removes the file, its pictures, its Recent entry and its place in every pack (`shelf::delete_character`). Presets are read-only: their button is **Start from this**.
- **Recent** is `recent.json` in the Character Studio folder: `{format_version: 1, characters: [file names in characters/]}`, newest first, at most 24. Opening a saved file (`CharacterWorkspace::open_file`) and saving (`save`) put it first; files outside `characters/` aren't tracked; a file that's gone drops out when it's listed; Delete takes it out. It's per user, like the folder.
- **Pictures** (`thumbnails.rs`) are the workspace's own preview drawn offscreen: a workspace of their own (the default character with the description applied by the applier, idled ten frames, Studio lighting, Grey backdrop, a full-body camera a little turned) drawn by the scene renderer into a 360 × 450 texture. They're cached as PNGs in `thumbnails/characters/<file>.<key>.png` and `thumbnails/presets/<preset ID>.<key>.png` (the ID with `/` as `-`), the key a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of what's drawn (the file's text, or the character the preset makes and its framing), the picture's version and the engine's. A changed file has a new key and is drawn again; writing a picture removes the older ones of the same file. Missing ones are drawn on a thread of their own ("character pictures") on the editor's GPU device (`StartScreen::set_gpu`, set each frame from the render state), one at a time, while the card shows a placeholder with the initials and a spinner; the page reads at most four from disk a frame. The thread stops after 20 s with nothing to do, and a picture that fails twice is given up on for the session. A save in the workspace (`CharacterWorkspace::saved_files`, drained by the app, and the Save of "Save changes?") asks for its fresh picture at once. The workspace and the picture thread both write the library into the folder; a lock (`WRITING_LIBRARY`) keeps them apart. Until the workspace has opened once, overviews use the library the engine ships (`shelf::library_for`).
- **Packs** (`packs.rs`) are a named set of saved characters, `packs/<name>.pack.json`: `{format_version: 1, name, characters: [file names in characters/]}`. **New Pack…** and a pack's **Edit…** open the pack editor (a name and a checklist of the saved characters with their pictures; Make Pack / Save Pack); a new name renames the pack (refused if another pack has it). A pack's card shows its name, how many characters, a trash button (Delete asks; the characters are kept), their pictures (then "+n"), **Edit…**, **Export…** and **Add to Project…**. A deleted character leaves its packs; a missing one is skipped.
- **Export** writes one plain JSON file, `<name>.characterpack.json`: `{format_version: 1, kind: "axcenity.character-pack", name, characters: [whole descriptions, format 2]}`, so it stands alone. **Import** checks `kind` and refuses a newer format, saves each character into `characters/` (one already there with the same contents is used; a different one with the same name becomes "Name 2", renamed inside too) and makes the pack (its name gets a number if taken).
- **Add to Project…** picks a game project's folder and writes the pack's characters to `assets/characters/descriptions/<name>.character.json` (replacing ones of the same name), beside the project's character library ([architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) §15), and writes the built-in library and the engine's packages into the project (`add_character_library`, `add_character_packages`) so the bodies and items they wear are there. A game applies one by setting a `HumanoidDescription`'s `Data` to the file's text.

## The tab

Character Studio's tab is a document tab beside the Scene (View > Character Studio, the ribbon's Panels menu, or Ctrl+K "Character Studio"). It shows the selected character's face in a preview of its own, with the **Face** workspace (facial animation F6, decision D9 in [research/facial-animation.md](../research/facial-animation.md)). The default-humanoid work adds its body and customization workspaces to the same tab.

Code: `crates/editor/src/character_studio.rs` (state, preview, the tab) with `character_studio/rig.rs`, `expressions.rs` and `lip_sync.rs` (one per section); the editor's side, which applies what the tab asks for, is `app/character_studio.rs`.

## What it shows

- It follows the selection: a FaceControls in a MeshPart, a MeshPart (with its FaceControls if it has one), or a Model or folder with a FaceControls in a MeshPart inside (`face_target`). With something else selected it keeps the last character.
- The preview draws a copy of the MeshPart alone, at the origin and unturned, posed at rest with its face set by `anim::pose_face` (blend shape weights and eye and jaw turns through the matched profile). The camera looks at the head bone (`anim::face_point`; a model without bones is framed on its top) from the way the body faces. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, double-click to frame again.
- What the face shows depends on the section: the FaceControls' own values (Rig), the expression (Expressions), or the visemes at the play head (Lip sync).

## Rig

- **Profile**: the FaceControls' `Profile` (Best fit, a built-in one, or one of the project's `*.face.json`), set as one undo step. The line under it names the profile matched and how many of the 48 controls move something on this model.
- **Check**: what doesn't fit (`anim::check_face`), errors first; after six, the rest fold.
- **Controls**: the FaceControls' 48 controls as sliders, grouped (eyes, eyebrows, mouth and lips, cheeks and nose, jaw and tongue), with a filter. A slider is an edit like Properties' (one undo step per drag). Controls that move nothing on the model are greyed. Without a FaceControls the sliders are off; expressions and lip syncs still preview.
- **Key in Animation** selects the FaceControls and opens the Animation tab, where its controls are rows to key in a clip (F4b). That is the face's timeline: keys, curves and playback are the Animation tab's.
- **Profile mapping** (folded at first): Edit mapping copies the matched profile, keeping only the shapes the model has. Each of the model's blend shapes can be given to one control with a weight (`map_shape`: a shape belongs to one control at a time). The Jaw, LeftEye and RightEye bones can be found by name, set to none, or named; the eye and jaw limits are in degrees. Save profile writes `assets/faces/<name>.face.json` (one undo step) and makes it the FaceControls' `Profile` (another). The preview and the check follow the draft while it's edited.

## Expressions

- The project's expressions, then the built-in ones it doesn't replace (`anim::expression_names`); built-in ones are greyed, and saving one makes the project's own copy.
- **Preview at** scales it; **Blend with** another expression mixes the two by **Blend amount** (preview only).
- The expression's own intensity, transition and the controls it sets (add, change, take out). Save expression writes `assets/faces/expressions/<name>.expression.json` (one undo step); a game plays it with `face:SetExpression("<name>")`.

## Lip sync

- Open any `*.lipsync.json` in the project (hidden folders and `target`, `build` and `node_modules` are skipped; the arrows list the files again).
- **Make from** makes one beside a sound (`.ogg`, `.wav`, `.mp3`, `.flac`: from how it sounds) or phoneme timings (`.lab`, `.tsv`, `.txt`: `start end phoneme` per line), as the Console's `lipsync` does, saves it (one undo step) and opens it.
- The timeline shows the lip sync's sound as a waveform over its length, and its visemes as blocks, each until the next. Click the waveform to move the play head; click a block to pick it, drag it to retime it (kept between its neighbours, `retime`); double-click to add a viseme there (`add_key`). The picked viseme's row changes which viseme it is, its width (weight) and its time, or deletes it; Add at play head adds one.
- Play plays the visemes on the face through the FaceControls' `VisemeMap`, `LipSyncSmoothing` and `LipSyncAnticipation` (or the defaults), as a game would. The sound doesn't play in the tab.
- Length changes the lip sync's duration. Save writes it (one undo step); "Not saved" shows until then.
