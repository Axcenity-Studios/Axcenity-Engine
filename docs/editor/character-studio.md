# Character Studio

Character Studio has two places: a **workspace** of its own, opened from the start screen with no project (`docs/research/character-studio.md` CS2, decision D9), for making and dressing characters; and the **tab** in the Game Editor, for a character's face.

## The workspace

Code: `crates/editor/src/character_workspace.rs` (the description, starting a character, the preview World, the library, saving) and `character_workspace/`, split by area (refinement R1, [audit-studio-ui.md](../development/character-studio/refinement/audit-studio-ui.md) section 3): `shell.rs` (the layout, shortcuts, notices; the one place that applies an `Intent`), `nav.rs`, `panel.rs` and `panel/` (parameters, the slider row, the skin materials), `toolbar.rs`, `bottom_bar.rs`, `compare.rs` (held versions and the strip over the viewport), `presets.rs` (presets by ID, a person's own, favourites), `random.rs` (Randomize and its locks), `autosave.rs` (the recovery file), `direct.rs` (editing on the character: hover, click to select, handles); `sides.rs` (the asymmetry tools), `motion.rs` (the preview's pose and motion), `browser.rs` (the Hair and Clothing browsers: what is offered, search, filters, favourites, recently used, drafts, a tile previewed while hovered and worn by a click; drawn by `panel/browser.rs`, the worn items' lines and controls by `panel/worn.rs`), `wardrobe.rs` (the Clothing page's online wardrobe: its list, downloads into the library and pictures; drawn by `panel/wardrobe.rs`); the contracts they share, `intent.rs` (everything a panel can ask), `selection.rs` (category, feature, level: editor state, never saved) and `edit.rs` (undo with transactions, reset by scope); `stage.rs` (camera, lighting, backdrop). Each area draws from `&CharacterWorkspace` and returns `Vec<Intent>`. The editor's side, which opens it and asks before leaving, is `app/character_workspace.rs`; the start page is `start/characters.rs`. The contract is [architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) (sections 1, 5 to 7 and 15); Character Studio overhaul CO12 moved the workspace onto it.

- **Opening it.** The start screen's sidebar (or top bar) has **Character Studio**, after Projects (its page: [the start page](#the-start-page)). `axcenity-editor --character-studio` opens straight into it. It needs no project; while it's open the Game Editor's menus aren't shown, and the window is titled with the character's name (`*` while unsaved).
- **Its files** are in the editor's data folder, `Character Studio/` (on Windows `%APPDATA%\Axcenity Editor\data\Character Studio`): the default humanoid's files (`project::add_default_humanoid`), the **character library** in `assets/characters/` and the saved characters, `characters/<name>.character.json`. The library is the built-in stylized package (`add_character_library`) plus every package the engine ships (`add_character_packages`, which copies a package's manifests, the files they name and its presets when they're missing or differ from the shipped ones, in size or bytes, so an older copy is brought up to date (audit D2), leaves the folder's own files and IDs alone, and writes the merged catalogue). The shipped library is found by `find_character_library` (`project::shipped_character_library`, then a debug build's source tree): `assets/characters/` (with a `catalogue.json`) in the folder the editor was started from, in a folder above the editor's program (an installed editor has it beside itself: `packaging/stage.py` puts it in every payload) or in a debug build's source tree. Without it only the stylized body is offered, with a notice. Beside them: a person's own presets (`presets/*.preset.json`), the favourites (`favourites.json`: the favourite presets, and since R6 and R7 the browsers' favourite items, `items`, and the items worn last from them, `recent_items`, newest first, at most 12; an older editor keeps only `presets`) and the autosave's `recovery/character.json`.
- **The layout** (brief §3.1). A toolbar (Home, the character's name to type in, "Not saved", the **Edit on character** switch, Undo, Redo, Randomize, New and Open menus, Reset with a scope menu, Save), the categories down the left in the brief's order (Body, Face, Hair, Clothing, Skin and Makeup, Accessories, Expressions, Presets; each with the count of its values that differ from the default, and from the Detailed level the open category's features: the manifest's groups, such as Eyes, Nose, Jaw), the viewport in the middle with the comparison strip over it and the bottom bar under it (views Full body, Face, Front, Three-quarter, Side, Back, the one the camera is at lit; lighting Studio, Daylight, Sunset, Night; backdrop Grey, Light, Dark, Sky: none of it saved; the bar fits the viewport, `bottom_bar.rs`'s `shape`: every choice is a chip where there is room for them all, and under a narrower viewport first the backdrop, then the lighting, then the views become a menu that says what is on, as the pose and motion is, so nothing is drawn over anything else), the contextual panel on the right, and a status line (the camera's mouse hints or the last notice, which clears itself after 8 s; where characters are saved). **The panel's width** is the person's: 400 points until they drag its inner edge (a strip between the viewport and the panel; a double click puts it back), between 320 and 640. It is editor state (`Intent::SetPanelWidth`: never saved with a character, never an undo step), kept in `favourites.json` (`panel_width`) when the edge is let go. A window too narrow for it narrows the panel first, down to 270, so the viewport keeps 420 (`shell::panel_width`); narrower still, the viewport narrows. The navigation is 15% of the width, 150 to 200 points.
- **Editing on the character** (refinement R2, brief §3.4, §4.3, §5.3; `direct.rs`, `direct/`). Moving over the character highlights the feature under the pointer (an overlay over the preview, never a material change: the regions a body feature names; for a face feature, the skin its shapes move strongly near its anchors and handles, leaving out a shape that moves over four times the skin its feature's shapes usually do (the mouth's height and the philtrum's length move the whole muzzle, the jaw's shift the lower face), shared with its neighbours where theirs move it more, so the eyes stop under the brows and above the cheeks; or round its anchors) and names it in the status line. **The highlight is a soft mask, read from what the camera sees** (`direct/depth.rs`, `direct/mask.rs`; 2026-11-24). Each time the camera, the pose, what is worn or the lit features change while something is selected or hovered, the character as posed (its body, then each part it wears) is rasterized once on the CPU into a buffer of the viewport, a texel per 2 points, that keeps for each texel the nearest triangle and where in it. A texel is the feature's when the weight of the surface it shows is at least a half, the same rule a click picks by. That cover is blurred (a box of one texel, four times each way) and eased: an even tint inside that fades out over about 8 points, a little stronger along its edge (a soft rim from the mask itself; there is no outline and nothing follows a mesh's edges). It is drawn as one textured quad, its texture allocated once per viewport size and updated in place where it changed. Nothing is compared with a depth a second time, so layers of cloth cannot flicker against each other (before, the feature's own triangles were drawn as a tint with an outline along them and tested against the depth buffer to 3 mm, which on clothes was blotches and zig-zags). Skin that faces away from the camera carries no weight, nor does a hollow (the eyes' corners, a nostril); an opening (an eye, the mouth) stays open. The hovered feature is lit stronger than the selected one; none is lit while a handle is dragged. With Idle motion the pose changes every frame, so the mask is made every frame (measured in a release build, the default character in a tee and joggers, a viewport of 830 by 780 points: about 2 ms, about 4.5 ms with the face filling the view); a still pose reuses it. **A feature shows on what covers it** (ADR-0045; `direct/worn.rs`): each point of a worn item takes the weights of the body corners near it at rest (up to 8, within 1.2 cm of the nearest's distance, none beyond 12 cm; `direct::worn::WornMap`, worked out once per body shape and worn model). The points are the item's corners and, on every triangle whose corners lie over different body regions (and, more coarsely, any over 4 cm), a lattice of points about 1.2 cm apart, so a feature's edge on a coarse garment runs where the skin's does, not along the garment's triangles; a clothed waist is lit on the cloth over it. **What is picked on cloth is what is lit on it**: a ray that meets a worn item first picks the smallest feature whose weight on the cloth there is at least a half; only where the cloth carries none does it pick the skin up to 25 cm behind (3 cm behind the base's own parts, an eyeball). A part with no skeleton to follow the body by, and the base's own parts (underwear, brows, lashes), carry no weights and count 1.5 cm farther than they are, so the skin just under them lights. **Handles are hidden only by skin** (the same buffer's depths of the body alone, taken before the worn parts are added), so a handle under a coat shows on the coat and can be dragged: the body is adjusted dressed. A handle more than 3 mm (plus a texel's slant) behind other skin isn't drawn unless skin within about 6 points of it shows (a handle at the skin's own outline, such as the side of the hips from the front, is drawn). So the far jaw angle's handle doesn't sit by the mouth in the Face view, and at three-quarter the far shoulder isn't lit over the chest. A hidden handle can't be grabbed. What's picked is what's lit: the smallest feature whose weight at the point under the pointer is at least a half (on a worn item, the cloth's own there); a handle under the pointer names its own feature (a shoulder's sits on the border of two regions). A click selects it: its page and its controls open, as from the navigation (Quick goes to Detailed, where features start), and the camera frames it when it's under 6% of the view's height or off the middle, over a quarter of a second (one well in view stays put). The selected feature shows its handles, small markers on the skin that move with it: the nose's tip, bridge and nostrils, each eye's upper lid and outer corner, the jaw's corners, the mouth's corners and lips, each shoulder, side of the waist and hip. Hovering one grows it, draws the directions a drag works in this view and says what each does. **A primary drag that starts on a handle edits; any other turns the camera** (Alt with a drag always turns it; the right button pans). The drag sends the slider's own intents, one undo step for the whole drag; Esc cancels it; Symmetry moves the mirror; the other handles hide while it lasts and a handle at its parameter's end turns amber. A drag means what the view shows: a drive works only in the framings its manifest names, along the direction its skin moves on the screen there; how far is the studio's numeric rule (below): the whole range in 110 points along that direction, whatever the zoom (the skin under the handle doesn't follow the pointer one to one), Shift a quarter, Ctrl double. A second drive of a handle waits until its own part of the drag passes 8 points. A click on the backdrop drops the selection. The toolbar's switch turns all of it off: no highlight, no handles, the viewport is the camera only.
- **The panel** shows only the open category, or the feature picked in it ("Face › Nose", "Body › Waist", with a close mark). Its header is one line: the title (cut short with "…" where it doesn't fit; its tooltip says what the page is for, which only the Expressions page still says in a line of its own), the close mark right after it, and at the right **Symmetry** (on by default; not shown at Quick, which always moves both sides, nor on a page without pairs; an icon where the title leaves no room for its word) and the **level**, a menu that shows the current one and lists Quick, Detailed and Advanced with what each shows (one flat filter; Quick shows presets and the body type, Detailed every feature's sliders, Advanced adds the manifest's advanced parameters). Under it, on the Body, Face and Skin and Makeup pages, **Search controls**: while it has text the page lists every control of the category that matches every word typed (capitals aside) in its label, ID or `about`, whatever feature is picked and at every level, its groups unfolded; a control above the level shown says its level after its label ("Advanced"); a pair shown as one row is found by either side; the Skin page's material values and makeup layers are searched too; the Body page's Family and Start from chips give way to what is found; "No control matches …" when nothing is. The text is editor state (`Intent::SearchControls`, `CharacterWorkspace::control_search`): never saved, never an undo step, cleared when another category is picked; the picked feature and the level stay as they were. A picked feature's page (Face › Nose, Face › Face shape) starts with **Shape** chips: the `feature` presets of that feature (`PresetKind::Feature`, `PresetInfo::feature`; ten noses, six face shapes), the applied one lit and **Custom** lit when none matches; a click is `Intent::ApplyPreset`, one undo step, and sets that feature's parameters only (`panel.rs`'s `preset_chips` with a feature). A changed value the level hides is said ("1 more change in Advanced", a link to that level). Until the manifest has `level`, `feature` and `about` (R1 step 1), `selection.rs` reads them from what it has: `advanced` is Advanced, a parameter with choices Quick, everything else Detailed; the feature is the group; the tooltip is the label. A slider row is one line, about 26 points (`panel/row.rs`): its label in a fixed share of the width (`responsive::name_width`; cut short with "…", its tooltip the whole label and `about`), its track, its value (48 points wide; click to type it, at every level) and, after it, a 16-point column for each of its marks: ⇄ (a pair's side tools; kept empty on the unpaired rows of a group that has pairs, so the group's tracks line up), the lock, and the reset to the default, drawn only while the value differs (also a double click on the track) in a column that is always kept, so a drag never moves the track it drags. A choice (Body type) is one line too: its label, its chips, its lock. A changed row has the inspectors' changed bar; what a row says about a typed value goes under it while it lasts.
- **Numeric controls** (RA1, Paul's drag rule of 2026-11-18; `numeric.rs`, the one helper): every numeric customization control, the body's and the face's slider rows, the Skin page's values, makeup and markings, and the handles on the character, follows one rule, for consistency. A whole range takes **110 points** of drag (horizontally on a row, however wide; along a handle's direction, whatever the zoom); **Shift** a quarter, **Ctrl** double (egui's `command`: Cmd on macOS), both half; a key pressed partway doesn't make the value jump. The drag is relative: a press on the track doesn't move the value to the pointer, a click changes nothing, a double click resets. A drag is one undo step; Esc cancels it back to where it started. **Typing**: a click or a double click on a row's value opens it (its text selected); Enter or clicking away sets it as one undo step, Esc leaves it; a value outside the range is set to the nearest end and the row says so under it for 4 s; text that isn't a number changes nothing and says so. A selected handle's parameters are typed in their rows. The value shows two decimals, or three where the range's ends need them (the waist's 1.175). With the track focused (Tab) the arrow keys move it 1/110 of the range. **A control added later** (the Expressions page's, a browser's, an item's own parameters) uses the panel's slider row (`panel/row.rs`) or `numeric::track` (which takes its width: the drag is the same whatever it is) and `numeric::field`, and a drag of its own `numeric::NumberDrag` or `numeric::Travel` with `numeric::points_per_unit`, never an `egui::Slider` or `egui::DragValue`. The colour buttons' picker is egui's and isn't covered.
- **Families** (plan section 0, decision 7): both the realistic and the stylized family are offered (`offers_base` is every base the library has; `presets::families` lists realistic first). New lists each family with its whole-character presets, or its default character where it has none (the stylized family has three since wave 3: Masculine, its default character as built, Feminine and Athletic); the Body page starts with **Family** chips (the one the character is on lit; another starts again on that body, one undo step). A stylized character's controls come from its own manifest: its groups stand in for features in the navigation, Randomize's feature scope and the levels (`selection.rs`'s fallbacks).
- **Presets** are the library's `*.preset.json` files and a person's own (architecture §15a), addressed by ID (`Start::Preset`, `Intent::ApplyPreset(id)`), applied by `avatar::apply_preset` (only what the kind owns; the name kept; one undo step). The Body and Face pages start with their kind's presets as **Start from** chips (a body preset tagged `male` or `female` shows only on that body type; on a base whose frame is a slider, the stylized one, a body preset names its frame and shows only on a character whose frame is within half the slider's span of it, so applying it keeps the frame: `presets::suits`), the applied one lit and **Custom** lit when none matches (`matches_preset`). The **Presets** page groups the character's base model's presets by kind (Characters, Bodies, Faces, then Hair, Outfits, Skin when there are any) as picture tiles drawn by the thumbnails painter (`thumbnails::preset_subject`: a part preset on its base's default character, a face preset framed on the face, a body preset framed neck to knees, `Subject::Body` and `body_orbit`, so the builds read apart at a tile's size: neighbouring realistic body tiles differ in 6 to 13% of their pixels, where the full-body framing gave 2 to 5%), favourites first with a star, and ends with **Save as preset**: a kind and a name write `presets/<name>.preset.json`, a format 2 description with a `preset` block (`my/<name>` IDs, only what the kind owns; a body preset leaves out the body type and is tagged with it; an existing file is never written over). Favourites are editor state in `favourites.json`, never in a character or the library.
- **Randomize** (brief §11, `random.rs`): the toolbar's menu draws Everything, the open category or the picked feature again with `avatar::randomize` (which never draws an item marked `draft`, asset-quality §5), as one undo step, saying how many locked values it keeps. Every slider row and the body type have a lock; the body type (`frame`) is locked by default. Locks are editor state. The seed is a counter mixed with the character (not the clock), so the same presses on the same character give the same result.
- **Comparison** (CO19, brief §12, `compare.rs`): the strip over the viewport holds the current character as A, B or C (up to three; never an undo step, never saved), shows one in the character's place with the same camera and light (editing is off and the viewport says so; an edit asked anyway goes back to the current one first), or **Side by side**: a second character on a floor of its own in the preview World, 30 studs to the side (both go when side by side is turned off), applied by the same applier, drawn in the left half with the same camera moved over to it. The strip and the viewport's labels ("A (held)", "Current", "Showing A") sit on small cards of the panel's colour, so they read on every backdrop. **Keep** makes the shown version the character (one undo step, the name kept); the trash discards it; starting another character drops them all. The status line says what is shown: "Showing A (held). Editing is off until you go back to Current.", "Side by side: A beside the current character…", "Showing the current character." (it used to keep the hint left from holding, "Held as A…"); discarding the version shown, or starting another character, takes a line about it away.
- **Asymmetry tools** (R4, brief §5.4, `sides.rs`): for left and right pairs (two parameters that name each other as `mirror`; the side by ID, `_left` or `_right`, then by label), **Mirror left to right**, **Mirror right to left**, **Reset left side** and **Reset right side**, left and right being the character's own. A group with pairs starts with a **Left and right** menu for the pairs of its feature that the level shows (`SidesOf::Feature { feature, level }`: a pair the level hides is left alone, and "N more changes in Advanced" still counts it); each paired row has a ⇄ menu for its own pair (`SidesOf::Pair`). Each is `Intent::Sides { tool, of }`, one undo step; a tool that would change nothing is greyed out, and asked anyway it makes no step. Mirroring copies a side's values across (each side's parameter is written for its own side); a reset puts the side back to the body's default character's value. Symmetry is unchanged: once the sides agree, a pair is one row again. The stylized body has no pairs.
- **Pose and motion** (R9, brief §10, `motion.rs`): **Still** (the rest pose, the A-pose), **Idle** (the state machine, what the preview always played; the default), **Walk**, **Run** and the default character's gestures (**Wave**, **Point**, **Cheer**, **Clap**, **Shrug**), as chips on the Expressions page and as a compact menu in the bottom bar after the views (the walking figure and what plays, in the accent colour while the body moves). `Intent::PreviewMotion`: editor state like an expression, never in the description, undo or a saved file, kept across pages and characters; an expression shows on top of it. A take plays as the capture tool plays a clip: the Animator's state machine is cleared and an Animation of the preview's own ("Studio Motion Preview"), looped at speed 1, plays a clip written once to `assets/animations/Humanoid/Preview/<take>.anim.json` in the workspace's folder, linked to the take over its whole length. **Walk is the Walk take at its own speed** (1.07 s a cycle), in place; the game's Walk state keeps the Run take, because the controller's default `WalkSpeed` is a jog (the R1 merge's decision), so the true walk is a studio preview. Run is the Run take at its own speed. The held version side by side moves too. While a take plays the camera follows the head (`follow_motion`: smoothed at 6 per second, from where the head was when the motion started; a close view follows it whole, a far one only across the floor); stopping frames the view again. In the Face view a run keeps the head 0.016 of half the picture's height off its middle on average and 0.10 at worst (0.15 and 0.28 without following).
- **Stylized presets** are `assets/characters/humanoid/presets/*.preset.json`, written by `presets.py` beside its manifest with the stylized parameters: Slim, Athletic, Heavy and Broad bodies for each end of its frame (each names `frame` 0 or 1), Round, Long, Angular and Soft faces, and the Masculine, Feminine and Athletic characters, listed in the catalogue.
- **Autosave** (CO20,
 `autosave.rs`): unsaved work (a change since the save, or since a new character started) is written to `recovery/character.json`, outside `characters/`, at most every 3 s and never during a drag; Save and a clean close remove it, and so does **Don't Save** when the editor asks before closing (`EditorApp::discard_unsaved` calls `discard_recovery`, which also stops the same work being written again before the window closes), so a quit that throws the work away never leaves it to be offered. When the studio opens on a folder with one it asks **Recover the unsaved character?**: Recover brings it back unsaved, to its own file if it had one; Discard removes it. The start page's picture painter never writes it.
- **Body**: the manifest's `body` groups, a section each with a slider per parameter, except a parameter with `choices`, which is a chip per choice (the chips go on to a second line when they don't fit beside the label, so none is past the panel's edge: the human base's five skins on the Skin and Makeup page): the realistic body's **Body type** is Male or Female (`frame` 0 or 1), not a slider. Picking one is one undo step and wears that choice's `items` (Male: boxer briefs; Female: briefs and a sports bra). What a choice wears is always on (`chosen_slots`, `tidy`): the slots it fills aren't on the Clothing page, so the underwear can't be taken off or swapped, and a saved character without it gets it when it opens. The same rule holds outside the studio (`avatar`'s `wear_choices`, run by `resolve` and the Character Runtime's applier): a script's `SetItem` or `RemoveItem` on those slots is an error, and setting `frame` dresses for it. A value between the choices (from a script or an older file) is taken to the nearest when the character is edited. **Face**: the manifest's `face` groups, folded (ten of them) until one is opened or picked in the navigation. Face and Expressions frame the face (the Face view aims at the head bone as posed, so a tall or short character is framed too); leaving them for another page frames the whole character again, unless the camera was moved. With Symmetry a mirrored pair is one slider ("Left ear protrusion" is "Ear protrusion") that sets both, while its sides agree; a pair whose sides differ shows both. A drag is one undo step. The realistic face (2026-11-24, `assets/characters/realistic/README.md`, "Finer face controls") starts with **Face shape**: the outline's width, length, taper to the chin (a heart at one end, a square jaw at the other), roundness and narrowness. Selecting it shows a ring of 14 handles round the face: the top of the forehead, each temple, the outer edge of each cheekbone, each cheek, each jaw corner, each side of the jawline, each corner of the chin and the chin. Dragging one reshapes the outline there; the ring's handles also move the forehead's height and slope, the temples' width, the cheekbones' height, the jaw's angle and definition and the chin's width, shape and projection (they are the one set of handles that drive other features' parameters). The nose has handles for its root, hump, the sides of its bridge and of its tip, and the columella; a nostril's handle also moves it up and down and, in profile, flares it. The cheeks (cheekbone, cheek, hollow), the jawline, the chin and its corners, the forehead and temples, the skin under each eye and each eye's inner corner have handles of their own. The sliders added with them: bridge hump, root and width, tip width, columella, nostril flare and height; inner-corner fold, under-eye fullness, upper-lid fullness and eye openness (which only narrows the eye, from -1 to 0: a blink is a shape added to the face and can't yet follow a widened opening); cheek hollow and cheekbone height; jawline definition, chin shape and chin cleft; cupid's bow, lip protrusion (the mouth with the teeth behind it) and mouth-corner height. The library's `feature` presets set one feature and leave the rest of the face (architecture §15a): ten noses (Straight, Aquiline, Hooked, Snub, Button, Upturned, Bulbous, Long and thin, Wide, Low bridge) and six face shapes (Oval, which is the face as built, Round, Square, Heart-shaped, Long, Narrow). **Hair** and **Clothing** are browsers (refinement R6 and R7, CO25 and CO26; brief §7.1, §8.2; `browser.rs`, `panel/browser.rs`, `panel/worn.rs`). **What is offered**: only items for the character's base model, in a slot of the page's category that a person picks in, every slot they `also_fill` one too; a slot a choice fills (the realistic body's underwear) isn't offered and one muted line pinned under the tiles says what the body type wears there ("Underwear: Boxer briefs, which comes with the body type."; `browser::body_type_wears`). With nothing offered at all the page is a short empty state ("No hairstyles yet"); with only drafts, it lists them (below). The realistic body's items so far are its underwear (`assets/characters/realistic/underwear.py`), the first hairstyle (the short textured crop, R6, `assets/characters/realistic/hair/`) and the first top, bottom and shoes (R7, `assets/characters/realistic/clothing/`), each a draft (`quality: "draft"`) until it passes `asset-quality.md` and is signed off. At the top, a **search** (every word against the label, ID, tags and `about`) with a **Filters** menu beside it, then the page's slots as one row of **categories** (All, Top, Outerwear, Bottoms, Shoes; Hair has one slot, so none). The menu holds **Fits this body** (on by default: hides an item with a `limitations` range on a base parameter the character is outside; off, the tile's tooltip says "May not fit this body" and why), Recolourable (a material slot with a colour role, or variants), Favourites and Kind (one of the listed items' tags); it stays open while they are picked, has **Reset filters**, and its button says how many differ from what the page opens with ("Filters · 2"; the search and the category aren't counted). In it too, when any garment says who it is cut for (the tags `masculine`, `feminine`, `unisex`), **Cut for** (`browser::Cut`, Paul 2026-11-24): **This body** (the default: the garments for the character's own frame, with the unisex ones and the ones that don't say), **Masculine**, **Feminine** or **All**; asked for another frame's garments, a limit on `frame` is no misfit, and those tags are left out of Kind. The online wardrobe's list is narrowed the same way (there the menu holds Cut for only). **Drafts** (`asset-quality.md` §5; an item whose `quality` is `draft`: every realistic item today) are marked **Draft** on their tiles. A page with no finished item lists its drafts without asking, so it never opens on "Nothing to show"; a page with finished items hides them until **Show drafts** is ticked, and has the box only then, saying how many ("Show drafts (2 hidden)") (`Listing::finished`, `drafts`, `hidden_drafts`). A draft that is worn is always listed. Randomize never draws a draft, whatever a page lists. Then **Wearing**, above the tiles so it is seen without scrolling: a line for each worn item of the page (its name, its slot, "Draft", and a ✕ that takes it off as one undo step; a click on the line unfolds the item's own controls where it has any: `Browse::Unfold`, `Browser::unfolded`, editor state, folded at first), then **Recently used** (the items last worn from a browser, not the worn ones, only on the unfiltered list) and the **tiles**, by slot, favourites first, each slot's empty choice ("Bald", "None") first, as many across as fit the panel at about 120 points each (`panel::tile_columns`: two at 320, three at the default 400, five at 640; the online wardrobe's and the Presets page's tiles too). A tile is a picture of the character being edited wearing the item (the thumbnails painter on its thread, cached as `thumbnails/items/<id>.<key>.png` and drawn again when the character changes, not during a drag; a hairstyle framed on the head and shoulders, `Subject::Hair`, `hair_orbit`; clothes on the whole body), its label, a star for favourites, a **Draft** badge, an accent border and **Worn** when it is worn (the empty choice of a slot with nothing in it is lit without the badge), and a tooltip with its `about`, slot, fit warning and what wearing it takes off (`browser::dressed`: what was in its slots and `also_fills`, anything it `excludes` or that excludes it; what it `requires` goes on). **A click wears, a hover previews** (Paul, 2026-11-24: a tile picked to try on and never worn went at the next edit or page change, which read as the clothes vanishing when the body was changed). A click on a tile wears it (`Intent::Wear`, one undo step, first in Recently used); a click on the worn tile takes the item off, as does a click on its slot's empty choice (one undo step). There is no "Trying on" bar, no Wear button, no picked tile and no double click. Hovering a tile shows the character wearing it while the pointer is there: no undo step and no change to the description (`compare.rs`'s `shown` returns the preview). Esc, moving off the tile, leaving the page, turning drafts off (for a draft) or any change of the description (an edit, undo or redo, keeping a held version, Randomize: the workspace's `edit`, `transact`, `undo`, `redo` and `cancel_edit` end it) put the character back. After a click (`Browse::Clicked`), the ✕ on a worn item's line, or Esc, the tile under the pointer is muted until the pointer moves off it (`Browser::muted`): a still pointer never brings a preview back, and should the tiles shift under it (a worn item's line comes or goes), the tile that comes under it isn't previewed until the pointer moves. A worn garment stays on through every change of the body: the same part on the body, wearing each new identity as the body does, while a slider or a handle is dragged and after it. **The worn item's controls**, unfolded from its line, are only what it declares (`browser::Declared`): its **variants** as chips ("As made" first; picking one gives back the colours and values it sets); for a hairstyle, **natural colours** (nine swatches, Black to Red, setting `melanin` and `redness` together and clearing a tint, one undo step) for its colour (the slot with role `primary`) and, only where the style has such slots, its **highlights** (role `secondary`) and **roots** (role `accent`), each with a tint button where the slot offers `tint`; for a garment a colour button per **colour role** (Primary, Secondary, Accent colour: the slots of that role, their `tint`); and its own **parameters** as slider rows at the page's level (the shared row, `panel/row.rs`, so the drag rule lands there for every page). A material slot with no role is no control: a style that declares no highlights shows no highlight control. The intents are `Browse` (filters, Show drafts, the tile hovered, a click's mute, a worn item's fold: editor state), `EndPreview`, `ToggleItemFavourite`, and the edits `SetItemVariant`, `SetItemMaterial` (several slots and values at once) and `SetItemParameter`. **Accessories** is still the earlier list: the manifest's slots, each a row with the items that fit and the slot's empty choice (R8 makes it a browser). The realistic body's items so far are its underwear (`assets/characters/realistic/underwear.py`); its hair and clothes are CO10 and CO11. The Face page's Brows group has brow height, arch, length (the brows' card drawn in or carried down the temple) and ridge. **Skin and Makeup**: every value of the base's skin-shaded materials (the realistic body's `Skin` slot: tint as a colour button; melanin, redness, undertone, roughness scale, detail strength and scatter scale as sliders), written to `description.materials` (a value at its neutral setting is left out). Since R5 the renderer draws every one of them (the Skin shading model, `docs/rendering/skin.md`). The page also lists `definition` and `softness`, which the realistic manifest drives from muscle and fat: a value set here overrides the body's. Under them, **Makeup and markings** (R5, ADR-0042; `panel/materials.rs`, `makeup`) lists the base's `skin_layers`, each with a check box; on, a makeup layer shows its colour (a colour button; "own tone" for foundation without one, with a reset to the layer's own colour), opacity and roughness, and a marking its opacity, amount and **New pattern** (the next seed). Each change is `Intent::SetSkinLayer` with the whole layer: one undo step, or one per slider drag; off keeps the layer's settings in the description. Reset of the Skin category clears them with the material values. **Expressions** (R9, brief §10; `expressions.rs`, `panel/expressions.rs`): one line saying the page is a preview only; **Pose and motion** chips (above); the **Face preview** switch; the 15 built-in expressions as tiles, three a row (Neutral first, lit when nothing is shown: it puts the face back); and the **face controls** in six folding sections, Brows (raise, lower, inner ends up), Eyes (blink, squint, wide, look down or up, look sideways), Cheeks (raise, puff), Nose (sneer), Mouth (smile, frown, open lips, wide, narrow, pucker, upper lip up, lower lip down, corners back, sideways) and Jaw (open, forward, sideways, tongue out). Left and right move together; a sideways or up-and-down row is one slider from -1 to 1 over two opposite controls. What the page shows is a FaceControls of the preview's own on the body ("Studio Expression Preview", as the capture tool does): the expression plays on its Expression layer and a moved control is held as a gameplay override (`FaceState::overrides`, the Override layer), so a slider shows exactly the value it says; a slider not moved shows what the expression gives its control, and its reset (or a double click) goes back to that. Picking an expression starts the controls again from its values. The sliders are the panel's shared slider rows, so they follow the studio's drag rule with the body and face sliders (see Numeric controls). **Face preview**: the camera straight on and close (2 studs, aimed 0.06 studs over the head bone as posed), the body Still, the face's blinking and idle motion off, the **Neutral** light (a lighting preset of the face preview's own, not in the bottom bar: soft shadows, more and greyer fill), the expression held; **Blink** and **Idle motion** (the face's and the body's Idle) each turn back on; switching it off, or leaving the page, puts back the camera, view, light and motion it found. All of it is editor state (`Intent::PreviewExpression`, `PreviewFaceControl`, `FacePreview`, `FacePreviewMotion`): never in the description, never an undo step (a test saves before and after a session on the page and compares the files byte for byte), and leaving the page takes the expression, the controls and the face preview off, as do New and Open (`start_over` leaves the page first, so the face preview puts back the light, camera and motion it found before the other character is shown).
- **The online wardrobe** (`wardrobe.rs`, drawn by `panel/wardrobe.rs`; the pack's reader and its downloads are `toolbox::character_pack`). The Clothing page has a switch above its search: **Your library** (the browser above) or **Online wardrobe**, which shows in the tiles' place what the [Axcenity Wardrobe](https://github.com/Axcenity-Studios/Axcenity-Wardrobe) has for the character's body (`Browse::Online`, editor state). The wardrobe is a character pack: a repository with a `pack.json` index (format `axcenity-character-pack`, version 1), read from the repository's own files. **Nothing is fetched until asked**: **Show the online wardrobe** asks for the list on a thread; the answer is kept in the studio's folder (`wardrobe/pack.json`, pictures in `wardrobe/previews/<version>/`), so the next session shows the kept list at once ("As listed the last time you asked.") and fetches nothing for it until the refresh button asks again. Under the pack's name and version: how many garments it has for this body, its licence in plain words, **Copy credit line** (the pack's `credit`) and **Download all N (size)**. Each tile is the garment's picture (the pack's own, cut to the tile's shape), its label, a **Draft** or **In library** badge and one button: its size (download it), **Update** (the library's copy isn't the files the index lists now), **Waiting**, its progress, **Remove**, or a greyed **Yours** (the library has an item of its own with that ID). The page's search and category chips narrow these tiles too.
  - **A download** (`Intent::Wardrobe` with `Ask::List`, `Get`, `GetAll`, `Cancel` or `Remove`) is one garment at a time, in the order asked, on a thread, and goes on while another page is open. Its files come into `wardrobe/staging/<item>/`, each through a `.part` file checked against the size and SHA-256 the index lists; only a whole, checked item is moved into the library, at the path the pack gives (`realistic/imports/<name>/`), and listed in `catalogue.json` with the entry its manifest makes (`wardrobe::install`; the catalogue is written whole, through a `.part`). A progress bar shows it, with **Cancel**: that stops it and forgets the ones waiting, and keeps the files that finished, so asking again goes on from them. A failure says why in the status line, leaves the library as it was and drops the ones waiting. When a garment is in, the status line says so and **Show drafts** is turned on if it is a draft (every garment of the wardrobe is one today), so what was asked for shows under Your library, where it is previewed and worn like any item. A download is never an undo step and never in the character.
  - **What is refused.** An index that isn't a character pack, or is a newer format; files anywhere but under the wardrobe's own address; an item with a path that isn't plain names, a file outside its own folder, a kind of file other than `.glb`, `.json` and `.png`, or no manifest among its files (left out and counted: "N items left out"). At install: a manifest that doesn't read or is another item's, a body the library hasn't, an ID the library has at another path (the engine's own item, or a person's) and a folder that holds anything else the catalogue lists. So a pack can add garments and replace its own, and nothing else.
  - **Remove** takes the garment's folder and its entry out again; it is refused while the character wears it. **Done** (the round trip below) gives a game's project the garments its character wears, as it does every item of the studio's library.
  - **Not built:** the wardrobe's outfits (`outfits/` in the pack), packs other than the Axcenity Wardrobe, a clothing kind in the Game Editor's Toolbox tab, and the pack's credit line in Build Game's `CREDITS.txt` (the licence asks for credit: Copy credit line is the way for now).
- **Undo** is the workspace's own (`edit.rs`): each change of the description is a step; a drag is one transaction (Esc cancels it back to where it started), and a change of nothing (a slider clicked where it is, the name field visited) makes no step and keeps redo. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z redo (redo is matched first: egui ignores an extra Shift). Reset offers Everything (the body's default character, keeping the name), the open category (its sliders, the items of its slots, for Skin its material values; the body type stays) and the picked feature, each one step. **Save** (Ctrl+S) writes `characters/<name>.character.json`; another character's file is never written over: the character is saved under the first free "<name> 2" and the status line says so. After a rename the old file is moved, with its Recent entry, pictures and pack places, not left beside the new one. **New** (a family or a preset) and **Open** (a saved character) ask Save, Don't Save or Cancel when there are unsaved changes, as Home does; so does closing the editor's window (the character is in the editor's list of unsaved documents).
- **The character file** is the engine-wide description, format 2 ([architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) §5): `name`, `base`, `parameters` by the manifest's IDs, `items`, `materials`. A format 1 file (CS2's `Look`: build, muscle and a part per slot) opens on the stylized body wearing what it wore and saves as format 2. Values out of a parameter's range are brought in and parameters the body hasn't are dropped; a file on a body the library hasn't opens the default character with a notice; a newer format is refused with a message.

## The start page

Code: `start/characters.rs` (the page and what its buttons do) with `start/characters/cards.rs` (the cards) and `dialogs.rs` (the pack editor, the questions before deleting); the files behind it are `character_workspace/shelf.rs` (listing, overview, delete), `recent.rs`, `packs.rs` and `thumbnails.rs`.

- **From the top:** New Character (the realistic default character); **Recent**, the characters last opened or saved, the first four as cards and "Show N more" / "Show less" for the rest; **Your characters**, every saved one, newest first; **Packs**, with New Pack… and Import Pack…; and **Start from a preset** (each family's whole-character presets, realistic first, and the stylized family's default character). The files are listed again every 3 s while the page shows. Cards are at least 150 points wide and a row fills the page (two columns in a narrow window, where New Character goes under the title). The presets use as few columns as keep the same number of rows (`balanced_columns`), so nine presets are rows of five and four rather than seven and two.
- **A card** shows the character's picture, its name and an overview (`shelf::overview`): the body type (each parameter with choices, such as Male or Female), the base model's label and how many other values are set, then the hair (or the hair slot's empty label, "Bald") and what it wears (the clothes, or the underwear its body type wears when that's all). The overview is of the character as shown, with what its body type wears put on. A saved character's card has **Edit** (opens it in the workspace; clicking the picture does too) and **Delete…** (a trash button), which asks first in the editor's standard dialog and then removes the file, its pictures, its Recent entry and its place in every pack (`shelf::delete_character`). Presets are read-only: their button is **Start from this**.
- **Recent** is `recent.json` in the Character Studio folder: `{format_version: 1, characters: [file names in characters/]}`, newest first, at most 24. Opening a saved file (`CharacterWorkspace::open_file`) and saving (`save`) put it first; files outside `characters/` aren't tracked; a file that's gone drops out when it's listed; Delete takes it out. It's per user, like the folder.
- **Pictures** (`thumbnails.rs`) are the workspace's own preview drawn offscreen: a workspace of their own (the default character with the description applied by the applier, idled ten frames, Studio lighting, Grey backdrop, a full-body camera a little turned) drawn by the scene renderer into a 360 × 450 texture. They're cached as PNGs in `thumbnails/characters/<file>.<key>.png` and `thumbnails/presets/<preset ID>.<key>.png` (the ID with `/` as `-`), the key a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of what's drawn (the file's text, or the character the preset makes and its framing), the picture's version and the engine's. A changed file has a new key and is drawn again; writing a picture removes the older ones of the same file. Missing ones are drawn on a thread of their own ("character pictures") on the editor's GPU device (`StartScreen::set_gpu`, set each frame from the render state), one at a time, while the card shows a placeholder with the initials and a spinner; the page reads at most four from disk a frame. The thread stops after 20 s with nothing to do, and a picture that fails twice is given up on for the session. A save in the workspace (`CharacterWorkspace::saved_files`, drained by the app, and the Save of "Save changes?") asks for its fresh picture at once. The workspace and the picture thread both write the library into the folder; a lock (`WRITING_LIBRARY`) keeps them apart. Until the workspace has opened once, overviews use the library the engine ships (`shelf::library_for`).
- **Packs** (`packs.rs`) are a named set of saved characters, `packs/<name>.pack.json`: `{format_version: 1, name, characters: [file names in characters/]}`. **New Pack…** and a pack's **Edit…** open the pack editor (a name and a checklist of the saved characters with their pictures; Make Pack / Save Pack); a new name renames the pack (refused if another pack has it). A pack's card shows its name, how many characters, a trash button (Delete asks; the characters are kept), their pictures (then "+n"), **Edit…**, **Export…** and **Add to Project…**. A deleted character leaves its packs; a missing one is skipped.
- **Export** writes one plain JSON file, `<name>.characterpack.json`: `{format_version: 1, kind: "axcenity.character-pack", name, characters: [whole descriptions, format 2]}`, so it stands alone. **Import** checks `kind` and refuses a newer format, saves each character into `characters/` (one already there with the same contents is used; a different one with the same name becomes "Name 2", renamed inside too) and makes the pack (its name gets a number if taken).
- **Add to Project…** picks a game project's folder and writes the pack's characters to `assets/characters/descriptions/<name>.character.json` (replacing ones of the same name), beside the project's character library ([architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) §15), and writes the built-in library and the engine's packages into the project (`add_character_library`, `add_character_packages`) so the bodies and items they wear are there. A game applies one by setting a `HumanoidDescription`'s `Data` to the file's text.

## From the Game Editor and back (refinement R10)

- **Edit in Character Studio** is on the Explorer's right-click menu of a character, its body or one of its parts (`character_workspace/game.rs`'s `editable_character`: a Model with a Humanoid and a skinned body that keeps a description, or that draws one of the engine's character models, so an imported character isn't offered; Done would put the engine's body in place of its own). The studio opens on that character's description (`game_description`: the kept one, or, for a character made before descriptions, the stylized default character named after it) in place of the Game Editor's panels; the Game Editor's menus, toolbar and keys are left alone while it's open (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+S are the studio's: they used to be taken by the Game Editor's shortcuts first, which also left the studio's own keys dead with no project open). The title is "<character> - Character Studio - <project>".
- **Cancel** (in Home's place) goes back with the character as it was. **Done** (beside Save) gives the project's library what the character wears (the studio's library: both families and a person's own items, `add_character_library` and `add_character_packages`), applies the edited description to the character with the Character Runtime's applier and records it as **one undo step**, "Edit <character> in Character Studio". Play shows it, since the description is kept in the character. If it can't be applied the studio stays open and says why. **Save** still saves a copy into the studio's characters. While a game's character is open the studio's autosave is off and its recovery file is left alone (a crash's file stays for the studio opened on its own).
- **Insert > Character** places the **realistic** default character (plan section 0, decision 7; OPEN-22), applied at once in the same undo step and named like the instance; the stylized family stays selectable in the studio. It also gives the project both families (`project::add_default_game_character`; an older project keeps its `Character` prefab and its characters). New projects from the character templates start with the same realistic `Character` prefab ([architecture.md](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) §15).
- **Edit mode shows described characters** (OPEN-21, `app/edit_characters.rs`): each edit frame the editor applies the kept descriptions this run hasn't applied (the Character Runtime's `DescriptionSync`, with no game system running) and poses the described characters **at rest** (the A-pose, the studio's Still) with their identity worn live, so a saved character shows its body shape, face, items and materials, not its base meshes, until Play. Posing runs again only when one of them changes (or a model is still loading). For a world saved after its description was applied this changes nothing in the world. A recorded step that puts a character back as it was before edit mode showed it (an undo to a whole-world snapshot, Revert all, a version restore) has the same `Base` and `Data`, so edit mode keeps what each character showed (its body, the body's model and identity, its parts) and applies it again when that changed (`DescriptionSync::forget`). Changing what the applier owns by hand (the body's `MeshId`, a description part) doesn't stick while the character keeps a description, as in Play.
- **Placed prefab copies of described characters** (the project's `Character` prefab placed as an NPC, say): a prefab file keeps its described character unapplied (Play applies it where it spawns), so what edit mode derives in a copy would show as the person's overrides. Edit mode instead shows the copy's **source** the same way, applied and posed at rest in memory (never written to the file), and sets it on the copy's prefab link, unrecorded. A copy nobody changed has no overrides (at most placement): Properties marks no body property, the Prefab menu offers nothing to apply or revert, Revert all puts back the shown prefab, and saving writes only the person's changes. Apply to prefab writes the copy as shown, so the prefab file then holds the applied character (as a prefab made from a shown character does).

## The tab

Character Studio's tab is a document tab beside the Scene (View > Character Studio, the ribbon's Panels menu, or Ctrl+K "Character Studio"). It shows the selected character's face in a preview of its own, with the **Face** workspace (facial animation F6, decision D9 in [research/facial-animation.md](../research/facial-animation.md)). The default-humanoid work adds its body and customization workspaces to the same tab.

Code: `crates/editor/src/character_studio.rs` (state, preview, the tab) with `character_studio/rig.rs`, `expressions.rs` and `lip_sync.rs` (one per section); the editor's side, which applies what the tab asks for, is `app/character_studio.rs`.

## What it shows

- It follows the selection: a FaceControls in a MeshPart, a MeshPart (with its FaceControls if it has one), or a Model or folder with a FaceControls in a MeshPart inside (`face_target`). With something else selected it keeps the last character.
- The preview draws a copy of the MeshPart alone, at the origin and unturned, posed at rest with its face set by `anim::pose_face` (blend shape weights and eye and jaw turns through the matched profile). The camera looks at the head bone (`anim::face_point`; a model without bones is framed on its top) from the way the body faces. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, double-click to frame again.
- What the face shows depends on the section: the FaceControls' own values (Rig), the expression (Expressions), or the visemes at the play head (Lip sync).

## Rig

- **Profile**: the FaceControls' `Profile` (Best fit, a built-in one, or one of the project's `*.face.json`), set as one undo step. The line under it names the profile matched and how many of the 48 controls move something on this model.
- **Check**: what doesn't fit (`anim::check_face`), errors first; after six, the rest fold.
- **Controls**: the FaceControls' 48 controls as sliders, grouped (eyes, eyebrows, mouth and lips, cheeks and nose, jaw and tongue), with a filter. A slider is an edit like Properties' (one undo step per drag). Controls that move nothing on the model are greyed. Without a FaceControls the sliders are off; expressions and lip syncs still preview.
- **Key in Animation** selects the FaceControls and opens the Animation tab, where its controls are rows to key in a clip (F4b). That is the face's timeline: keys, curves and playback are the Animation tab's.
- **Profile mapping** (folded at first): Edit mapping copies the matched profile, keeping only the shapes the model has. Each of the model's blend shapes can be given to one control with a weight (`map_shape`: a shape belongs to one control at a time). The Jaw, LeftEye and RightEye bones can be found by name, set to none, or named; the eye and jaw limits are in degrees. Save profile writes `assets/faces/<name>.face.json` (one undo step) and makes it the FaceControls' `Profile` (another). The preview and the check follow the draft while it's edited.

## Expressions

- The project's expressions, then the built-in ones it doesn't replace (`anim::expression_names`); built-in ones are greyed, and saving one makes the project's own copy.
- **Preview at** scales it; **Blend with** another expression mixes the two by **Blend amount** (preview only).
- The expression's own intensity, transition and the controls it sets (add, change, take out). Save expression writes `assets/faces/expressions/<name>.expression.json` (one undo step); a game plays it with `face:SetExpression("<name>")`.

## Lip sync

- Open any `*.lipsync.json` in the project (hidden folders and `target`, `build` and `node_modules` are skipped; the arrows list the files again).
- **Make from** makes one beside a sound (`.ogg`, `.wav`, `.mp3`, `.flac`: from how it sounds) or phoneme timings (`.lab`, `.tsv`, `.txt`: `start end phoneme` per line), as the Console's `lipsync` does, saves it (one undo step) and opens it.
- The timeline shows the lip sync's sound as a waveform over its length, and its visemes as blocks, each until the next. Click the waveform to move the play head; click a block to pick it, drag it to retime it (kept between its neighbours, `retime`); double-click to add a viseme there (`add_key`). The picked viseme's row changes which viseme it is, its width (weight) and its time, or deletes it; Add at play head adds one.
- Play plays the visemes on the face through the FaceControls' `VisemeMap`, `LipSyncSmoothing` and `LipSyncAnticipation` (or the defaults), as a game would. The sound doesn't play in the tab.
- Length changes the lip sync's duration. Save writes it (one undo step); "Not saved" shows until then.
