## Anatomy

The outer ear (auricle) is a single sheet of springy cartilage under tight skin, with a soft lobe that has no cartilage. Its outer rim (helix) curls from above the canal round the top and down the back to the lobe. Inside the rim a second fold (antihelix) forks near the top. Between them and the canal lies the bowl (concha). The small flap in front of the canal is the tragus; tragion is the notch just above it. The ear attaches along its front edge and stands off the head behind, leaning back a little from the vertical. By the artists' rule of thumb its top sits near brow level and its base near the base of the nose. The lobe either hangs free or joins the cheek directly. Ears keep growing through adult life, mostly in length.
