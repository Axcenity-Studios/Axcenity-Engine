## Anatomy

The eyeball sits in the orbit, cushioned in fat, with only part of its front showing between the lids. The upper lid is the larger and does nearly all the moving; a stiff plate inside it holds its curve, and a muscle lifts it by folding the skin back under the brow, which makes the lid crease. The lower lid is shorter and barely moves. The lids meet at the inner corner (endocanthion), which holds a small pink mound and may be covered by a fold of skin from the upper lid, and at the outer corner (exocanthion), which is sharp and usually sits a little higher. At rest the upper lid covers about the top 2 mm of the iris (its edge sits 3.5 mm above the pupil's centre, and the iris is 5.9 mm in radius) and the lower lid sits at or just over its lower edge. The visible iris is the cornea seen from the front, about 11.8 mm across. The pupil's size is set by light, not by the person. Under the eye, the lid's thin skin meets the thicker cheek at a groove that deepens with age.
