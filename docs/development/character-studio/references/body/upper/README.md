## Region by region

What gives each region its outline, where fat sits, where bone shows, and what that means for its controls.

| Region | Muscle that shapes it | Where fat sits | Bone at the surface (no fat) | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neck | Sternocleidomastoid from behind the ear to the collarbone's inner end; trapezius behind | Under the chin and at the nape | Larynx, the pit between the collarbones | Neck girth follows weight weakly (exponent 0.3). A thick neck on a thin body is muscle, not fat |
| Shoulders | Deltoid: the cap. Trapezius: the slope from neck to shoulder | Little | Collarbone, the shoulder's point (acromion), the shoulder blade's spine | Bone width (biacromial) hardly follows weight (exponent 0.16); the widest point (bideltoid) does (r = 0.88 / 0.86). So "shoulder width" is two things: bone and deltoid |
| Chest | Pectoralis major, a fan from collarbone and breastbone to the arm; it makes the front fold of the armpit | Lower and outer chest; the breast sits on the muscle | Breastbone, the collarbones, lower ribs on a lean body | Depth varies more than breadth (SD 10% against 6%). Rib cage and soft tissue are separate controls |
| Upper arms | Biceps in front, triceps behind (the larger mass) | The back of the arm, over the triceps | The elbow's point and its two side knobs | Biceps and forearm girth move together (r = 0.88 / 0.85) |
| Forearms | Bellies near the elbow, tendons at the wrist: it tapers | Little | The wrist's two knobs, the ulna's edge | A forearm thicker at the wrist than a third of the way up is wrong at any setting |
| Hands | Thumb and little-finger pads | Almost none; the back of a heavy hand softens | Knuckles, tendons on the back | Hand breadth ÷ length 0.46 men, 0.43 women: the frame should change breadth, not only size |
| Abdomen | Rectus abdominis in bands down the middle; obliques at the sides | Men: the whole front, pushed out from inside (visceral fat is 6-20% of body fat and higher in men, B12). Women: lower belly, below the navel | Lower ribs' edge, hip-bone points on a lean body | A heavy belly gains depth before breadth (exponents 0.73 and 0.56) |
| Waist | Obliques | Over the hip-bone's crest at the sides and back (flanks) | None between ribs and pelvis | The waist is all soft tissue: its width cannot be a bone |
| Back | Latissimus dorsi (the V), the two columns beside the spine, trapezius above | Under the shoulder blades and over the lower back (B12 names the subscapular depot) | Shoulder blades, the spine's knobs at the neck's base | Fat fills the spine's furrow last; a heavy back still has it |

## Poses to check

The body has three shoulder correctives per side (level 0 to 45 degrees of raise, raised 45 to 105, lowered 0 to 35) made for a sideways raise with the collarbone still, and none at the elbow (`assets/characters/realistic/README.md:155-159`, `docs/development/character-studio/co5/README.md:87`).

| Pose | What happens in a real body | What to look for | Number to hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arms lowered | The deltoid hangs as a rounded cap; the trapezius slopes down to it | One smooth slope from neck to arm; no squared cap; no ridge beside the neck; the armpit closed without the arm sinking into the chest | Shoulder's local volume within a few percent of rest (2.4 / -1.4% today) |
| Arms out to the side, 90 degrees | The deltoid bunches over the joint; the armpit opens; its front wall is the pectoral, its back wall the latissimus | Two clear folds with a hollow between them; the collarbone still reads | |
| Arms overhead | The shoulder blade turns upward and the collarbone rises: the joint alone cannot do it (U05). The deltoid moves up beside the neck; the rib cage and the serratus show; the waist lengthens | No collapse of the cap; the armpit a hollow, not a crease; the neck not swallowed. Raise the arm forward as well as sideways: the correctives cover sideways only | Shoulder flexion 169 to 172 degrees (U04). Local volume kept at 150 degrees: target 0.8, today 0.46 / 0.51 (`co5/README.md:23`) |
| Elbow bent 90 degrees | The biceps shortens and rises; the elbow's point stands out behind; a crease forms in front | The point stays sharp (it is bone); the inside does not pinch through; the forearm keeps its thickness at the crease | |
| Elbow fully bent | Forearm flesh presses against the biceps and both flatten | No interpenetration; no "rubber hose" bend | Elbow flexion 145 to 150 degrees (U04) |
| Forearm turned | The radius crosses over the ulna; the wrist turns, the elbow does not | The twist spread along the forearm, not wrung at the wrist | Pronation 77 to 82, supination 85 to 91 degrees (U04) |
| Any of these at the build's extremes | Fat softens folds; muscle sharpens them | Heavy and soft, heavy and muscular, slight: the three bodies the keep-apart pass cut most at the armpits (`Realistic.build.json`, `kept_apart`) | No new crossings in pose (`co5/posed_crossings.json`) |

Photo sets to look for (links only, B20 and B21): the same person with arms down, out and overhead from front, back and three-quarter; bent elbows from the inside and the outside; ordinary, lean, heavy and muscular builds, not only bodybuilders.
