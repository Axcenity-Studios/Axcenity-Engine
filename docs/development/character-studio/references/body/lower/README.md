Women's hips are wider than men's in absolute terms though women are shorter (hip breadth ÷ stature 0.217 against 0.197), and more of that width is soft tissue (hip breadth ÷ pelvis bone 1.30 against 1.26). Thigh girth is the same in both (625 and 616 mm) on legs 7% shorter.

## Region by region

| Region | Muscle that shapes it | Where fat sits | Bone at the surface (no fat) | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hips | Gluteus maximus makes the buttock; gluteus medius the upper side | Women: buttocks, hips and thighs first (B12). Men: less, and later than the belly | The hip bone's crest and its two front points; the tailbone | The widest point is at the thigh bone's outer knob, below the pelvis' crest. Buttock depth is about 0.7 of hip breadth: a hip control must add depth too |
| Thighs | Quadriceps in front, with a teardrop above the knee on the inside; hamstrings behind; adductors fill the inside from groin to knee | Outer and inner thigh, more in women | The knee | The thigh bone slants inward to the knee (L02), more in women. Thigh and buttock girth move as one (r = 0.94) |
| Knees | Tendons only | A pad below the kneecap; the inner knee on a heavy body | Kneecap, the knob below it, the thigh bone's two ends | The knee is the narrowest, boniest part of the leg at every build; fat shapes are held off it (`shapes.bony`) |
| Calves | Gastrocnemius in two heads, the inner one lower and fuller; soleus beneath, showing at the sides | Evenly, little | The shin's front edge and inner face; both ankle knobs | The calf's bulge sits in the upper half and tapers to the tendon. Calf ÷ ankle girth 1.7 |
| Feet | Pads of heel and ball | Almost none | Ankle knobs (the inner one higher), the instep's bones, toes | Foot breadth ÷ length 0.38 in both sexes. Foot ÷ hand length 1.40 men, 1.36 women |

## Poses to check

The rig has hip and knee helper bones and thigh and shin twist bones, and no corrective shapes at the hip or knee (`docs/development/character-studio/co5/README.md:87`).

| Pose | What happens in a real body | What to look for | Number to hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standing | Knees almost straight; the kneecap sits high and loose; the buttock is rounded with a fold under it | The fold under the buttock; a clear knee, not a tube; the inner ankle knob higher than the outer | |
| Knee bent 90 degrees | The kneecap stays on the thigh bone's end, so the knee's front squares off; the hamstring tendons stand out behind and a hollow opens between them | A boxy knee from the side, not a smooth arc; no dent across the front; the calf keeps its volume | |
| Knee fully bent (squat, kneel) | Calf presses into the back of the thigh and both flatten; the quadriceps stretch thin over the front | No interpenetration at the back; the thigh not ballooning sideways; the knee's point still bone | Knee flexion 138 to 142 degrees (L04) |
| Hip bent 90 degrees (sitting, high step) | The buttock stretches and flattens; a deep crease forms at the groin; the belly folds over it on a heavy body | The crease at the groin without the thigh passing into the belly; the buttock keeping volume | Hip flexion to 130 to 134 degrees (L04) |
| Leg behind (the back of a stride) | The hip extends only a little; the rest is the pelvis turning | The buttock bunching, not the thigh sliding into it | Hip extension 17 to 18 degrees (L04) |
| Walking | 60% stance, 40% swing; the pelvis rotates and tilts; the knee is nearly straight at heel strike and bends about 60 degrees in swing (O06 for the phases) | Thighs brushing but not crossing on heavy bodies; the crotch clean in mid stride; the waist and hips not shearing as the pelvis turns | No new crossings in the walk at heavy and soft, heavy and muscular |
| On the toes | The calf shortens into a ball high on the leg; the tendon stands out | The calf's bulge moving up, the ankle staying thin | Ankle 55 to 62 degrees down, 13 to 14 up (L04) |
| Any of these at the build's extremes | Fat softens and deepens folds; thin bodies show tendon and bone | Heavy thighs with wide hips; slight bodies at the knee | The keep-apart pass checks the rest pose only (`assets/characters/realistic/shapes.py:39-41`), so pose these bodies |

The swing-phase knee bend of about 60 degrees is common knowledge in gait analysis and was not read on a source today; treat it as a guide.

Photo sets to look for (links only, B20): the same person standing, mid stride, seated, squatting and kneeling from front, side and back; legs of ordinary, lean, heavy and muscular builds; feet from above, inside and outside.
