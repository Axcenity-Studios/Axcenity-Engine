Read these with two cautions:

- **Soldiers are not the public.** They are younger and fitter, and the survey excludes people the Army would not accept. NHANES (B04) is the public: use it for the heavy end.
- **Definitions matter.** ANSUR takes the waist at the navel; NHANES at the top of the hip bone. ANSUR's bideltoid breadth is with arms hanging; the build stands in a 45 degree A-pose, so its widest point at the shoulders is not the same measure.

## Heads tall

- ANSUR 1988 (B03), menton to top of head: men 5th / 50th / 95th percentile 217.6 / 231.6 / 246.9 mm (mean 232.0, SD 8.8); women 203.6 / 217.6 / 231.5 mm (mean 217.6, SD 8.5). Read from a scanned table (measure H30, printed pages 526-527).
- At the median stature that is 7.6 heads for men and 7.5 for women. Drillis and Contini give 0.130 of stature, 7.7 heads (B06).
- Head size barely follows height (growth exponent 0.25 to 0.35 below), so tall people are more heads tall and short people fewer. With the two spreads and a weak correlation, the population runs from about 6.8 to 8.4 heads (1st to 99th percentile; an estimate, not a measured distribution).
- Artists' canons (O01): 7.5 average, 8 ideal, 8.5 heroic. Use them as the ends of the basic range, not as targets: 8 heads is already the tall, small-headed tail of real people.

What this means for the sliders:

- Weight goes to the waist first (depth before breadth), then thighs and arms, and least to wrists, ankles and the bony shoulder width. A fat control that thickens wrists and ankles as much as the belly is wrong; `shapes.bony` already holds those places back.
- `height` moves stature by about 0.75 of its own change (it scales 1.31 m of 1.75 m). To keep a body in proportion as `height` changes by d: `arm_length` by 0.8 d, `hand_size` and `foot_size` by 0.65 d, `shoulder_width` by 0.55 d, `head_size` by 0.2 d. These are the `follow` factors proposed in [../README.md](../README.md) section 7.
- Legs grow faster than the trunk with height. `height` scales both alike, so a very tall body has a slightly long trunk; `leg_length` up by a few percent corrects it. Not worth a rule.

Correlations worth knowing (men / women): stature with crotch height 0.86 / 0.85, with span 0.82 / 0.82, with foot length 0.72 / 0.72, with biacromial breadth 0.53 / 0.57, with hip breadth 0.38 / 0.39, with head circumference 0.34 / 0.31, with weight 0.47 / 0.53. Biacromial with hip breadth only 0.47 / 0.30: shoulder and hip width are close to independent, which is why "broad" needs both sliders.

## What to look for, by view

| View | Look for | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Front | Shoulder to hip ratio for the frame (bideltoid ÷ hip breadth about 1.5 for men, 1.3 for women). The waist is the narrowest point between ribs and pelvis and sits near the navel. Legs are half the height: the crotch at 0.48 of stature | A waist that narrows like a belt because a bone was scaled without depth |
| Back | The slope from neck to shoulder (trapezius), the shoulder blades, the spine's furrow, two dimples over the pelvis on a lean body, the fold under each buttock | A flat back with no furrow at high fat; a ridge beside the neck with arms down |
| Side | The S of the spine; the chest's depth against the belly's; buttock depth about 0.7 of hip breadth; the ear over the shoulder over the hip over the ankle (O03) | Fat added only sideways, so a heavy body is wide but flat |
| Three-quarter | How the rib cage turns into the waist and the pelvis; the thigh's outer curve; the deltoid's cap against the chest | Hard transitions where region shapes meet |
| Standing | Weight on both feet; knees nearly straight; arms hang with the elbows slightly bent and the palms toward the thighs | Judging shapes only in the A-pose: hang the arms before judging the shoulders |
| Walking | 60% stance, 40% swing (O06); the pelvis rotates and tilts toward the swinging leg; the opposite arm swings forward; the hip extends less than 20 degrees behind (see [../lower/README.md](../lower/README.md)) | Thighs crossing each other and the crotch on a heavy body in mid stride. The keep-apart pass works in the rest pose only (`assets/characters/realistic/shapes.py:39-41`) |

Bodies to collect for each view (brief §13.3), with where to find them: an ordinary man and woman (O04); athletes and lean bodies, heavy bodies and old bodies (B20, links only); muscle and bone under the skin (B15, B16, B17); cross-sections for fat and muscle depth (B13). Do not judge normal anatomy from bodybuilders: the heavy and muscular corner of the shape grid already is one (`assets/characters/realistic/README.md:182`).
