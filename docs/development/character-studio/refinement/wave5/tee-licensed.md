# Wave 5 hand-back: the first licensed T-shirt (decision 13)

Branch `tee-licensed`, from `refinement-wave5`. Task: the first licensed T-shirt, from BlenderKit's "Plain T-shirt" (CC0), through the intake, refined, and passing the walking fit test (Paul's decision 13: "Proper garment construction, clean back and deformation-resistant fit"; it "must pass the walking animation fit test, not just the static character fit test"), with the fit work it needs.

**In one paragraph.** **The tee does not pass. It is not ready for sign-off and stays Draft.** The licensed base came through the intake with its provenance recorded and its CC0 grant evidenced by the asset's page saved as retrieved; authorship by the uploader is not confirmed, and the record says so. It was refined by script: its texture coordinates laid out again (the source's were stretched over 10% on 90% of its area; now 6.5%), a rib neck band, turned hems 3 mm thick at the neck, the hem and both sleeves, and new maps baked from Poly Haven's Cotton Jersey with seam valleys and twin-needle stitching. I fixed two problems the tee showed in the pipeline. The intake wrote BlenderKit's packed WebP maps under a `.png` name, and the engine refused the model. The engine's fit also let a coarse garment's flat triangles cut through the skin between its corners: the source's 2,800 triangles failed the gate at rest on 52 of 54 bodies, up to 10 mm. The push-out now also keeps the body's corners under the cloth's triangles. Without hems that took rest, the elbows bent and the knees bent from 52 failing bodies to none, and every other item's record stayed as it was. **The walk is not solved.** In the stride the tee fails on every gate body, up to 32 mm. It also fails on 53 bodies in Idle (38 mm) and every body with the arms forward (30 mm). The cause is the armpit, as with every tee before it: the cloth folds over itself and flips, and skin shows. The 4K captures show it: torn, flipped flaps of cloth at the front of the armpit, skin specks on the belly in the walk, and dark flaps under the arms when they are raised. The hem also rides up between the legs in the walk, so on the feminine bodies the tee reads as a bodysuit. Of the task's routes I measured (a) and (b) with eight experiments (the table below). Neither got the walk under 10 mm on any third of the stride. I did not build (c), per-pose cloth simulation correctives, or (d), hiding the body under the cloth. Those are the routes left, and I estimate (c) as the one that can work.

**What the table says.**
- **Coarse triangles were the rest failure** (E0 to E1). The base's median edge is 24 mm, and its flat triangles cut a convex arm by up to 10 mm between corners that were all clear. The push-out both ways fixed that for rest and the bent poses, and left every other item's gate and wardrobe record where it was (31 of 31 tests pass unchanged).
- **The armpit is unchanged by (a) and (b)** (E2, E3). Laying the cloth on the skin round the armpit, and giving it the skin's own weights there, moves Idle and the arms forward a little but not the walk: 50 to 54 bodies at 11 to 13 mm. The debug lines put it where tee-walk did, at the torso's side and the arm's inner skin at armpit height. On the feminine bodies the side of the breast by the front of the armpit is added, under folds flipped towards the skin (way out "from outside, flipped", 9.6 to 26 mm). The arms raised got worse: 2 bodies became 22.
- **The turned hems interact with the gate's hem rule** (E4 to E7).
  - The gate takes two rings of triangles in from an **open** edge as the hem. A turned hem's open edge is inside, at the end of its turn-up, and its rings are 1.5 to 18 mm wide. So the fold, and the panel just above it, count as covering the skin just past the fold.
  - With the hems, rest fails on 7 bodies at 28.2 mm, and the briefs on 11 at 17.9 mm. These are corners 15 mm below the waist hem and below a sleeve's end. Their nearest cloth is the fold's underside, and their way out meets nothing.
  - In the poses the misreading is not the whole story (corrected in review fixes 1). With the fold taken as a hem edge (E6), the arms forward still fail on 54 bodies at 30.5 mm against 7.7 mm without hems (E1), Idle on 51 at 19.9 against 38 at 11.1, and the T-pose on 1 at 24.6. **The turned hems as built also make the cloth itself worse in motion**: the sleeve hems' fold triangles under the arm stretch into the T-pose strips and the arms-forward spike (section 5). Look at the turn-up's inner layer in the poses before choosing a gate rule.
  - **Two gate rules were measured and not committed.** Taking the fold as an edge (E6) passes the tee at rest. It also lowers fourteen records of other items, among them the stylized briefs' visible buttock patch in the walk, which it stops seeing: that is too much. Taking only the fold (E7) leaves the tee 1 body at 4.7 mm at rest. It raises the crew tee's rest count from 51 to 52 bodies, a record I may not raise.
  - The gate is unchanged. The hem-edge cases are recorded as this draft's open defects, with this explanation. A rule for turned hems is the lead's decision; the diff is kept outside the repository and can be handed over.
- **Routes not tried:**
  - **(c), cloth-simulated correctives per pose.** It needs a second pass of the import: dump the posed body and garment per fit pose, simulate the cloth in Blender against the posed body, and solve the garment's corrective offsets to those targets instead of the binding's (`posed::follow`'s target). It is the only route left whose target is cloth that doesn't fold through itself. My estimate is a day's work.
  - **(d), hiding the body under the cloth.** It would take the armpit's skin out of the count. But the captures show cloth flipped and torn there, and with the body hidden the flips would show the background through the shirt. I did not take it.
  - **(e), the cut.** The base's armhole is as it came.

## Open issues

1. **The tee fails decision 13's walk**, and the motion check in the run (6 mm). It also fails Idle and the arms forward. The armpit folds and flips, and the hem wraps between the legs. Route (c), per-pose cloth-simulated correctives, is the one left. It is a contract-level change to the import (pose targets in `FitOptions`), so it is for the lead.
2. **The base is too small for the realistic masculine body**, and the push-out shrink-wraps it (up to 34 mm). Its look at rest is painted-on, not a draped tee. A licensed base cut for this body, or a drape of this one (a cloth simulation with its rest lengths grown), is needed before the walk is worth fixing.
3. **The gate and turned hems** (section 3). The lead should decide a rule. E6 and E7 are measured, with what each does to other items' records.
4. **§2 fails:**
   - stretch on every level (6.5%, 37.5%, 65.2% against 5%);
   - weights past the next joint (1,919 corners);
   - mostly triangles;
   - LOD1 and LOD2 into the skin;
   - no simulated folds.
