# Character clothing on a morphable body: workflow research

Research note, 2026-11-24. Question: what is the best-practice workflow for clothing that conforms reliably to a morphable body (many sliders, a masculine and a feminine frame) across snug tops, loose tops, trousers, skirts and dresses, one-pieces, outerwear and shoes, and how should AI-generated garment meshes be prepared for it? This is research and a recommendation, not a plan. **Built the same day** ([ADR-0045](../../wiki/Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0045-What-Is-Worn-Over-Skin-Or-Cloth-Hides-It-By-A-Cover-Map.md)): D5 and D9 (a cover map per item in the body's texture space, hiding the skin and the cloth of garments worn beneath), D3 in part (cloth shared between the body's parts, for conforming and for skin weights; not a separate proxy surface), D1 in part (the frame as a tag and a `frame` limit; one source per garment still), D7 in part (edges tidied after thinning, not remeshed with locked loops), D12 (the list says slot, frame and conform class). **Not built:** D2 (a fit proxy), D4 (inpainted weights), D6 (triangle clearance near openings), D8 (layer envelopes), D10 (outerwear and shoe kinds), D11 (a per-frame gate). What exists is in [architecture.md §9](../development/character-studio/architecture.md) and [ADR-0044](../../wiki/Architecture/Architecture-Decisions/ADR-0044-Garments-Not-Made-On-The-Body-Come-Through-An-Intake-Before-The-Fit.md).

## Summary

1. No shipped system relies on pushing cloth off the skin to stop poke-through. All of them **remove the skin under the cloth**, per face or by a mask in the body's texture space (MetaHuman, Mutable, Character Creator, MakeHuman, VRChat tooling), and keep a small clearance only near openings. Axcenity's 16 coarse regions that must be covered whole in every pose are the outlier, and they are why skin shows.
2. No shipped system binds loose cloth to the raw nearest skin. They bind to a **smooth stand-in for the body**: an authored cage (Roblox), helper geometry such as a skirt or tights surface (MakeHuman), a single-sided simulation mesh (MetaHuman), or a sampled radial-basis field (Unreal's resizing nodes). Blender's manual calls the artifacts of far-from-surface binding "an inherent characteristic of surface binding".
3. Skin weights copied from the closest point on the body are known to fail under the arm, at the crotch and on skirts. Epic's 2023 method copies only where the match is trustworthy (within 5% of the bounding diagonal, normals within about 30 to 35 degrees) and solves for the rest over the garment. It ships in Unreal, and the paper is 5 pages with MIT sample code. Epic's own MetaHuman guide still warns of tearing "in areas like armpits and upper thigh regions" afterwards, so a pose test must gate the result.
4. Frames: Epic recommends "at least 2 source bodies (masculine, feminine)" per garment and picks the nearest source for each target body, because warping far from the source body distorts. One garment morphed across frames is the cheap path and the one that shows as malformed.
5. Layering is by an explicit order plus one of: an outer surface per item that the next layer wraps (Roblox), a shrink shape on the inner garment switched on by what is worn over it (Cyberpunk 2077), or hiding the inner faces (Character Creator, Mutable). None resolves two arbitrary garments against each other at run time by collision alone.
6. A 2 mm per-vertex clearance cannot hold for a coarse garment over a curved limb: a flat triangle edge of length L across a limb of radius r dips L²/8r under the arc. At 2 mm that allows edges of about 2 cm at the wrist and 2.7 cm on the upper arm (own arithmetic, section "Clearance").
7. Generated meshes: vendors document remeshing to a face count, quad or triangle output, part segmentation and whole-character auto-rigging in an A- or T-pose. None documents turning a generated garment into a wearable, rigged, single-layer item. That gap is real; the intake of ADR-0044 is not duplicating a vendor feature.
8. Recommended for Axcenity: keep the import-time fit and the morph-target runtime; add a per-base **fit proxy** to bind to, **inpainted weights**, a **per-item hide mask in body UV space**, **per-frame gating and tagging**, **layer envelopes** for garments imported apart, and a gate that tests triangles, not vertices. Decisions D1 to D12 below.

## What exists (so it is not proposed again)

- Fit at import, nothing at run time: each garment vertex is bound to the closest body triangle within 25 cm (`fit/bind.rs`, `BIND_REACH`), takes that point's skin weights (smoothed over the garment for a few rounds) and every identity and corrective shape as a morph target, and is pushed 2 mm clear per vertex.
- Body hiding by about 16 map regions, hidden only when covered whole in every fit pose. The test tee hides only `waist`.
- An intake for generated garments (ADR-0044): winding, cross-section reading, one wall, kind guess, pose, size and place, back to rest, carry from the `cut_for` body to the default one by nearest skin, decimation to a budget. Plus `smooth_shapes` and a longer `push_reach` for those garments.
- Item manifests have `slot`, `also_fills`, `layer` 0 to 3, `requires`, `excludes`, and `limitations` with a base parameter and a range that drive the "Fits this body" filter.

Observed defects this note is measured against: (a) skin patches through cloth at rest and in poses; (b) a feminine-cut garment malformed on the masculine frame; (c) tops and trousers imported apart cross at the waist; (d) webs and tears under a raised arm; (e) ragged hems.

## How shipped systems do it

### Roblox layered clothing (cage deformer)

- Each body carries an **outer cage**; each garment carries an **inner cage** (the surface it sits on) and an **outer cage** (the surface the next layer sits on). Staff announcement: the inner cage "determines the inner surface of the renderable mesh"; "The difference between the two cages determines the 'thickness' of the renderable mesh and is used by the deformer to determine how exactly the renderable mesh shape should be changed" ([Cage Mesh Deformer, Studio Beta](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/cage-mesh-deformer-studio-beta/1196727)).
- Cages of different shape are matched by **UV coordinates**, not by position: "Different cage meshes may be arbitrarily shaped, but as long as they share a UV layout, they are compatible" (same post). So every cage is the same template mesh reshaped, and the docs forbid deleting cage vertices or UVs because "they are used to match coordinates between other cages" ([specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/avatar/layered-accessories/specifications)).
- The deformation "runs only once", then the result is skinned and animated normally; layers are processed "from lowest to highest" `Order`, each wrapping the one below; the deformer is "bi-directional (a layer can be driven by other layers below or can drive/compress them)" (same post). `WrapTarget.Stiffness` (now deprecated) set how far the body may be compressed by clothing, 0 removing all intersections at the cost of visible deformation, default 0.9 ([WrapTarget](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/reference/engine/classes/WrapTarget)); `WrapLayer.Order` sets the composition order and `ShrinkFactor`/`Puffiness` (deprecated) tuned a layer ([WrapLayer](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/reference/engine/classes/WrapLayer)).
- Authoring rules that matter: the garment must cover the inner cage everywhere and stay inside the outer cage; anything outside the outer cage is not deformed; keep the outer cage close to the mesh; do not move cage vertices the garment does not cover ([caging best practices](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/avatar/layered-accessories/caging-best-practices)). A garment is a single mesh of at most 4,000 triangles, skinned to the R15 rig with at most 4 influences, or left to automatic skinning transfer (specifications).
- **Automatic skinning transfer** computes the garment's weights at run time from the body's geometry and cage (`AutoSkin` `EnabledPreserve` or `EnabledOverride`); the docs say it is based on the closest distance between the accessory and its inner cage, so it can attach cloth to the wrong body region, and it is not meant for rigid items ([automatic skinning transfer](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/avatar/automatic-skinning-transfer)).
- Not found: the deformer's mathematics. The launch post says only that the team "found a way to wrap every vertex of one mesh around another" and was inspired by papers on rocket aerodynamics ([Layers of Genius Behind Layered Clothing](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2022/04/layers-genius-behind-layered-clothing)). That it is a radial-basis mesh deformation, as used in aerodynamic mesh morphing, is a guess, not a documented fact. Also not found: any removal of body faces under layered clothing, or a documented default order per clothing type.

What transfers to Axcenity: the body is never bound to directly. A smooth, fixed-topology stand-in is. And layering is solved by each item publishing an outer surface, not by colliding garment against garment.

### Unreal Engine

**Transfer Skin Weights with inpainting (Chaos Cloth and Geometry Script; the pages read are for 5.7 and the current release, and the release it first shipped in was not confirmed).** Two methods: `ClosestPointOnSurface` and `InpaintWeights`. Epic: "Inpaint Weights is much better at handling non-skin-tight clothing at the cost of slightly slower execution times. It works in two stages" ([action utility guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/create-an-action-utility-for-transferring-skin-weights-in-unreal-engine)). The node's documented defaults ([TransferSkinWeights node reference, 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/node-reference/Dataflow/TransferSkinWeights?application_version=5.7)): `RadiusPercentage` 0.05 of the bounding box diagonal, `NormalThreshold` 30 degrees, `LayeredMeshSupport` true (retry with a flipped normal for inner walls), `NumSmoothingIterations` 10 at `SmoothingStrength` 0.1 on the vertices that were solved, an optional `InpaintMask` that forces vertices to be solved, at most 8 influences. Algorithm in "Algorithms" below.

**MetaHuman parametric clothing (UE 5.6 and later).** The Outfit Asset "associates your clothing with the body it was made for (source body). It then warps the clothing based on the difference between the source body and a new body with different measurements (target body)." And: "In some cases, the source body and target body are too different, which can cause warping... we encourage you to create multiple outfits for multiple source bodies... Our system automatically detects which source body is the best match for each new target body" ([getting started](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/getting-started-for-creating-parametric-clothing-in-metahuman)). Source bodies: "we recommend you create at least 2 source bodies (masculine, feminine)... The more source bodies you have, the higher the quality"; and "Make sure the armpits and thigh gap have a bit of clearance. When they are clipping into each other, it is likely to cause some issues with resizing" ([creating your MetaHuman characters](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/creating-your-metahuman-characters)).

- A garment may carry a **simulation mesh**: "a simplified, single-sided mesh" that "drives [the] render mesh" and "also can be used for resizing and skinning functionality" (getting started). The render mesh "can have thickness". So the thing that is fitted and weighted is one clean layer, and the detailed, thick mesh follows it.
- Body hiding is a painted texture in the body's UV space, the **Body Hidden Face Map**: "This map tells MHC which faces of the body are always hidden by the clothing, and removes them in the assembly process. This makes the garment look better by reducing clipping, and also makes the MetaHuman more efficient" ([testing and configuring](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/testing-and-configuring-your-parametric-outfit-asset)). Note the word "always".
- Weights come from the body by the transfer above, and Epic states the limit: "Due to the limitations of skin weight transfer from the body, you may occasionally encounter strange looking artifacts, such as tearing in areas like armpits and upper thigh regions", cured by painted maps for relax, prune, hammer, clamp and normalise ([building with an FBX export](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/building-a-metahuman-parametric-outfit-with-an-fbx-export)).
- The resizing itself is exposed as experimental Dataflow nodes that "sample points and generate RBF Interpolation data for a given Source mesh" ([GenerateRBFResizingWeights, 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/node-reference/Dataflow/GenerateRBFResizingWeights?application_version=5.7)). The kernel, the point count used for outfits and how garment and body samples are combined are not documented on the pages read. Unverified beyond the node descriptions.

**Mutable (Customizable Objects).** Read from Anticto's documentation wiki (Anticto wrote Mutable; the wiki's pages state which engine version they describe).

- *Following body morphs:* Mesh Reshape. "Vertices in the Base Mesh are bind to faces in the Base Shape by proximity" and are then moved with the matching faces of the Target Shape, usually a morph of the base shape; base and target must share topology. Laplacian smoothing and normal recomputation are options because the output "may loose some quality in the re-targeting process". A vertex colour channel can weight the effect per vertex, and others can mark **rigid clusters** that take one transformation (buttons, buckles) ([Skeletal Mesh Reshape](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Skeletal-Mesh-Reshape), [Node Mesh Reshape v5.5](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Node-Mesh-Reshape-%28v5.5%29)). This is the same nearest-face binding Axcenity has, with the same admitted loss.
- *Removing the body under cloth:* three tools. Clip With UV Mask removes geometry selected by a mask texture in a UV channel, and "only the faces that fall completely inside the mask are removed" ([Clip With UV Mask](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Clip-With-UV-Mask)). Remove Mesh removes the vertices shared with a supplied mesh fragment, per LOD ([Mesh Remove (Modifier)](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Mesh-Remove-%28Modifier%29)). Clip Mesh With Mesh removes what is completely enclosed in a closed volume ([Mesh Clip With Mesh](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Mesh-Clip-With-Mesh)). All take a "Face Cull Strategy"; its options are not described on the pages read.
- *Tucking:* Clip Mesh With Plane and Morph cuts a mesh at a plane round a bone and morphs it near the cut "so it blends in more naturally" ([page](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Clip-Mesh-With-Plane-and-Morph)): a trouser leg ending inside a boot, by rule, not by collision.
- Epic's official Mutable pages for 5.8 and later did not render for the fetcher; the 5.7 "Physics and Clothing" page read covers cloth simulation only.

### Daz Studio

- **Auto-Fit** converts a garment made for another figure using a **clone shape** of that figure and a **projection template** chosen by garment type; it "preserves existing morphs" and "isn't always a perfect process" ([Auto-Fit tutorial, Carrara 8.5 user guide](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/software/carrara/8_5/userguide/finding_loading_and_organizing_content/tutorials/auto_fit/start); the Daz Studio page itself was not found). The template per garment type is the point: a skirt is projected differently from trousers.
- **Morph projection ("auto-follow")**: the Transfer Active Morphs action "projects the active morphs on a host figure to the items that are fit to it" ([DzMorphFollowerAction](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/software/dazstudio/4/referenceguide/interface/inline/advanced/dzmorphfolloweraction)). How the projection is computed is not documented there.
- **Smoothing modifier with collision**: an iterative smoothing of the garment that also collides it against a chosen item. Defaults from the API reference: type "Base Shape Matching", 2 smoothing iterations, 3 collision iterations, a collision smoothing interval of 1 ([DzMeshSmoothModifier](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/software/dazstudio/4/referenceguide/scripting/api_reference/object_index/meshsmoothmodifier_dz)). Daz's own note: the mesh resolution decides how many iterations are needed, and a low-poly mesh changes shape under generic smoothing ([smoothing issues](https://docs.daz3d.com/public/publishing/smoothing_issues_content/start)). This is a per-frame, offline-renderer cost; it is the closest shipped analogue of "push the cloth out", and it runs on dense garments with smoothing in the loop.

### Reallusion Character Creator

- **Hide body mesh, per face, stored on the garment**: hidden faces are chosen with a dummy or by hand; "Visibility data will be stored on the clothes and reapplied" when worn, valid for characters of the same generation ([Hiding Body Mesh Faces](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-4/Content/ENU/4.0/08_Cloth/Hiding_Body_Meshes_by_Faces.htm)). The developer guideline gives the reason: for tight garments "skin weight adjustments alone may not prevent mesh interpenetration", so hide the regions underneath, then replay a calibration motion and confirm no poke-through ([Hiding Body Mesh](https://wiki.reallusion.com/Content_Dev:CC_Clothing_Hide_Body_Mesh)).
- **Auto Hide Mesh**, for body and for garments under garments: for each vertex "a ray is sent from the bind bone to the vertex hiding the geometries that lay between it and the outermost mesh"; wrists, head, neck and shoulders are always shown; around see-through areas it "will maintain a ring of extra visible mesh faces as a safe visual buffer"; it is preferred over conforming "to maintain the shape integrity of the outer-most layer, whereas conforming would wrap-deform the mesh" ([Automatically Hiding Inner Meshes](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-4/Content/ENU/4.0/08_Cloth/Automatically_Hiding_Inner_Meshes.htm)).
- **Layer order and "Run Collision"**: every garment has a cloth type and a layer number; a larger number covers a smaller; the conform calculation depends on the layer values ([Clothing Layers Settings](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-5/Content/ENU/5.0/08_Cloth/Cloth-Layer-Settings.htm), [Cloth Type and Collision Settings](https://wiki.reallusion.com/Content_Dev:CC_Clothing_Type_and_Collision_Settings)). CC 5.2 has eight cloth types and **nine skin weight templates** chosen "based on the garment's pattern structure and cut", with Glove and Shoe singled out; Shoe enables "Adjust Floor Contact" ([Skin Weight Templates](https://wiki.reallusion.com/Content_Dev:CC_Clothing_Skin_Weight_Templates)). The names of the other templates are in the user manual and were not read.

### MakeHuman, MakeClothes and MPFB2

The closest relative of Axcenity's binding fit, with twenty years of documented failure modes.

- **Binding**: each clothes vertex is tied to a triangle of human vertices, "a point on a body triangle, described in barycentric coordinates, and the offset from that point" ([MakeClothes, old documentation](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/oldsite/documentation/mhblendertools_makeclothes.html)). In MPFB2's `.mhclo` a vertex line is three body vertex indices, three weights and an offset, and the offset is scaled per axis by a body measure (`x_scale`, `y_scale`, `z_scale`: the distance between two named body vertices) ([mhclo.py](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/src/mpfb/entities/clothes/mhclo.py), [clothesservice.py](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/src/mpfb/services/clothesservice.py)).
- **The binding is restricted by vertex group**: "Only human vertices in the correct vertex group will be considered" (old documentation), and MPFB2 refuses a vertex whose group is missing on the body; its face match also requires the face normal to be within 45 degrees of the direction to the cloth vertex ([vertexmatch.py](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/src/mpfb/entities/clothes/vertexmatch.py)). That is how a sleeve is kept from binding to the torso and one leg from the other.
- **Helper geometry**: the base mesh carries invisible helper surfaces (a helper skirt, helper tights, a helper sweater) that follow every body morph; clothes bind to those, not to the skin. A skirt binds to the helper skirt, which spans both legs ([introduction](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/makeclothes/introduction.html), old documentation). The docs also note "problems increase with distance between clothes and human vertices" ([controlling the result with vertex groups](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/makeclothes/controlling_the_result_with_vertex_groups.html)), that a group bound to a single body vertex makes a part rigid, and that midline groups keep vertices on the mirror plane.
- **Delete groups** are the stated cure for skin through cloth. The MakeClothes page explains the cause exactly as observed here: "the skin has a higher resolution than the cloth... Because of lower geometry an intersection cannot be avoided"; more space between cloth and skin helps, "but it is not a guarantee for very low poly clothes"; "use delete-groups if you create non-transparent tight clothes... No skin will intersect with clothes then" ([delete groups](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/makeclothes/makeclothes_deletegroups.html)). Rule: "Don't assign a vertex to the Delete group unless all faces containing it are hidden" (old documentation). MPFB2 enforces it in code: `_conservative_mask` drops from the delete list every vertex that belongs to a face with a vertex outside the list (clothesservice.py), so only faces hidden whole go.
- **Stacking**: a `z_depth` per item, 0 for skin to 100 for a backpack, with named bands (underwear, socks, shirt and trousers, sweater, indoor jacket, shoes and boots, coat) that decide "which clothes should hide others" (old documentation).

### Cyberpunk 2077 GarmentSupport

Source is the modding community's wiki, not CD Projekt; it documents the shipped data, not the studio's intent.

- Each garment mesh carries a shrink morph and two vertex colour layers. `_GARMENTSUPPORTWEIGHT` sets how much each vertex gives way when something is worn over it; `_GARMENTSUPPORTCAP` marks where the garment must not crush what is under it ([painting garment support parameters](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/for-mod-creators-theory/3d-modelling/garment-support-how-does-it-work/painting-garment-support-parameters)).
- Which garment gives way is decided by a **garment score** from the component's slot prefix plus a size tag: shoes 50, legs 60, inner torso 70, outer torso 120; tags Tight −1000, Normal 0, Large +1000, XLarge +2000; "a high garment score means that the item is 'on top', squishing anything worn 'below'"; components with the same prefix do not squish each other ([how does it work](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/for-mod-creators-theory/3d-modelling/garment-support-how-does-it-work)).
- How the shrink morph is generated in CD Projekt's pipeline is not documented there. Unverified.

### VRChat and VRM practice

- Avatar bodies sold for dress-up ship with "shrink" blend shapes per body part. Modular Avatar's Shape Changer lets an outfit "delete or shrink parts of the avatar base model which are hidden by or interfere with the outfit mesh"; in Delete mode it removes the polygons the blend shape moves, recommended "when the blendshape would shrink a bodypart to (almost) nothing" ([Shape Changer](https://modular-avatar.nadena.dev/docs/reference/reaction/shape-changer)). Mesh Cutter removes parts of a mesh selected by mask texture, bone weight, blend shape, axis or UV tile, with the stated use "removing portions of an underlying body mesh that clips through outer layers of clothing" ([Mesh Cutter](https://modular-avatar.nadena.dev/docs/reference/reaction/mesh-cutter)).
- VRoid Studio's export has a "delete transparent meshes" option and its release notes list deleting base mesh hidden by clothing; only third-party descriptions were found, not the official help page. Unverified in detail.

### Blender modifiers (the DCC baseline)

- **Surface Deform** binds each vertex to a face of a target surface. The manual: "The further a mesh deviates from the target mesh surface, the more likely it is to get undesirable artifacts. This is an inherent characteristic of surface binding in general, so it is recommended to have reasonably well matching meshes". Its documented use is "a proxy mesh for cloth simulation, which will in turn drive the motion of your final and more detailed mesh" ([Surface Deform](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/surface_deform.html)).
- **Mesh Deform** uses a closed cage round the mesh; binding is slow, and large changes to the cage cause artifacts that are "a known limitation" ([Mesh Deform](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/mesh_deform.html)).
- **Shrinkwrap** with an offset and the "Outside" mode moves only vertices that are inside the target; the manual says these modes "can be used for very crude collision detection" ([Shrinkwrap](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/shrinkwrap.html)). It is per vertex, like Axcenity's push.
- **Data Transfer** copies vertex groups by "Nearest Face Interpolated" (closest point, barycentric) or "Projected Face Interpolated" (along the destination normal), with an optional maximum distance ([Data Transfer](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/modify/data_transfer.html)). This is the closest-point transfer the Epic paper measures against.

### The Sims 4

No first-party documentation was found. Community tooling threads describe garments as replacement parts for body regions with separate meshes for the masculine and the feminine frame, which would make it a "replace, don't layer" system. Unverified; not used as evidence below.

## Algorithms

### Carrying body shape changes to cloth

| Method | How | Good for | Fails on | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nearest-surface binding | Vertex tied to a body triangle with an offset in its frame | Cloth within about a centimetre of skin | Loose cloth: neighbours bind to far-apart skin, creases and concavities pull spikes | MakeHuman, Mutable Mesh Reshape, Blender Surface Deform, Axcenity today |
| Binding to a stand-in surface | Same binding, but to a smooth helper or cage that follows the body | Skirts, coats, anything spanning a concavity | Needs the stand-in authored once per base | MakeHuman helpers, Roblox cages, MetaHuman simulation mesh |
| Closed cage coordinates | Vertex is a fixed mix of cage vertices (mean value or harmonic coordinates) | Smooth volumetric deformation | Slow to bind, needs a closed cage, no notion of "on the surface" | Blender Mesh Deform; Pixar's harmonic coordinates (Joshi et al. 2007, bibliographic details only, not read) |
| Scattered-data field | Body displacement sampled at points, interpolated in space by radial basis functions | Loose cloth, uniform quality at any distance | Space, not surface: a sleeve next to the torso feels the torso unless samples are restricted by part | Unreal's RBF resizing nodes; the method comes from fluid-structure mesh motion (de Boer, van der Schoot, Bijl 2007, bibliographic details only) |
| Delta smoothing | Any of the above, then the per-shape moves are smoothed over the garment | Removing spikes | Hides, does not fix, a wrong binding | Mutable's Laplacian option, Axcenity's `smooth_shapes` |

Two points come out of the sources. First, the distance from cloth to the bound surface is the failure variable (Blender, MakeClothes), so the cure is to bring the bound surface to the cloth, not to smooth harder. Second, every binding method needs a **part restriction** so that proximity in space is not mistaken for belonging (MakeClothes vertex groups; the 45 degree normal test in MPFB2; the distance and normal test in Epic's paper).

### Skin weights: closest point and inpainting

Abdrashitov, Raichstat, Monsen and Hill, "Robust Skin Weights Transfer via Weight Inpainting", SIGGRAPH Asia 2023 Technical Communications ([project page](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~rinat/projects/RobustSkinWeightsTransfer/index.html), [preprint](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~rinat/projects/RobustSkinWeightsTransfer/preprint.pdf), [sample code, MIT](https://github.com/rin-23/RobustSkinWeightsTransferCode)). Read in full.

- Why closest point fails: pairs of points "are close to each other in world space, they are far apart in shape space". A sleeve vertex matches the torso; a flared trouser leg matches the other leg; the lower part of a dress "has a sharp break in the middle due to vertices being matched to the closest points on the legs". In creases, "the vertices in the armpit area are split between being influenced by the torso bones and the arm bones. So when the character raises the arm, edges connecting the two vertices that are influenced by a different set of bones end up being stretched". That sentence is defect (d).
- Stage 1, matching: for each garment vertex take the closest point on the body and accept it only if it is within D = 0.05 × the garment's bounding box diagonal and the normals differ by at most Θ = 35 degrees. For an inner wall, an optional second pass with the normal flipped. Accepted vertices copy the barycentric mix of the body's weights.
- Stage 2, inpainting: for the rest, minimise trace(Wᵀ(−L + L M⁻¹ L)W) with the accepted rows fixed, where L is the cotangent Laplacian and M the lumped mass matrix (a mix of the Dirichlet and the Laplacian energy). One sparse linear solve per bone that touches the unknown set, by Cholesky. Weights come out slightly negative at worst (no lower than −0.03 in their tests): clamp to 0 and renormalise.
- After: a few rounds of Laplacian smoothing on the solved vertices, "especially beneficial in areas like the armpits and the crotch".
- Artist control: a painted mask forces vertices into the solved set (their example is a collar that would otherwise follow the jaw).
- Stated limits: corrective bones whose area falls mostly in the solved set are not reconstructed; no guarantee against interpenetration in extreme poses; and "our method assumes that all clothing parts are connected", so a garment made of separate sheets must be welded first.

### Body masking

The agreement across systems is close to complete:

- Hide **faces**, chosen per garment, not regions of the body (Character Creator, MakeHuman, Mutable, MetaHuman, Modular Avatar).
- Store the choice so that it survives a change of body tessellation where possible: a **mask in the body's UV space** (MetaHuman's Body Hidden Face Map, Mutable's Clip With UV Mask, Mesh Cutter "by mask") rather than face indices (Character Creator's data is valid only within one body generation; Mutable's Remove Mesh must match vertices at every LOD).
- A face goes only when all of it is covered (MPFB2's conservative mask; Mutable removes "only the faces that fall completely inside the mask"; MetaHuman hides faces that are "always hidden").
- Keep a ring of visible skin at openings and see-through areas (Character Creator's "safe visual buffer"); never hide wrists, neck and the like by automation.
- Verify with motion, not at rest (Reallusion's calibration motion). Axcenity's rule that covered-at-rest is not enough is the same idea and stays; what changes is the unit it is applied to.

### Layering, slots and inner-garment shrink

Three mechanisms, all driven by an explicit order and none by run-time collision between arbitrary garments:

1. **Published outer surface** (Roblox): each item carries the surface the next one sits on, so any pair composes. Costs an outer cage per item and a deformer run when the outfit changes.
2. **Shrink shape on the inner item** (Cyberpunk 2077): a morph that pulls the item in, weighted per vertex, switched on when a higher-scoring item is worn over it, with a cap so thin parts are not crushed. Costs one morph and two vertex attributes per item; nothing at run time beyond a weight.
3. **Hide the covered faces of the inner item** (Character Creator's Auto Hide, Mutable's Remove and Clip): the same masking as for skin, applied to garments.

Order values are per category with a size modifier: MakeHuman's `z_depth` bands, Cyberpunk's slot score plus Tight/Normal/Large, Character Creator's cloth type plus layer number, Roblox's `Order`. Slot exclusivity (one top, one bottom, a dress filling both) is handled by the catalogue, as Axcenity's `slot` and `also_fills` already do.

### Clearance and tessellation

Own arithmetic, not from a source; the sources only state the effect (MakeClothes: with lower cloth geometry "an intersection cannot be avoided").

A garment edge of length L whose two ends sit a margin m off a limb of radius r is a chord: its middle is L²/8r nearer the axis than its ends. The skin stays covered only while L²/8r < m, so L < √(8rm). With m = 2 mm: 2.0 cm at a wrist or ankle (r ≈ 2.5 cm), 2.7 cm on an upper arm (r ≈ 4.5 cm), 4.9 cm across the chest (r ≈ 15 cm). A 6,000 triangle top has edges near 2 cm if they were uniform, and quadric decimation makes them longer wherever the cloth is flat, so the margin is already spent at rest, before a shape or a pose bends the limb more sharply (an elbow or knee has a radius near 2 to 3 cm). This is defect (a) in numbers. Consequences: a per-vertex test cannot certify a garment; the test has to be on the skin's points against the cloth's triangles (which `fit::outside_cloth` already does for the gate); and the affordable fix is hiding the skin, with clearance and edge length budgeted only for the band near openings.

Per-triangle or distance-field collision (resolving each cloth triangle against a signed distance field of the body) is how simulators do it; no character-creator system in this survey documents doing so for conforming clothes, and none was read as a primary source here. Not needed if masking carries the load.

### Masculine and feminine frames

- Epic: several source bodies per garment, at least one per frame, nearest source chosen per target (above).
- Daz: a clone shape per source figure, and garments are sold per figure generation and gender, with Auto-Fit as the cross-figure fallback that "isn't always a perfect process".
- Roblox: one garment for every body, but through an authored cage and at 4,000 triangles of stylised cloth, where warping reads as style.
- MakeHuman: one garment for every body through the binding; the docs concede clothes fitted on one body "will not look as good" on a different one.

So: a garment has a home frame. Systems aiming at realism make or fit it per frame and tag it; systems aiming at universality accept visible warping.

## Preparing AI-generated meshes

What vendors document:

- **Meshy**: Remesh rebuilds a model to a target face count with quad or triangle output; the API defaults are `triangle` and 30,000 faces, range 100 to 300,000 ([Remesh API](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/remesh), [Remesh guide](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/3d-model/remesh)). The guide warns that holes can appear when the source has non-manifold edges and that small parts vanish at low budgets. Whether textures are rebaked onto the new mesh is not stated in the guide; the API returns texture URLs with the result. Rigging is for whole characters: "the character stands in a T-pose or A-pose, with a clear gap between the limbs and the body", humanoid or quadruped, and for clipping it says to remesh first ([Rigging guide](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/3d-model/rigging)). Nothing on garments.
- **Tripo**: segmentation into parts, "part completion" that closes each split part into a watertight piece, retopology per part. Only vendor blog posts and third-party API mirrors were found, not first-party reference pages. Unverified. Note that part completion produces closed solids, which is the opposite of what a garment needs.
- **Rodin (Hyper3D)**: no first-party documentation relevant to garments was found.

Honest reading: the 30,000 triangle closed shells in ADR-0044 match a generator's default remesh output. No vendor documents removing the inner wall, opening the mesh into one layer, posing a garment, or fitting it to a body. Practitioner write-ups found were marketing. The nearest authoritative guidance is from the receiving side:

- The mesh that is fitted and weighted should be "a simplified, single-sided mesh" (MetaHuman), and the inpainting method assumes one connected surface, with a flipped-normal pass as the workaround for an inner wall (Epic paper). ADR-0044's "one wall" step is therefore the right first move, and welding loose sheets before weighting is required, not optional.
- Model on the body, in its rest pose, with clearance at the armpits and between the thighs (MetaHuman source bodies). A generated garment with hanging arms has no such clearance, which is why the sleeve-to-hip problem in ADR-0044 exists; asking the generator for an A-pose (where a tool offers a pose control, as Meshy does for characters) attacks it at the source. Whether pose prompts hold for garments without a body is untested.
- Remesh before fitting, to an edge length the clearance needs, not after to a triangle count: the bound √(8rm) above is local, a global budget is not.

## Recommended workflow for Axcenity

Each decision names its evidence and the defect it answers. Runtime cost is unchanged throughout: a fitted garment stays a skinned part with morph targets.

**D1. A garment has a home frame and is offered only where it passes.** Keep `cut_for` in the item as its home frame. Fit and gate it on each frame separately (D11); write the result into the item's existing `limitations` on the `frame` parameter so the "Fits this body" filter hides it elsewhere. Where a design matters on both frames, import two sources (one per frame) as two items or two variants. *Evidence:* MetaHuman's two source bodies and nearest-source rule; Daz clones. *Answers:* (b). *Authoring rule:* generate or model each design once per frame when it is closer than loose.

**D2. Give each base a fit proxy and bind garments to it, not to the skin.** A fit proxy is a coarse, smooth surface per base model whose vertices are fixed mixes of body vertices, so it carries every identity and corrective shape exactly (the construction the realistic underwear already uses). It bridges the armpit, the crotch, the gap between the breasts and the buttock cleft; a second sheet, the **skirt proxy**, wraps both legs from hip to ankle as one tube. Garment vertices bind to the proxy by the binding that exists. *Evidence:* Roblox cages, MakeHuman helper skirt and tights, MetaHuman simulation mesh, Blender's and MakeClothes' statements that distance from the bound surface is the failure variable. *Answers:* (b), spikes on loose garments, skirts and dresses breaking between the legs, and part of (d).

**D3. Restrict every binding by part and by direction.** A garment vertex may bind only to proxy triangles of the limb or trunk its intake reading assigned it to (sleeve to arm, leg to that leg, trunk to trunk; the intake already knows this from its cross-sections), and only where the normals agree within about 35 to 45 degrees; otherwise it is left unbound and takes its shape moves from its neighbours (D4's solve, applied to shape deltas). *Evidence:* MakeClothes vertex groups; MPFB2's 45 degree test; Epic's distance and normal test. *Answers:* sleeves that follow the torso's shapes, (b), (d).

**D4. Replace closest-point weights with the two-stage inpainting transfer.** Match within 5% of the garment's bounding diagonal and 30 to 35 degrees, plus D3's part restriction; solve the rest with −L + L M⁻¹ L per bone; clamp, renormalise, cap at four influences; ten light smoothing rounds on solved vertices. Weld the garment into one connected surface first. Per-kind forced masks: collars and hoods, hems below the knee on skirts and dresses, cuffs wider than the wrist. The solve needs a sparse symmetric solver: either a new dependency (pinned in the workspace and licence-checked) or a conjugate-gradient solve written in place, which is enough at garment sizes. *Evidence:* the paper; Unreal's defaults. *Answers:* (d).

**D5. Hide skin per item with a mask in the body's UV space.** At import, find the body triangles under the fitted garment in every fit pose, on every pose state and at the shape extremes (the `pose_safe_hides` rule that exists, applied per triangle), pull the set back from every garment opening by a fixed band (start at 2 cm measured over the skin), and rasterise it into a small one-bit mask in body UV space stored with the item. At run time the body is drawn without the triangles that lie wholly inside the union of the worn items' masks, through the `RegionHiding` path that exists (keyed by the mask set instead of the region set). The 16 regions remain as the coarse fallback for the stylised base. *Evidence:* MetaHuman's Body Hidden Face Map, Mutable's Clip With UV Mask, Character Creator's stored face hiding and safe buffer, MPFB2's conservative mask. *Answers:* (a).

**D6. Budget clearance and edge length only for the visible band.** Inside the mask no clearance is needed. In the band between the mask and the opening, and wherever skin stays drawn under cloth, require L < √(8rm): keep edges under about 2 cm at cuffs, ankles and necklines with m at 3 to 4 mm, and resolve with the skin's points against the cloth's triangles (move the triangle's corners), not corner by corner. *Evidence:* MakeClothes delete groups page; own arithmetic. *Answers:* (a) at openings.

**D7. Remesh with locked boundaries before the fit, decimate nothing after.** Simplify or remesh the single wall to the edge lengths D6 needs with every open boundary loop preserved (boundary edges collapse only along the loop), then smooth each loop in its own plane. Keep UVs through the simplification, or rebake the source maps when a remesh replaces them. Count boundary loops before and after; a change fails the import. *Evidence:* Meshy's warning that remeshing opens holes and drops small parts; the paper's connected-surface assumption. *Answers:* (e).

**D8. Compose garments imported apart through layer envelopes.** Define, per base, one envelope surface per `layer` (0 to 3): the fit proxy pushed out by a fixed thickness per layer. An item of layer n is fitted so that, wherever another slot's items can overlap it (the waist band for tops and bottoms, the ankle for trousers and shoes, the whole trunk for outerwear), it lies between envelope n−1 and envelope n. Give tops and bottoms a `tuck` rule instead of sharing a layer: by default a top hangs outside the bottom (the top's hem zone is fitted to the outer half of the layer, the bottom's waistband to the inner half), and an item may declare the opposite. *Evidence:* Roblox's outer cage, reduced to a fixed surface per layer; MakeHuman's `z_depth` bands; Cyberpunk's score with a size tag. *Answers:* (c). *Cost:* loose cuts are pulled toward the envelope only in the overlap zones.

**D9. Hide or shrink the inner garment under the outer one.** Each garment vertex knows a body UV through its binding, so an outer item's mask (D5) can hide lower-layer garment triangles the same way it hides skin: a triangle of a lower layer whose three corners map inside a higher layer's mask is not drawn. Where an inner garment is bulky (a hoodie under a jacket), add one generated shrink shape toward envelope n−1, switched on when a higher layer in an overlapping slot is worn. Start with hiding; add the shrink shape only when a pairing needs it. *Evidence:* Character Creator's Auto Hide over body and clothes; Mutable; Cyberpunk's GarmentSupport. *Answers:* (c), outerwear over tops.

**D10. Fit rules by garment kind.** One table the import follows; `GarmentKind` grows `outerwear` and `shoes`.

| Kind | Binds to | Weights | Hide mask | Layer and slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snug top | Body proxy, part restricted | Inpainting, few unknowns | Trunk and upper arms minus the band | 1, `top` |
| Loose top | Body proxy; delta smoothing kept | Inpainting; underarm and hem forced | Trunk above the hem band only | 1 or 2, `top` |
| Trousers | Body proxy, each leg to its leg | Inpainting; crotch forced | Hips and legs minus waist and ankle bands | 1, `bottom` |
| Skirt, dress skirt | Skirt proxy below the hip, body proxy above | Inpainting from the waist and from where the cloth meets the thighs; hem forced | Hips to the hem band; nothing hidden under a short or slit skirt beyond what every pose covers | 1, `bottom` (dress: `top` and fills `bottom`) |
| One-piece | Body proxy; skirt proxy if it has no legs | As top plus trousers | Union | 1, `top` fills `bottom` |
| Outerwear | Envelope of layer 2 (so it fits over any top) | Inpainting; collar, hood and open front forced | Hides lower garments by D9, skin by D5 | 3, `outer` |
| Shoes | Foot and toe bones only; scaled by foot length and width; no body shapes carried | Authored or by bone distance | The whole foot below the shoe's top line | 1, `feet`; heel height as a foot pose the item sets |

*Evidence:* Daz projection templates per garment type; Character Creator's nine weight templates with Shoe and Glove special; MakeHuman helpers; MetaHuman's note that the fitted layer is the single-sided one.

**D11. Gate every item per frame, on triangles, in motion.** For each frame the item claims, at each body extreme the manifest marks for testing and in each fit pose: (1) no drawn skin point more than 1 mm outside the cloth (the rule that exists, after D5's mask); (2) no garment triangle stretched more than about 2 times its rest area or edge length (catches webs under the arm); (3) no garment triangle through a triangle of the reference item of each overlapping slot (a reference trouser, top and shoe per base); (4) boundary loops unchanged in count and smooth within a tolerance; (5) share of vertices matched in D4 and the largest bind distance, reported, with a floor per kind; (6) triangle budget and file size. A frame that fails is removed from the item's `limitations` range rather than shipped; an item that passes on no frame is not installed. *Evidence:* Reallusion's calibration motion test; Epic's admission that transfer still tears; Roblox's validation of cage configurations before sale.

**D12. Ask more of the source before the intake has to guess.** For generated garments: request an A-pose matching the body's rest pose where the tool has a pose control; generate per frame (D1); take triangle output at the generator's default density and let the import remesh (D7); list `kind`, `ease`, home frame and `tuck` in `garments.json` rather than inferring them. For garments modelled by hand or draped in a cloth tool: model on the exported body at rest, one layer, with the hem turned in a single ring, which already bypasses the intake. *Evidence:* Meshy's pose and remesh documentation; MetaHuman's source body rules.

Defect to decision:

| Defect | Cause, per the evidence | Decisions |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Skin through cloth | Skin drawn under coarse cloth at a margin below L²/8r | D5, D6, D11 |
| (b) Feminine cut malformed on the masculine frame | Carry by nearest skin across a large shape change; no home frame | D1, D2, D3, D11 |
| (c) Top and trousers cross at the waist | No shared surface or rule between items fitted apart | D8, D9, D11 |
| (d) Webs and tears under a raised arm | Closest-point weights split across the crease | D3, D4, D11 |
| (e) Ragged hems | Decimation and shell removal without boundary handling | D7, D11 |

Suggested order by return for effort: D5 (largest visible gain, reuses `RegionHiding` and `pose_safe_hides`), D4, D7, D1 with D11, then D2 and D3 together, then D8 to D10.

## Open questions and what could not be verified

- **Roblox's deformer mathematics** is not published in the pages read. The radial-basis guess rests on one sentence about rocket aerodynamics. Whether Roblox hides body faces under layered clothing, and its default `Order` per clothing type, were not found.
- **Unreal's outfit resizing**: only the node descriptions were readable (experimental, "RBF Interpolation data"). Kernel, sample count and how body and garment samples combine are unverified. Several Epic pages (Mutable for 5.8 and later, the Panel Cloth Transfer Skin Weights tutorial) did not render and were not read.
- **Mutable's Face Cull Strategy** options and the exact removal rule of Remove Mesh are not described on the wiki pages read.
- **Daz**: the Daz Studio Auto-Fit and Transfer Utility reference pages were not found; Auto-Fit is cited from the Carrara guide, and how morph projection is computed is undocumented in what was read.
- **Character Creator**: the eight cloth types, nine weight templates by name, and the internals of "Run Collision" are in manual pages and images not read.
- **Cyberpunk 2077**: community documentation of shipped data; how CD Projekt generates the shrink morph is unknown.
- **The Sims 4 and VRoid Studio**: no first-party source found; statements about them are unverified.
- **Harmonic coordinates (Joshi et al., SIGGRAPH 2007) and RBF mesh deformation (de Boer et al., Computers & Structures 85, 2007)**: bibliographic details confirmed through search listings; the papers themselves were not read.
- **Generated meshes**: no vendor documents a garment workflow. Tripo and Rodin were not read from first-party reference pages. Whether a pose instruction holds for a garment generated without a body is untested.
- **Fetch method**: the Epic, Blender, MakeHuman, Roblox announcement and paper texts were read as page text and are quoted exactly. The Roblox reference pages, Anticto wiki, Daz, Reallusion, redmodding, Modular Avatar and Meshy pages were read through a fetcher that summarises; their quotations should be rechecked against the page before being repeated elsewhere.
- **Design questions this note does not settle**: the size of D5's band (2 cm is a starting guess, not a sourced number); whether a body UV mask of 256 or 512 pixels a side resolves the realistic body's smallest triangles; who authors the fit proxy and the skirt proxy for the realistic base and how they are validated; whether D8's envelopes flatten loose cuts unacceptably at the waist; whether item size (11 to 43 MB today, mostly carried shapes) falls enough once D2 and D3 make shape deltas smooth enough to store sparsely or at lower precision; and which ADR these decisions amend (ADR-0035 for hiding and binding, ADR-0044 for the intake).
