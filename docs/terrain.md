# Terrain

Read this when changing terrain data, brushes, generation, or how a Terrain is saved and drawn. The Terrain Studio in the editor is `crates/editor` (`terrain_studio.rs`). Under the terrain picker it has three tabs (`terrain_studio/tabs.rs`, `StudioTab`, the design system's segmented control, icons from 300 points wide), each with its row of tools and their settings: **Sculpt** (Raise / Lower, Smooth, Flatten, Sculpt, Stamp, Noise, Holes), **Materials** (Paint, Water, Auto Materials, then the Layers section with Clear painted, Biomes and Scatter Rules) and **Terrain** (Generate with World Presets, Erosion, Masks, then Tiles, Water and the folded Terrain data: resolution and heightmap import and export). A running job's progress, a waiting preview's Apply and the View section show on every tab. The tab shown is always the held tool's, however it was picked (keys, the ribbon's Brush menu and universal search go through `TerrainStudio::pick`); with no tool held it's the last one shown, kept across restarts in egui's persisted memory, and selecting a TerrainLayer, Biome or ScatterRule then shows Materials. Clicking a tab with a tool held picks that tab's last-used tool (its first, the first time); with none held it picks nothing (tests in `terrain_studio/tests.rs` and `a_tool_picked_by_key_ribbon_or_search_shows_its_studio_tab`). A tab's tools show as icons, with each tool's name, key and hint on hover; from 400 points wide it also names each tool beside its icon (`CAPTIONS_WIDTH`; test `a_wide_panel_names_its_tools`). This page is the algorithms in `crates/terrain`.

`TerrainData` lives on the Terrain Instance in `core`. This crate only computes on that data. It does not draw, simulate contacts, or touch the disk: `scene` meshes it, `physics` collides with it, and `project` reads and writes the files.

Foliage is painted onto the terrain with the editor's **Scatter** pane (the ribbon's Models group; [editor/scatter.md](editor/scatter.md)), not a Terrain Studio tool: a palette of items picked from the Assets panel (built-in props or models), each with its weight in the mix, size range, collide, random turn, align to slope and sink, and a brush with a size, a density and a minimum spacing. One stroke is one `Operation::Scatter` (`brush/scatter.rs`): each stamp places a weighted random mix of the palette's enabled items, deterministic for the stroke's seed and the stamp's middle, skipping spots closer than the spacing to any painted foliage (so going over the same ground fills gaps instead of piling up) and hole cells; with `erase` it takes away the palette's enabled items under the brush, or any painted foliage (`ScatterErase::Everything`). An item's masks (`brush/scatter_mask.rs`, `ScatterItem::mask`: noise patches, a slope range, a height range, and moisture worked out from the terrain's own shape, hollows wet and rises dry) thin it out where they say; open masks, the default, place exactly what the brush placed before they existed ([editor/scatter.md](editor/scatter.md)). Instances sit on the surface (sample XZ, so they follow sculpting). They are not Instances. Solid ones are collided with; visual ones let parts fall through to the terrain. One stroke is one undo step. Painted foliage saves as optional `foliage.json` in the terrain's data folder; a missing file means none. Each record keeps `lean` and `sink` when they aren't 0 (older files without them read as upright, on the surface), so baked scatter-rule output and palette items that align to the slope keep it. Older paints that named a built-in plant still load. Tests: `brush/tests.rs` (`scatter_mixes_items_by_weight_with_each_items_settings`, `scatter_keeps_its_spacing_and_stamping_again_adds_no_closer_items`, `scatter_erases_the_palettes_items_or_everything_and_undoes_as_one_step`), `files.rs` `foliage_keeps_lean_and_sink_and_leaves_them_out_when_upright`. Painted plants are drawn only within a cull distance of the camera, fading out before it (grass 120 studs, flowers 150, bushes 300, trees 800, times their scale; props at any distance; a painted model by its size as placed, 120 studs per stud of its largest extent, from 60 to 1,500, so a half-stud fern goes at 60 studs and a 10-stud clump of trees at 1,200, with its levels of detail before that; `scene::foliage::model_cull_distance`). `scene` skips a plant or model past its distance (measured flat, in XZ) before building its draw items, so a terrain with thousands painted costs only those near the camera; the renderer's own cull and fade do the rest (see [rendering/pipeline.md](rendering/pipeline.md)). Painted plants sway in the Workspace's `GlobalWind`, and their shadows with them ([rendering/wind.md](rendering/wind.md)). Painted instances are kept between frames as groups, 48 studs square and short-reach plants apart from trees, rebuilt only when the terrain or a painted model changes, so a painted forest costs the frame what it can see ([rendering/performance.md](rendering/performance.md)).

Scatter rules (procedural worlds PW1, `procedural/src/terrain_scatter.rs`): a `ScatterRule` inside a Terrain places plants, props or models wherever its own conditions hold: `Spacing` (studs between neighbours) and `Density` (plants per 100 square studs: each `Spacing`-wide cell is used with a chance of `Density × Spacing² / 100`, at most 1, so full cover at a spacing of 2 studs needs a `Density` of 25), a height window (`MinHeight`, `MaxHeight`, `HeightFade`), a slope window (`MinSlope`, `MaxSlope`, `SlopeFade`, in degrees), a material `Layer` (the TerrainLayer's name, with `LayerThreshold` of its weight), `Clumping` and `ClumpSize` (noise that gathers plants into patches), `AvoidRadius` (kept clear of what rules above it placed), `Underwater`, `RotationRange`, `ScaleRange` and `CanCollide`. `Models` lists what it places (`plant:Tree`, `prop:Crate` or a model path, one per line). Rules run in order, like TerrainLayers. Placement is a hash grid: each `Spacing`-wide cell gets one spot from `Seed` and the cell's number, so every 32-sample chunk is worked out on its own, on every device, by `procedural::Generators::update`, and the same terrain, rules and seed give the same layout. The result sits in `TerrainData` beside painted foliage (`generated_chunks`, `all_foliage`), is never saved or sent, and changing it bumps `generated_version`, not `revision`. Only chunks whose ground (or a neighbour's, within the largest `AvoidRadius`) or rules changed are made again: a sculpt stroke on a 2,048-stud forest of 348,000 plants remakes about 16 chunks in 2 to 5 ms; the whole forest takes 0.12 s in release (`bench_forest_on_a_2048_stud_terrain`). Drawing (`scene/src/foliage.rs`) keeps each chunk's groups and builds again only chunks whose list changed; physics, navigation and preloading read `all_foliage`. The Terrain Studio's Scatter Rules section (`editor/src/terrain_studio/scatter.rs`, actions `app/terrain/scatter.rs`) adds rules from presets (the Forest set of three, Forest Trees, Undergrowth, Meadow Flowers, Grass Tufts, or an empty Scatter Rule), edits the selected one, picks its models from Assets, and Bake turns what the rules placed into painted foliage and switches them off, one undo step.

Terrain and objects (procedural worlds PW5): a ScatterRule with `AvoidParts` (on) places nothing under or into an anchored, colliding part whose box comes within 12 studs of the ground over a spot (`procedural/src/obstacles.rs`: the column from a stud under the ground (so a floor flush with it counts) to 12 studs up against the part's box grown sideways by `PartMargin`, 1 stud), so a house, a fence or a placed rock clears its own ground; a platform high above doesn't. A character's parts don't count (inside a Model with a Humanoid or a character controller, `in_a_character`): its controller anchors the parts it moves, and until 2026-11-27 a walking player was an obstacle, so the chunks round it were made again every frame it moved, the terrain data was copied each time and the scene rebuilt its foliage, and the player's memory grew by about 400 MB a second (Overgrown Street; test `a_walking_character_remakes_no_chunks`). Scatter rules write their chunks through `World::terrain_generated_mut`, whose copy of shared data keeps its uid (`TerrainData::clone_keeping_identity`): what was built from the heights and paint (weights, the drawn ground, painted foliage by uid and revision) stays current, and a collider is rebuilt only when colliding generated foliage comes or goes (`generated_collide_version`; test `generated_foliage_changes_keep_the_terrains_identity`). A moving anchored part near rules still remakes its chunks, as it should, and memory stays level. `Obstacles` keeps the Workspace's parts between frames (the list read again when the Workspace's structure changes, a part's box when its revision does), and each part's hash goes into the key of the chunks it reaches, so moving one remakes only the ground near it. `CastShadow` (on) off leaves what a rule places out of the shadow maps and ray traced shadows (`FoliageInstance::shadow`, [rendering/shadows.md](rendering/shadows.md)); it's in the rule's key, so changing it makes its plants again. `AlignToSlope` (0 to 1) leans what a rule places toward the ground's normal and `SinkDepth` (studs at scale 1) lowers it into the ground; they're `FoliageInstance::lean` and `sink`, used alike by drawing (`scene/src/foliage.rs`) and solid foliage's colliders (`physics/src/shapes.rs`); the Scatter pane's items set them too (Align to slope, Sink). The rule presets lean grass, flowers and bushes and sink trees a little. Conform to Terrain and Blend into Terrain are in the ribbon's Solid Modeling group ([architecture/solid-modeling.md](architecture/solid-modeling.md)).

Holes and cave pieces (procedural worlds PW7): `TerrainData` keeps a hole mask, one flag per cell (cell (x, z) runs from sample (x, z) to +X and +Z), empty until the first hole (`holes`, `is_hole`, `hole_at`, `holes_mut_in`). A hole change counts as a height change: it bumps the chunk's height version, goes through `read_region`/`write_region` for undo, and is kept by `replace_with` and `resampled`. Holes save as optional `holes.png` (8-bit greyscale, 255 a hole), written only when there are some; `project` removes a leftover when they're all filled, and an older engine ignores the file. A cut-out cell is left out of the drawn chunk (a chunk with holes is always drawn at full detail, so a hole looks the same far away), out of the collider (parry's `HeightField` cell `CELL_REMOVED`), out of the navmesh's ground (a cave floor of parts under it is walkable), out of `ground_y` and raycasts (a ray passes through, and a ray from under the surface never hits it), and scatter rules place nothing on it. A terrain with holes isn't "solid below" for queries (`open_below`), so caves under it work. The Terrain Studio's **Holes** tool (`brush::Operation::Holes`, Shift fills) cuts and fills them, one undo step a stroke; the AI tools' `terrain_edit` sculpt `hole` and `fill` do the same. Cliff and cave pieces are built-in props for rules: `prop:Cliff` (upright slabs for steep ground) and `prop:Rock` (a boulder), with the rule presets **Cliff Rocks** (38 degrees and steeper, leaned a little with the slope and sunk 2.5 studs, solid) and **Boulders** (8 to 40 degrees, in groups); the Alpine world preset adds Cliff Rocks. They aren't in the Assets panel's props. A cave mouth is a hole in a hillside, a tunnel of parts under it, and rock parts round the opening, which rules keep clear of (`AvoidParts`): see `crates/samples/projects/caves` and its game test, which walks in (and fails without the hole).

Roads and paths (procedural worlds PW4): a `TerrainPath` inside a Spline keeps every terrain's scatter rules off its strip, live (thinning them out over its `ClearFalloff` past it), and the editor's Apply and Route flatten, paint and lay out the road; Apply also paints a shoulder layer out over the falloff with a noise-broken edge and takes painted foliage off the strip. See [architecture/splines.md](architecture/splines.md#terrain-paths).

Biomes (procedural worlds PW2, `terrain/src/biomes.rs`): a `Biome` inside a Terrain is an area with its own ground layer (`GroundLayer`, a TerrainLayer's name) and its own ScatterRules (its children). It covers ground by itself where its height, slope and moisture windows hold (`MinHeight`…`SlopeFade`, `MinMoisture`, `MaxMoisture`, `MoistureFade`; moisture is shared noise `MoistureScale` studs across, the same for every biome at the same scale), with edges broken up by `EdgeNoise` over `NoiseScale`, later biomes covering earlier ones (`AutoEnabled` off: only where painted). Painting a biome is painting its ground layer: the ground layer's paint is the biome's paint, and the automatic weights fill only what paint leaves, so changing a biome never loses paint and nothing new is saved, undone or sent. `MaterialRules` holds the biomes too: each biome's ground layer is laid where it covers, under every layer's own rule (rock on cliffs and snow on peaks still win), and its `key` changes with them, so the weight textures follow. A rule inside a Biome places only where that biome covers, its chance times the biome's weight, so it thins out at soft edges (`materials::biome_weights`). The Terrain Studio's Biomes section (`editor/src/terrain_studio/biomes.rs`, actions `app/terrain/biomes.rs`) lists them, edits the selected one, adds them from presets (Lowland Forest, Meadow, Alpine, Beach, an empty Biome; each brings its ground layer, made if the terrain lacks it, and its rules, one undo step), and Paint this biome picks the Paint tool on its ground layer. With a Biome or a rule in one selected, Add rule adds into that Biome.

World presets (procedural worlds PW3, `project/src/world_presets.rs`): a `.axworld` file (JSON, `WorldPreset`) holds a whole landscape setup: the generator's settings, the terrain's TerrainLayers, Biomes (with their rules) and ScatterRules as a prefab file's JSON (so every property is kept), the water level as a share of the height range, and the height range the objects were made on (heights scale to the terrain they're applied to; 10000, "no top", stays). `apply_objects` checks the objects in a world of their own first, then replaces the terrain's layers, biomes and rules and sets its water. Six are built in, made from the studio's own layer, biome and rule presets (`editor/src/terrain_studio/world_presets.rs`: Temperate Forest, Alpine, Desert, Tropical Island, Tundra, Meadow); the project's own are in its `presets/` folder. The Generate tool's World Presets section picks one: **Generate with it** sets the generator and makes a preview, and Apply then puts in the landscape, the objects and the water as one undo step (`app/terrain/world_presets.rs`); **Only its materials and rules** keeps the shape; **Save as preset** writes the terrain's setup, with the settings its landscape was generated with, to `presets/<name>.axworld`.

Water (LE4, `scene/src/terrain_water.rs`): a Terrain's `Water` switch floods every chunk whose lowest ground is below `WaterLevel` (world Y), drawn as the renderer's water (`docs/rendering/water.md`: waves, what's under seen through it, reflections, depth tint and foam where it meets the ground). `WaterColor`, `WaterClarity`, `WaveHeight`, `WaveLength`, `WaveSpeed` and `WaveDirection` are the water material's values. Each flooded chunk gets one water item, the top of a box as wide as the chunk at the level; its waves follow world positions, so neighbours meet seamlessly. A chunk's lowest ground is kept by its heights' version, so only an edited chunk is looked at again. The Terrain Studio's **Water** section has the switch, a level slider over the terrain's height range, the color and the waves. `WaveVariation` (0 to 1, default 0.6) is how much the waves differ from place to place: above 0 they gather into drifting patches of rougher and calmer water and their crests bend, so a sea doesn't look like one wave repeated (`docs/rendering/water.md`). Water is only drawn: nothing swims or floats in it yet. Test: `scene/src/terrain.rs` `terrain_water_fills_only_the_chunks_whose_ground_dips_below_its_level`.

Painted water: the Materials tab's **Water** brush (`brush::Operation::Water`, Shift removes it) fills the cells under it with water at a level of their own, for ponds, lakes and rivers above or without the flood. The level is the brush's **Level**: some studs (**deep**, default 3) above where the stroke starts, or a fixed world Y, which Ctrl+click on the ground picks. Painting over existing water blends levels smoothly across the brush's falloff border (`brush.rs`). A stamp works out each cell's new level first and writes, and marks as changed, only the cells that change, so the brush held still over water it has filled (it stamps every 0.05 s) marks no chunk. `TerrainData` keeps one level a cell (cell (x, z) runs from sample (x, z) to +X and +Z), in the heights' units so the water moves with the terrain, `NO_WATER` (-1) where there's none, and nothing until the first paint (`water`, `water_at`, `water_mut_in`, `has_water`, `chunk_has_water`). Painting bumps only the chunk's water version (`chunk_water_version`), not its heights, so the ground isn't rebuilt, nor the painted foliage, which follows `revision_apart_from_water` (until 2026-11-26 every stamp built Desert Homestead's whole painted foliage again, 20 ms a frame of a stroke); it goes through `read_region`/`write_region` for undo (one step a stroke) and is kept by `replace_with` and `resampled`. It saves as optional `water.png` (16-bit greyscale: 0 none, 1 to 65535 the bottom to the top of the height range), written only when there's some; `project` removes a leftover, and an older engine ignores the file. `scene/src/terrain_water.rs` gives each chunk with painted water a mesh of the cells that show, averaging shared corners across adjacent cells so differing stroke levels join seamlessly, feathering exposed unpainted border vertices via `tangent.w` (`edge_mask`), leaving out cells whose ground is above their water all round and, with the flood on, cells at or under its level; a chunk's mesh is rebuilt only when its water, the ring of cells around it (in the eight chunks around, read again only when one of them changes), its heights change, or the flood's level crosses one of its painted levels (how many of them it covers is all the level changes of the mesh), and then under the chunk's own key with a new `MeshSource::version`, so the renderer rewrites its copy, and its ray traced BLAS, in place (until 2026-11-26 each rebuild had a key of its own, and dragging `WaterLevel` with flooding on left 22 meshes a frame in the renderer and the ray traced scene, Uploads 15.5 ms a frame); and the renderer uploads water meshes immediately even with background uploads on (`ScenePipeline::upload_new`) so active painting never flickers. Painted water has the terrain's look (the Water section's color and waves, shown under the brush's settings). Tests: core `painted_water_is_cells_that_regions_undo_and_resizing_keeps`, terrain `water_fills_the_cells_under_the_brush_at_its_level_and_undoes`, `a_water_stamp_that_changes_nothing_marks_no_chunk`, `water_brush_falloff_blends_existing_water_levels_smoothly`, and `painted_water_saves_only_when_there_is_some_and_loads_the_same`, scene `painted_water_draws_only_its_cells_that_show`, `dragging_the_water_level_keeps_each_painted_chunks_mesh` and `painted_water_feathers_exposed_outer_edges_and_blends_adjacent_levels`, render `a_rebuilt_water_mesh_draws_immediately_with_background_uploads`, editor `the_water_brush_paints_a_pond_that_saves_undoes_and_shift_removes` and `dragging_a_water_slider_is_one_undo_step` (a Water section slider dragged over many frames is one undo step: each frame's change merges into the open gesture's step until the pointer is let go).

Tiles (`tiles.rs`): a landscape can be several Terrains side by side, like Unity's neighbour terrains. There's no tile class: two Terrains are neighbours when their `Size`, samples along each side and `Position.Y` match and their middles are exactly one `Size.X` (or `Size.Z`) apart along X (or Z), so they share a whole edge and every sample on it (`side_between`, `neighbours`, `neighbour_on`, `connected`; `free_sides` is the sides with no Terrain overlapping where a tile would go). Each tile keeps its own copy of the edge's samples: `stitch` makes a shared edge the mean of both, and `blend_edge` pins a new tile's edge (heights and paint) to the tile it meets, easing the difference away over `BLEND_BAND` (16) samples. Holes and painted water are per cell, and a seam's cells belong to one tile or the other, so only heights and paint are shared. The Terrain tab's **Tiles** section (`editor/src/terrain_studio/tiles.rs`, actions `app/terrain/tiles.rs`) says how many tiles the landscape has, picks how the new ground is made, and has a button for each side of the edited tile (−Z above, +Z below, as seen from above; off where a Terrain is already). **Continues it** generates on the studio's job thread with the tile's own saved generator settings (or, for a terrain never generated, the Generate tool's), `offset` moved one tile along; **Flat** is flat at the edge's mean height. Either way the new tile's edges are then blended into every tile already round where it goes. The tile is a copy of the one it grows from (`World::duplicate`: its TerrainLayers, Biomes, ScatterRules and water settings, no painted foliage) with a unique name, a data folder of its own and its new ground, added after it as one undo step, and becomes the selected, edited terrain. `GeneratorSettings::offset` (studs, X and Z, default zero, left out of the saved JSON when zero, so older files and non-tiles are unchanged and `GENERATOR_VERSION` stays 1) moves where the fractal noise and dunes are sampled; island and crater shapes stay each tile's own. The scene draws a chunk side on a tile edge with a neighbour at full detail (`lod_at` past the border returns level 0 there), so tiles meet with no crack whatever level their edge chunks are at. Tests: `tiles_tests.rs`, `generate.rs` `tiles_moved_by_their_offset_meet_at_their_edges`, scene `neighbouring_tiles_meet_at_full_detail_along_their_seam`, editor `app/terrain/tile_tests.rs` and `terrain_studio/tests.rs` `the_tiles_section_adds_a_tile_on_a_free_side_only`. Not yet: brushes work on one tile at a time (the target), and sculpting after a tile is added doesn't stitch the seam again; normals at a seam come from each tile's own samples, so lighting can show a faint line there.

Chunk meshes (`mesh.rs`, drawn by `scene/src/terrain.rs`): each 32-cell chunk is drawn at one of five levels (every 1st, 2nd, 4th, 8th or 16th sample), picked each frame by its nearest point's distance from the camera (full detail within `detail_distance`, 160 studs, halving each time the distance doubles); a chunk with holes is always at full detail (`drawn_lod`). The scene picks every chunk's level first, then builds each chunk to meet its neighbours: a side next to a finer chunk takes that chunk's samples, its cells along that side fanned out to them (`side_lods`, `chunk_mesh`), so neighbours share every corner along their seam and the surface is watertight wherever levels meet, with no crack or T-junction to see through. Only the coarser side of a seam changes, so a full-detail chunk is always exactly the collider. Nothing hangs from the edges: the terrain's outer border is the surface's own edge (before 2026-11-16, every chunk hung a skirt from all four sides to hide cracks, which showed at the border as stretched, stepped walls and at level seams as slivers). A mesh is keyed by its chunk, level and sides' levels; it takes well under a millisecond, so it's always built in the frame that needs it (streaming makes only the weights on its thread). The renderer uploads terrain chunk meshes right away even with background uploads on (`ScenePipeline::upload_new`), like their weights, so a chunk changing level is never missing for a frame; before, in the editor (which always uploads in the background) it was, a sky-coloured square in the ground while the camera moved. Tests: `mesh_tests.rs` (`seams_between_levels_of_detail_are_watertight`, `the_border_ends_at_the_surface`), scene `moving_the_camera_draws_every_chunk_every_frame_with_closed_seams`, render `a_terrain_chunk_switching_level_is_drawn_in_the_frame_it_switches`.

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| Placement | World studs and the sample grid |
| `brush` | Raise, lower, smooth, flatten, sculpt, noise, stamps, holes, material paint, painted water, and scatter (`brush/scatter.rs`: a palette's weighted mix of foliage), with undo records |
| `convert` | A part turned into ground: samples under it raised to its top and painted (the editor's Solid Modeling ▸ To Terrain) |
| `generate` | Landscapes from settings and a seed, including presets |
| `erosion` | Water and gravity |
| `materials` | Layers by height and slope, blended with paint |
| `mesh` | Chunk meshes at several levels of detail, stitched where levels meet |
| `raycast` | Where a ray meets the surface |
| `tiles` | Several Terrains side by side: neighbours, where a new tile goes, stitching and blending shared edges |
| `volume` | The PW8 voxel spike: a signed-distance volume in 32³ chunks, ball brushes and a surface-nets mesher (not an Instance yet; [research/procedural-worlds.md](research/procedural-worlds.md#pw8-voxel-spike-whats-built-and-how-its-decided)) |
| `files` | PNG and JSON codecs (`heights.png`, paint maps, optional `foliage.json` (with each instance's optional `lean` and `sink`; a terrain holds at most 32,768 instances, `MAX_FOLIAGE_INSTANCES`; a hand-written file's instances past that are left out when it's read, and `files::decode_reporting` says how many, which `project::load_terrains` reports as a `LoadError::Terrain` on the `foliage.json` (the terrain still loads; an unreadable `foliage.json` is reported the same way and the terrain loads with no painted plants). Use ScatterRules for more, `holes.png` and `water.png`) |
| `job` | Slow work on another thread, with progress and cancel |

Generators stay deterministic for a seed and `GENERATOR_VERSION`. Saved terrains record both. Bump the version when the output changes. Chunk triangles split cells the same way as collision, so the drawn surface and the collider match.
